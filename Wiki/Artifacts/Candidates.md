# Candidate Artifacts

> **Speculative roster.** None of these reframings are canon yet. Brainstorm — mythic artifacts whose properties *look like* agency tech, with their likely true nature, location, and recovery mission angle.

See [Overview.md](Overview.md) for the artifact framework and reward mechanics.

## How to Read This Page

- **True nature** is my best read on what the artifact actually is in the setting's mechanics.
- **Attribution** is which agency made or planted it (often: which agency *lost* it).
- **Location / window** is where and when it's most accessible for recovery.
- **Mission angle** is the recovery hook.

## Mark-Leak Tech

### Excalibur

- **True nature:** EPOCH Mark VI infused longsword. Self-sharpening edge (carbon-nanotube composite host, orichalcum-bonded), forged of **smoke-clear translucent alloy** with a shard fragment set at the pommel — so the blade is **always lit** by the fragment's radiance (cold blue-white, warming to gold in its keyed handler's hand). It is a pure [shard-glow](Anachrotech.md#the-one-principle-the-shard-does-everything): no lights, no switch, **cannot be turned off** — hiding it is a permanent problem (a bonfire to EPOCH [agent-sense](../Core-Setting/Orichalcum.md#agent-sense)). The "sword in the stone" is the original infusion mount — an EPOCH delivery cradle that bonds to the rightful-handler's biometric signature. Anyone *not* keyed to it can't pull it free; the keyed handler pulls it effortlessly. The "Lady of the Lake" is almost certainly an EPOCH embed handler (probably the operative who lost it). See [Anachrotech.md](Anachrotech.md#worked-examples) for the full luminous build.
- **Attribution:** EPOCH. Probably a failed extraction during a 5th–6th century operation around the fall of post-Roman Britain. The sword should have come back home with the operative; instead it ended up in the wrong hands and stayed in-era.
- **Location / window:** Post-Roman Britain, late 5th to mid-6th century CE. The "Arthurian" period is when the sword is at its most narratively accessible. Returned to the Lake (i.e., re-claimed by EPOCH recovery? or stashed in a hidden anchor) at the end of the Arthurian cycle.
- **Mechanical effect:** Best blade in any ATA armory if recovered. Boost-transit cost effectively zero (saturated infusion). The biometric-key feature means whoever ATA chooses as the keyed handler becomes the sole effective wielder — a major political decision inside the agency.
- **Mission angle:** **The Camelot Arc** — fully built out at [Missions/Camelot-Excalibur.md](../Missions/Camelot-Excalibur.md). Players insert into the Arthurian court as knights, advisors, or rivals. The mission isn't just recovery — it's figuring out who the Lady of the Lake is, who Merlin is (almost certainly *also* an agent — ATA?), and whether Arthur himself is a player or a pawn. The legendary "return of the sword to the lake" might be the team's extraction event.

### The Spear of Destiny (Lance of Longinus)

- **True nature:** Mark V EPOCH morale-and-command weapon. Projects a low-amplitude empathic field that biases nearby observers toward following the wielder's lead and feeling that the wielder is *destined* to win. Operationally: lets a mediocre general perform like a great one, *if* the army doesn't realize what's making them follow him.
- **Attribution:** EPOCH, planted deliberately in the historical record. Possibly seeded multiple times — every time a "Spear of Destiny" emerges in history (Charlemagne's, the Hapsburg one, Hitler's obsession with it) might be EPOCH redeploying the same artifact to influence pivotal leaders.
- **Location / window:** Currently held in the Imperial Treasury in Vienna (Hofburg). Almost certainly a decoy at this point — the original is probably back in EPOCH's 2200s vault. But the *decoy* might still be infused.
- **Mission angle:** Modern-era intelligence op. Determine if the Hofburg spear is original, decoy, or empty mundane prop. If decoy with infusion, recovery is high-stakes diplomacy (Austria will not just hand it over). Historical-era angle: Charlemagne's coronation, with the spear visibly in EPOCH operative hands during a moment ATA is trying to control.

### The Holy Grail

- **True nature:** Mark V ATA medical-regeneration cradle (small, cup-shaped, fits in two hands). Originally a field-medic device for embedded agents in eras without modern medicine. Healing capability is real — drinking from it (i.e., activating the bio-printer interface) repairs significant tissue damage on the operator and provides extended cellular regeneration to anyone in close contact. The "eternal life" mythology is a folk read of the extended-regeneration effect.
- **Attribution:** ATA. Lost during a 1st-century CE operation around the Crucifixion (yes, players will need to deal with that era — and the implications of whether Jesus himself was an agent, an ATA-supported reformer, or mundane). The grail was probably the field kit of the ATA agent embedded with the early Jesus movement.
- **Location / window:** Unknown. The historical-record trail goes cold after the 1st century, picks up again in 12th-century Arthurian romance (suggesting it surfaced in Britain around the same time Excalibur did — possibly a Britain-as-artifact-stash pattern worth investigating).
- **Mission angle:** **The Grail Quest Arc.** Mid-campaign multi-session arc. Players follow the historical trail through the 1st-century Levant, the 4th-century reliquary archives, the 12th-century Cistercian abbeys, the Cathar repositories before the Albigensian Crusade, and possibly into the modern day (Rennes-le-Château, etc.). Each step is a mission.

### Mjölnir, the Ark of the Covenant, etc.

Both candidate Mark-V+ artifacts under analogous patterns. Flagging for future development — too much to chew on each in this brainstorm pass.

### The Ulfberht Swords

- **True nature:** A leaked *production run* of Mark-grade blade steel. Roughly 170 surviving swords (9th–11th c.) carry the inlaid signature +VLFBERHT+ and are forged from crucible steel of a purity and carbon content Europe could not reliably reproduce for another ~800 years (near-slag-free, ~1% carbon). In-setting that isn't a lone genius — it's an embed's forge running Mark-infusion metallurgy with period-available inputs, its output escaping into Norse and Frankish trade. Tellingly, many surviving "Ulfberht" blades are *inferior forgeries* with misspelled inlays (+VLFBERH+T and the like) — exactly the pattern the [cover-forgery doctrine](Overview.md#cover-forgeries) predicts: real articles plus a flood of period knockoffs muddying the record.
- **Attribution:** Ambiguous by design — an embed forge, agency uncertain (that's the hook). Whoever ran it, the technique got loose.
- **Location / window:** Frankish/Rhineland and Norse trade networks, ~800–1000 CE. Individual blades surface in river finds, graves, and modern museum collections across northern Europe.
- **Mechanical effect:** A genuine blade is a superior weapon, but the real prize is *foundry-technique intel* — proof and method for making Mark-grade steel from Iron Age inputs, worth more than any single sword.
- **Mission angle:** Trace the forge. Recovering *who is making them* — a rogue embed? which agency? a mundane who stumbled onto a leaked billet cache? — is a knowledge-recovery op that matters more than the loot. And if the forge is still running, someone is quietly seeding period-breaking tech into medieval Europe.

### The luminous gear-up loadout (arming for EPOCH)

A cluster of [luminous anachrotech](Anachrotech.md) artifacts that, recovered across the campaign, bring the team from mundane-Mark-IV gear toward **EPOCH Mark VI parity** — the in-fiction justification for the late-campaign arcs where the players finally fight EPOCH on their own terms. Each is a rare, roughly one-per-arc showpiece, and each stands alone as an upgrade — this is a *loose loadout* (blade · shield · power · sight · mobility), **not** a collect-all-five-to-unlock-a-superweapon set (that trap is flagged in [Open Questions](#open-questions)). [Excalibur](#excalibur) is the flagship blade; these fill out the kit.

#### The Aegis (Athena's shield / a hero's radiant panoply)

- **True nature:** EPOCH Mark VI personal defense — a shield (or breastplate) of **translucent smoke-clear alloy**, shard-cored so it glows, that turns blades and blunts impacts an ordinary shield can't. The "Gorgon's face" on Athena's Aegis reads as a low-amplitude **psi-fear field**: attackers falter, their nerve breaks, they cannot press the charge. This is the *survivability* piece — the first artifact that lets a PC take a direct hit from an EPOCH operative's weapon and live.
- **Attribution:** EPOCH (or high-Mark ATA). The "made by the gods" framing is exactly the anachrotech tell.
- **Location / window:** A classical-Mediterranean temple treasury or a hero's barrow — an *aegis* / *gorgoneion* relic, or the shield of a named hero (Achilles, Perseus) in a tomb.
- **Mechanical effect (gear-up):** Survivability parity + melee/crowd control (the fear-field). Glows in use; cannot be carried covert without a suppression cover.
- **Mission angle:** Temple or tomb recovery — and EPOCH may be guarding it as *their own* lost kit, turning the op into a race/heist against a counter-team.

#### The Nimbus (the saint's halo / a god-king's diadem)

- **True nature:** A head-worn shard circlet that casts a ring of radiance — **the literal halo** of saints, bodhisattvas, and god-kings in religious art. Bulk shard proximity to the brain amplifies [psi](../Core-Setting/Psionics.md): the Nimbus turns a latent or weak psi into a strong one, or extends a trained psi's range and power. It is the team's **psi force-multiplier** — how their psi comes to match EPOCH's.
- **Attribution:** EPOCH (a deliberate "chosen one / god-king" enabler, like the [Spear](#the-spear-of-destiny-lance-of-longinus)) or ATA.
- **Location / window:** A reliquary "saint's crown," or a divine ruler's diadem in a tomb (a pharaoh, a deified emperor). Late-antique/medieval relic, or Bronze-Age royal burial.
- **Mechanical effect (gear-up):** Major psi upgrade for one PC — and a total renunciation of anonymity: you *cannot* wear a halo undercover, and prolonged wear risks over-saturating the wearer's brain (an [overload](Anachrotech.md#open-questions) danger).
- **Mission angle:** Reliquary heist or tomb op; the wearer becomes a marked target the instant it's donned. Best claimed just before it's needed, not carried around lit.

#### The Undying Fire (the eternal flame / ever-burning lamp)

- **True nature:** An exposed shard in an open orichalcum cradle — a self-powered flame that gives light and heat **forever, with no fuel**. The "sacred fire that never dies" (Vesta's hearth, the Zoroastrian Atash Behram, perpetual sanctuary lamps, the "lamp still burning" in a sealed tomb) is this, venerated by cultures with no other word for it. A larger fragment carries a faint psi bed (calm / aversion) a psi can shape.
- **Attribution:** ATA / Atlantean cache — plausibly a *fuel deposit hidden in plain sight as holy fire*, or the power unit of a long-abandoned embed outpost.
- **Location / window:** Any perpetual-flame cult site — Rome's Temple of Vesta (until 394 CE), a still-burning fire temple (Yazd), or a tomb rumored to hold a burning lamp.
- **Mechanical effect (gear-up):** The kit's **utility/power backbone** — warmth in a lethal-cold era, light in a lightless op, a trickle-charge for other anachrotech, a psi anchor, and raw refined-fuel value (it *is* a fragment).
- **Mission angle:** Temple infiltration (taking a civilization's sacred flame is sacrilege *and* an omen when it "goes out" — leave a decoy flame per [cover-forgery doctrine](Overview.md#cover-forgeries)), or a tomb recovery.

#### The Swift Sandals (Talaria / seven-league boots — mobility)

- **True nature:** An EPOCH personal **kinetic-assist rig** — short-range leaps, glides, a running boost — reframed across cultures as Hermes' winged sandals, seven-league boots, or a flying carpet. Shard-powered, glowing at the soles (a telltale trail of light). The **mobility-parity** piece: EPOCH operatives move with Mark VI kit; this is how the team keeps up, closes distance, or escapes.
- **Attribution:** EPOCH.
- **Location / window:** A Hermes/Mercury cult relic, a folk "seven-league boots" artifact, or a Solomon's-carpet legend.
- **Mechanical effect (gear-up):** Battlefield mobility, verticality, and escape. The sole-glow leaves a light trail — a beacon that makes stealthy use costly.
- **Mission angle:** Recovery from a myth/relic site; a fun candidate for a lighter, caper-flavored arc.

## Refined Fuel & Orichalcum Artifacts

### The Philosopher's Stone

- **True nature:** A block of *refined raw orichalcum* — pre-foundry, ready for infusion alloy. The "transmutation of base metals into gold" mythology is a folk-read of its actual function: infusing host metals to convert them into orichalcum. The "elixir of life" / "immortality" mythology is the slow-aging effect of high saturation in those who handled it for extended periods.
- **Attribution:** Lost Atlantean cache. May have surfaced multiple times across history (alchemical traditions in Hellenistic Egypt, Islamic Golden Age, European medieval period) before being lost again.
- **Location / window:** Last historically credible appearance: Nicolas Flamel's Paris, late 14th / early 15th century. Possibly held by a sequence of European alchemical guilds until the Enlightenment dispersed them.
- **Mechanical effect:** **Massive foundry boost.** A single Stone could lift ATA's foundry by half a mark for years. The single most valuable artifact in this entire roster.
- **Mission angle:** Modern-era reconstruction of Flamel's chain of custody, combined with historical-era cross-checks. Possibly the Stone has been broken into multiple pieces (each tracked separately, each a sub-mission).

### The Antikythera Mechanism (the famous one)

- **True nature:** A *real lost ATA Antikythera device* (see [../Core-Setting/Antikythera-Device.md](../Core-Setting/Antikythera-Device.md)). An agent operating in the Aegean lost it in a ~60 BCE shipwreck near the island of Antikythera. The wreck was rediscovered by sponge divers in 1900 CE. The remains, missing the orichalcum core and meteor fragment (corroded or extracted by the agency at some point after rediscovery), were interpreted by modern researchers as an "ancient Greek astronomical computer." The interpretation is *partially correct* — the visible gear-trains do compute astronomical positions — but it misses the parachronic-coordinate function that the missing internal sections enabled.
- **Attribution:** ATA. Confirmed canon.
- **Location / window:** The device fragments are currently housed at the National Archaeological Museum of Athens, having been studied continuously since 1901. Modern ATA has periodic access to the artifact for inspection and to confirm no critical pieces have been overlooked.
- **Mission angle:** Not a *recovery target* per se (the artifact is already in friendly hands, in plain sight, with its true nature hidden by misinterpretation). But the original *cover-shaping operation* that established the "ancient Greek calculator" interpretation in academic literature is a great late-19th / early-20th century campaign hook. Players could be sent to manage the 1900s academic discourse, ensuring the dominant interpretation locks in.

### The Emerald Tablet of Hermes Trismegistus

- **True nature:** Atlantean foundry-technique documentation, inscribed on an orichalcum-infused tablet. The "as above, so below" cosmological mysticism is a folk-read of actual technical content about gradient mechanics and infusion ratios.
- **Attribution:** Lost Atlantean — possibly the original primary-source document for the entire post-Atlantean alchemical tradition.
- **Location / window:** Origin lost in antiquity. Translation chains exist (Arabic, Latin, Hermetic). The *original* may still exist somewhere, deeply buried.
- **Mission angle:** Recovery of the original — possibly buried near the Atlantean crater, possibly in a North African or Mesopotamian site. The translations are knowledge artifacts in their own right (see below).

### Various "Sacred Stones" (Black Stone of Mecca, Stone of Scone, etc.)

Multiple religious traditions venerate a specific stone or stone fragment with attributed mystical properties. Several are plausibly orichalcum cores embedded in mundane host stone. Each needs its own analysis pass — some are real, most are mundane. The risk profile for *investigating* major-religion sacred objects is significant.

## Knowledge Artifacts

### Lost Scrolls of the Library of Alexandria

- **True nature:** Variable. Some are mundane lost knowledge (engineering treatises, drama, history). Some are agency-relevant: ATA embed reports filed in plain sight, Atlantean translation work, Hellenistic copies of Egyptian technical texts. **At least one scroll is canon-significant:** the lost work of *Manetho* containing pre-dynastic Egyptian chronology, which includes documentation of pre-Bronze-Age population groups no modern source mentions.
- **Attribution:** Mix. The most valuable scrolls are usually ATA-deposited (hiding them in plain sight in the largest library of antiquity was *exactly* the right move).
- **Location / window:** The Library, before the Caesar fire (48 BCE), before the Theophilus destruction (391 CE), and before Hypatia's death (415 CE). Three windows, three sub-missions.
- **Mission angle:** **The Alexandria Salvage Arc.** Multi-session arc spanning the three loss events. Players run interleaved missions to extract specific scrolls before each fire/destruction. Hypatia is the embedded ATA contact for the final window. This arc directly connects to her canon extraction in 415 CE.

### Newton's Missing Alchemy Notebooks

- **True nature:** Newton's working notes from his decades-long attempt to reverse-engineer parachronic theory from theological hints he was fed. Mostly wrong — but contains a few partial-correct derivations that could meaningfully accelerate ATA modern physics if applied.
- **Attribution:** Newton was ATA per [Embedded-Agents/Candidates.md](../Embedded-Agents/Candidates.md). His notes are agency property posthumously suppressed.
- **Location / window:** Surfaced 1936 (Sotheby's auction, partially acquired by Keynes); some volumes still missing.
- **Mission angle:** Modern era, contemporary intelligence op. Possibly tied to a present-day private collector. Lower-key, espionage-flavored.

### The Voynich Manuscript

- **True nature:** An embed's encrypted field journal — or an EPOCH cipher document. The early-15th-c. codex has resisted every cryptographer, linguist, and machine for a century *because it isn't a natural language or a period cipher* — it's an agency operational cipher. Its content maps suspiciously onto tradecraft: the "botanical" folios read as a [Temporal-Production](../Design-Notes/Temporal-Production.md) grow-log (plants that match no real species), the "balneological" folios as bio-asset culturing notes, the "astronomical" folios as transit-coordinate tables.
- **Attribution:** Deliberately ambiguous (the hook). Radiocarbon-dated vellum ~1404–1438; provenance runs through Rudolf II's Prague court.
- **Location / window:** Currently the Beinecke Library, Yale. The historical trail (Prague → the Jesuit college at Frascati → Wilfrid Voynich's 1912 purchase) is a chain-of-custody sub-plot.
- **Mechanical effect:** Deciphered, it unlocks a cache of intel — an embed roster, temporal-cache coordinates, or an EPOCH ops log, depending on which agency wrote it.
- **Mission angle:** Deciphering *is* the mission — a modern-era + historical cross-check op. The key is probably not in the book: a companion codebook, the writer's surviving descendant, or an embed's memory recovered elsewhere.

### The Copper Scroll (3Q15)

- **True nature:** An ATA temporal-cache manifest. Alone among the Dead Sea Scrolls, the Copper Scroll is not scripture or commentary — it's a terse inventory of 64 buried caches of gold, silver, and "vessels," with locations. In-setting it's exactly what it looks like: a *stash list* of ATA aging-sites and loot caches (see [temporal-caching](../Design-Notes/Temporal-Production.md), Artifact Vault I-7), engraved on incorruptible copper precisely because it had to survive centuries until a recovery team came for it.
- **Attribution:** ATA. Cached with the Qumran material for concealment in a stable 1st-c. Levantine site.
- **Location / window:** Found 1952 in Cave 3 at Qumran; now in Amman. The caches it lists are scattered across the 1st-c. Levant — described by landmarks that mostly no longer exist.
- **Mechanical effect:** Each surviving entry is a recoverable cache — refined fuel, gear, or a matured Temporal-Production batch. Collectively a major resupply.
- **Mission angle:** A whole sub-arc of cache hunts. The catch: the manifest uses vanished landmarks, so each recovery needs historical research — or a period insert to read the landmarks while they still stand — and EPOCH may hold a copy of the same list.

### The Akashic Records (?)

- **True nature:** Multiple esoteric traditions reference a "cosmic memory" repository. Working hypothesis: the Reality Log itself, occasionally glimpsed by particularly strong precognitive psis who don't have agency context to interpret what they're seeing.
- **Attribution:** N/A — not an artifact, but worth flagging because players will eventually hear about it from a mundane occultist contact and need an in-fiction answer.
- **Mission angle:** Investigation reveal rather than recovery. Late-campaign GM tool.

## Biological Artifacts

### Helen's Descendants

- **True nature:** Living humans carrying the engineered glamour-psi genetic trait. Each one is a potential recruit, intelligence asset, or research subject — depending on agency ethics.
- **Location / window:** Modern era, scattered across Mediterranean diaspora populations. Dormant trait, occasionally surfacing as "natural" charisma in pop stars, politicians, or cult leaders.
- **Mission angle:** Long-arc thread. Each surfaced descendant is a session. Some join ATA. Some join EPOCH. Some refuse both and become independent variables.

### The Shroud of Turin

- **True nature:** Residue of the 1st-c. [Grail](Candidates.md#the-holy-grail) operation — or the cover-relic that commemorates it. The full-body photonegative "image" reads as a Mark medical-cradle *scan burn-in*: a full-body diagnostic imaging pass left a latent image on the wrapping linen. The 1988 radiocarbon dating to ~1260–1390 CE is usually cited to debunk it — but it *fits* ATA doctrine perfectly if the Turin cloth is a **14th-c. planted cover-relic** (an inert-infused replica carrying a faint scan-ghost), deployed centuries after the original op while the genuine 1st-c. article sits elsewhere.
- **Attribution:** ATA.
- **Location / window:** Turin Cathedral. Documented history begins ~1354 at Lirey, France — squarely inside the radiocarbon window, supporting the "planted in the 14th c." read.
- **Mechanical effect:** Analysis confirms Grail-op details and Mark scan signatures — a knowledge/bio lead, not a power item. Its real value is as a *thread* into the Grail Quest.
- **Mission angle:** Determine what it is — 1st-c. residue smuggled forward, or a 14th-c. cover-planting — and follow whichever answer toward the genuine [Holy Grail](Candidates.md#the-holy-grail).

### Preserved Myth-Engineered Tissue

- **True nature:** Samples from Minotaur, Anubis-line, Sphinx, Oracle programs. Most extant samples are *very* degraded. A well-preserved sample is biotech research gold.
- **Location / window:** Tombs, reliquaries, museum private collections, ancient burial sites near former program facilities.
- **Mission angle:** Tomb/site recovery, often combined with cover-story management (ATA cannot acknowledge that a mummified "monster" is a 4500-year-old engineered asset).

### The Embalmed EPOCH Operative

- **True nature:** Speculative — possibly canonical — an EPOCH agent killed during an ancient operation who was preserved by Egyptian mortuary practices and buried in a sealed tomb. Recoverable as a near-pristine biotech sample with full 2200s enhancements.
- **Location / window:** Egypt, exact dynasty unknown. Possibly multiple sites.
- **Mission angle:** Excavation arc. Either modern-era archaeological cover op (race a private collector or rival agency for the find) or historical-era insertion to *cause* the burial (if ATA learns the operative is about to die anyway, securing the tomb's location pays off centuries later).

## East Asian Artifacts

East Asia is EPOCH home turf. ATA artifact recovery operations here are bolder, costlier, and more politically delicate than Mediterranean operations. EPOCH has had centuries to embed artifacts in cultural-religious institutions whose modern custodians have no idea what they're holding.

### Kusanagi-no-Tsurugi (Japan)

- **True nature:** EPOCH Mark VI infused longsword. One of the Three Imperial Regalia of Japan. The myth claims it was found in the tail of the eight-headed serpent Yamata-no-Orochi — which reads as an EPOCH delivery cradle (the "serpent" is the cradle's storage geometry, the "eight heads" are activation prongs, the "tail" is the housing chamber). The blade's appearance on Susanoo's emergence from the wilderness reads as a recovery event from a buried EPOCH cache.
- **Attribution:** EPOCH. Probably a *deliberate* placement, not a failed extraction — EPOCH wanted the sword in the hands of the proto-Japanese imperial line as a stability artifact (similar pattern to the Spear of Destiny seeded in successive European leaders).
- **Location / window:** Currently housed at Atsuta Shrine in Nagoya. The shrine has never publicly displayed it; even emperors do not see it directly. It is shown wrapped in cloth at imperial enthronement ceremonies.
- **Mechanical effect:** EPOCH Mark VI blade, equivalent to Excalibur. Self-sharpening, biometric-keyed to imperial-line genetic markers (which makes it functional only for actual descendants of the Yamato line — a tighter key than Excalibur's). Boost-transit cost effectively zero.
- **Mission angle:** Extremely high-stakes recovery. The Japanese government, the Imperial Household Agency, and the Shinto religious establishment all have strong views about the Regalia. An overt recovery is politically impossible. A covert recovery would have to handle (a) the political fallout when the shrine notices, (b) the biometric key (the team would need an actual Yamato-descended wielder, which most ATA agents are not), and (c) EPOCH almost certainly has the site under continuous monitoring as one of their most valuable strategically-placed artifacts.

### The Three Imperial Regalia (Japan, complete set)

- **True nature:** Three EPOCH artifacts placed together as a regional anchor cache. **Kusanagi-no-Tsurugi** (the sword — above). **Yata-no-Kagami** (the mirror, at Ise Shrine) — working hypothesis: an EPOCH communications array, possibly capable of receiving signal from Shambhala when properly aligned. **Yasakani-no-Magatama** (the comma-shaped jewel, kept with the Emperor) — working hypothesis: a key/identification artifact that unlocks the other two when held by a biometric-matched wielder.
- **Attribution:** EPOCH. Almost certainly placed as a *set* during a major operation seeding the Japanese imperial institution. The fact that all three travel together at imperial enthronement is not ceremonial — it is *functional*. The Regalia are EPOCH technology that *does something* when the imperial line operates them as a set.
- **Mission angle:** Endgame-tier multi-arc campaign. Recovering the complete set is comparable in stakes to recovering Excalibur, the Spear, and the Grail simultaneously — *and* requires solving the political-religious access problem that recovering any single piece would already face. May not be a *recoverable* artifact in the campaign's frame; possibly only a *neutralizable* one (disable the function without removing the objects).

### Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum

- **True nature:** Speculative — see also [../Embedded-Agents/Candidates.md](../Embedded-Agents/Candidates.md) Qin Shi Huang entry. The mausoleum's unexcavated inner chamber (sealed in 210 BCE, confirmed to contain mercury rivers by modern soil surveys) is the prime artifact-recovery hook in East Asia. Working hypotheses: (A) the chamber houses an EPOCH equipment cache the First Emperor was sitting on; (B) the mercury rivers are functional cooling infrastructure for a still-operating EPOCH installation that was never extracted; (C) Qin Shi Huang's body was *successfully extracted by EPOCH* and the tomb is a cover, with the chamber containing something more valuable than a dead emperor.
- **Attribution:** EPOCH, certainly.
- **Location / window:** The sealed inner chamber of the Mausoleum of the First Qin Emperor, Lintong District, Xi'an. The Chinese government has refused excavation since the 1970s discovery of the surrounding pits (the Terracotta Army). The chamber has been continuously sealed since 210 BCE.
- **Mission angle:** Modern-era arc — ATA recovery op against a target the Chinese state will not authorize anyone to open. Multi-session: research the historical sources, identify the chamber's contents via Reality Log cross-reference, plan an extraction that bypasses Chinese security AND EPOCH counter-presence (EPOCH almost certainly monitors the site). Alternative: historical-era insertion at the moment of sealing (210 BCE) — race to be inside the chamber before the seal completes. The historical-era approach is more dangerous (Qin's security apparatus + EPOCH's deep East Asian presence + the spatial-cost overhead of operating in Qin China from the Origin).

### He Shi Bi (the Heshibi Jade)

- **True nature:** A piece of fine jade discovered in the state of Chu during the Spring and Autumn period. Historically described as having unique optical properties — luminescent in dim light. Probably orichalcum-cored, with the jade serving as host material in a Chinese alchemical analog to Mark I orichalcum infusion. The mythology is fascinating: the jade's first owner, Bian He, had his feet cut off twice before kings finally believed him about its authenticity. The classical tradition treats this as the canonical story of unrecognized truth — in the setting, it reads as someone whose unusual sensitivity to orichalcum radiance let him *feel* the artifact's nature when nobody else could.
- **Attribution:** Atlantean or early ATA cache. Possibly a fragment of the original Atlantean operations in Bronze Age China (a thin presence, but not zero — see [../History/](../History/) entries on East Asian Atlantean expeditions, TBD).
- **Location / window:** Fashioned into the imperial seal (the *Heirloom Seal of the Realm*) for Qin Shi Huang in 221 BCE. Lost during the Five Dynasties period (~10th century CE). Has not been seen since.
- **Mechanical effect:** Recovery would be a modest foundry boost (small orichalcum mass, but properly refined). The historical mystique is the bigger payoff — the seal was the legitimating object for two millennia of Chinese imperial succession.
- **Mission angle:** Multi-period investigation. Start with the historical chain of custody (Spring and Autumn period through Qin through Han through successive dynasties). Follow the trail through to the 10th-century loss event. Recover from wherever it ended up — likely buried in an unmarked grave or hidden in a temple reliquary the modern Chinese state has no record of.

### Xu Fu's Expedition Cache (Japan, speculative)

- **True nature:** If Xu Fu actually landed in Japan (Honshu, per Japanese folkloric tradition), and if he was an EPOCH agent (per [../Embedded-Agents/Candidates.md](../Embedded-Agents/Candidates.md)) — then his expedition almost certainly cached resources at the landing site. Equipment, fuel charges, possibly the bodies of the "three thousand virgins" if they were carrying samples of value.
- **Attribution:** EPOCH (probable).
- **Location / window:** Multiple candidate sites in Japan, primarily Saga Prefecture (Kyushu) and Shingū (Wakayama). The Xu Fu Park in Shingū and the Jofuku Shrine are built on traditional landing sites.
- **Mission angle:** Modern-era archaeological cover op. Race against (A) Japanese archaeologists who might publish findings, (B) EPOCH recovery teams who know exactly where their old cache is, (C) the modern Japanese government's protection of culturally significant sites.

### The Sacred Mirrors of Ise and Atsuta

See Three Imperial Regalia above. Yata-no-Kagami (Ise) and Kusanagi-no-Tsurugi (Atsuta) are the housed-at-shrine components. Both shrines have strict access restrictions that make even reconnaissance difficult.

### Daoist Alchemical Artifacts (varied)

Chinese alchemical tradition produced a substantial body of "immortality elixir" research that closely parallels the European Philosopher's Stone tradition. Most was poisonous nonsense (mercury-based elixirs killed multiple Tang emperors). A small subset may have been *attempted* orichalcum-infusion work by ATA-adjacent or independent practitioners. Possible recovery targets:

- **Ge Hong's *Baopuzi*** (4th c. CE) — alchemical treatise containing surprisingly detailed metallurgical observations. Some passages may encode orichalcum infusion technique.
- **Sun Simiao's *Beiji Qianjin Yaofang*** (7th c. CE) — medical treatise with anomalous diagnostic capabilities.
- **Unfinished Daoist alchemical instruments** recovered from various tomb sites, which may be early Chinese attempts at foundry tech.

Most mission targets here are *knowledge artifacts* — recovered documents that accelerate ATA's understanding of independent Bronze Age / Iron Age orichalcum research, hinting at whether there was a third East Asian agency or just isolated cultural recognition of the meteor-fragment principle.

## Open Questions

- **Excalibur's biometric key** — is the bond to a *person* or to a person's *signature* (transferable via cloning, descendants, or genetic match)? Has massive succession-of-the-wielder implications.
- **The Lake** — is it a geographic location, an EPOCH facility, or an extraction anchor? Recovering Excalibur properly might require finding the Lake and what's *in* it.
- **The "Holy" tier of artifacts** — does ATA actively *avoid* recovering objects venerated by living religions, or do they just operate quietly enough that no one notices? Different doctrines have very different play implications.
- **Excalibur and the Spear together** — Mark-VI sword and a morale-boost lance in the same wielder's hands. Has any historical leader had both at once? Should the campaign avoid letting players field both, or lean into the "this is what makes a god-king" implication?
- **EPOCH artifact recovery operations against ATA** — symmetric problem. When EPOCH learns ATA has the Grail in a Vatican vault (say), they run their own arc to extract it. Are these on-screen for players to oppose, or background tension?
- **The Atlantean Crown Jewels** — is there a defined "set" of major Atlantean-era artifacts that, when reassembled, unlock something significant? Tempting trope but might be too video-gamey.
