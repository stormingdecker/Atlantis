# Tunguska Impact, June 30 1908

> **Status:** Draft. A [4D shard-fall](../Core-Setting/The-Origin.md#4d-fragmentation) recovery race in the Siberian taiga — raw meteor matter is the single most valuable prize class. See also the [Missions Overview](Overview.md) and [Orichalcum](../Core-Setting/Orichalcum.md).

## Premise

At 7:17 a.m. local time a fragment of the Origin meteor arrived over the Podkamennaya Tunguska with no warning and no trajectory — a [4D fragmentation](../Core-Setting/The-Origin.md#4d-fragmentation) event, a chunk "falling from nowhere" into 1908. It airburst over the taiga and flattened roughly 2,000 km² of forest — some eighty million trees blown down in a radial fan — yet **left no crater.** The fragment partially *phased* on arrival: it did not fully land where the blast pattern says a body that size should have.

That non-landing is the whole prize. Raw, unrefined meteor matter is the richest source of foundry fuel there is (per [Orichalcum § What It Does](../Core-Setting/Orichalcum.md#what-it-does-mechanically)) — recovering it is a major, campaign-scale boost to whichever agency's foundry gets it. Both ATA and EPOCH register the fall almost immediately. **The mission is the race**, not a battle: get a team into one of the most remote places on Earth, find a fragment that isn't where physics says it is, and get it home before the rival team — or the first human witnesses — arrive.

The clock is real. History gives the site a **~19-day head start** before the earliest documented human reactions and years before any scientific expedition reaches it. Inside that window the taiga is empty, hostile, and contested by exactly one other party that wants what you want.

## Map & Setting

Wilderness-scale and horizontal, but staged as three linked tiles descending toward the locus (per [top-down VTT conventions](Overview.md#vtt-map-conventions)); the "vertical interest" here is thematic depth toward the buried fragment, not literal stacking.

- **Outer — the Blast Fan (30-ft wilderness scale).** The radial blow-down: kilometres of felled trunks all pointing outward from the airburst, char, standing "telegraph pole" snags at the center where trees kept their trunks. *Exploration pillar.* Movement is the challenge — the felled forest is difficult terrain that funnels and exhausts. Reading the fan's geometry is the first clue.
- **Middle — the Epicenter Marsh (10-ft scale).** The swampy ground beneath the burst point, where the blast pattern *says* the impact should be — and where there is conspicuously nothing. *Puzzle pillar.* Bog, thermal scarring, anomaly readings that don't line up with the crater that never formed.
- **Inner — the Phase Locus (5-ft tactical scale).** The true resting place of the phased fragment, offset from the geometric center. *Combat / tension pillar.* A small, strange clearing where the shard "arrived sideways" — warm, humming, lightly radiant, the recovery objective and the likely flashpoint if the rival team is close.

The locus tile earns a [boss-sequence](Overview.md#boss-sequence-escalation) art pass: the fragment is the "boss," even though the encounter may resolve without a shot.

## Objectives

1. **Insert and survive the taiga.** Cross the blast fan under period cover — no roads, no support, sub-arctic remoteness inside the historical blackout window.
2. **Find the true impact locus.** The blast geometry lies; triangulate where the fragment actually phased to.
3. **Recover the fragment.** Extract raw meteor matter — high-value, heavy, and (usefully) *negative-cost* to boost once secured.
4. **Contest or evade the rival team.** Beat EPOCH's recovery crew to the shard, or deny it to them if you can't carry it.
5. **Leave the record intact.** The Tunguska event stays a "natural" mystery — no recoverable tech, no anomaly a 1927 expedition could photograph.

## Encounters & Beats

**Exploration — the Blast Fan.** The insertion *is* the exploration challenge: navigating a flattened forest where every tree points the same way, managing fatigue and cold, and using the radial fan as a giant compass toward the burst point. Environmental hazards — unstable snags, scorched bog, lingering radiation/anomaly aftermath — do the work an enemy would elsewhere.

**Puzzle — the Offset Locus.** At the epicenter the team confronts the central mystery: the crater that should exist doesn't. The puzzle is triangulating the *phased* fragment from the mismatch between where the blast centered and where the mass actually went — a direct consequence of [4D arrival](../Core-Setting/The-Origin.md#4d-fragmentation). Solving it well beats the rival team to the locus; solving it slowly means arriving second.

**Combat / Tension — the Phase Locus.** The rival EPOCH recovery team is the pressure, not a set-piece slugfest. Reaching 1908 is comparatively cheap for ATA (near home in time) and costly for EPOCH — so their crew is small, focused, and reluctant to bleed for it. The beat can resolve as a stealth denial, a standoff over an object neither side can safely fight beside, a hazard-driven scramble, or a genuine firefight if both sides commit. The fragment's own instability is a hazard both teams share.

**Reveal (at debrief — not on this map):** whichever agency carried the shard home banks a foundry-fuel windfall that shifts the campaign's macro-progression (see [Orichalcum](../Core-Setting/Orichalcum.md)). If ATA lost the race, later EPOCH missions arrive better-equipped.

## Open Design Questions

- **What was the impact?** The stub asks whether Tunguska was a failed op. **Resolved: it was a natural [4D shard-fall](../Core-Setting/The-Origin.md#4d-fragmentation), not anyone's operation gone wrong.** Neither faction caused it; both are reacting to it. This keeps the "shards fall from nowhere" cosmology clean and makes the *race* — not a mistake to clean up — the mission.
- **How much fragment is recoverable?** One carry-home charge, or a scattered field the teams must pick over under time pressure? A single object makes a clean tug-of-war; a scatter field makes a longer, riskier recovery with partial-success outcomes.
- **The 19-day window as mechanic.** Do we model the historical blackout as a hard timer (arrive early, leave before witnesses) or as flavor? A visible clock sharpens the race; a soft one keeps the focus on the terrain puzzle.
- **Rival team composition.** A lean EPOCH far-traveler-supported crew (canon-clean, given the cost of reaching 1908) or a larger force that implies a nearby EPOCH anchor? The former keeps the temporal-gradient logic honest; recommend it.
- **Radiation / anomaly aftermath.** How lethal is the environmental hazard, and does contact with the phased fragment carry a lasting effect on agents (a boon, a mark, a cost)? Flag for the Genetics/Reality-Analysis teams before setting numbers.
