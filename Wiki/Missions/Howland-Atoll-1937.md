# Howland Atoll, July 2 1937

The Mercury extraction. ATA's second successful Reichenbach Protocol operation and the mission that retroactively codified the doctrine. Pacific theater, equatorial, hostile climate, EPOCH ambush in progress.

## Mission Brief

**Objective.** Extract Amelia Earhart from the wreckage of the Lockheed Electra moments before the historical disappearance becomes "permanent" in the record. Leave the scene forensically clean — no recoverable remains, no recoverable aircraft hardware that contradicts the canonical search-failure record.

**Window.** The extraction window opens at the moment the Electra ditches and closes when the US Navy search begins its first overflight (~36 hours). Within that window, EPOCH has its own operation in progress.

**Complications.**
1. **Noonan.** Fred Noonan, Earhart's navigator, is present and conscious. Doctrine says leave him — his disappearance is also canonical and unrecoverable. But players will want to save him. (See *Branching Outcomes.*)
2. **EPOCH ambush camp.** EPOCH has anchored a four-operator team on the atoll twelve hours before the ditching. They are *also* there for Earhart — sublimation attempt. ATA's intel is incomplete; the team learns about the EPOCH presence on insertion.
3. **The atoll itself.** Howland is uninhabited, ~1.5 square miles, low coral, sparse vegetation, no fresh water, no cover from above. Daytime operation is exposed. Nighttime operation is the doctrine.

## Map Layout

The mission uses three connected maps. Players move between them in sequence; each rewards a different gameplay pillar.

### Map 1: Insertion Approach (30-ft scale, wilderness)

Aerial-overhead view of the southwestern third of the atoll. Coral beach, scrub vegetation, low dunes. The ATA team inserts via small boat from an offshore drop point (an ATA submarine cover-asset). The map covers ~600 yards of beach approach toward the central interior.

**Features.**
- Boat landing zone (south edge).
- Coral reef tide line — visible from above as a color shift; restricts boat approach to two channels.
- Three scrub-vegetation patches usable for cover during daylight crossings.
- A single freshwater cache (ATA pre-positioned via earlier anchor) marked on GM layer only — finding it requires a successful Per check.
- EPOCH watchpost (NE corner of map) — a single operator with binoculars on a low dune. Players who fail stealth approach trigger the alarm and the mission shifts to combat-first.

**Pillar.** Exploration. Reading the map, choosing the approach line, finding the cache, spotting the watchpost.

### Map 2: The Crash Site (5-ft scale, tactical)

The Electra has ditched ~80 yards offshore in shallow water and washed onto the eastern reef. The map shows ~150 feet of reef-and-beach with the wrecked aircraft as the central feature.

**Features.**
- The Electra, broken-backed, partially submerged. Cockpit accessible from above (Earhart and Noonan inside, injured, conscious). Cabin section flooded.
- Reef pools — shallow water cover, line-of-sight blockers from above.
- A debris field of scattered cargo and aircraft fragments — usable as improvised cover, also as forensic-cleanup objectives (each piece left behind risks downstream anomaly).
- EPOCH operator (sublimation specialist) approaching from the north along the beach, ~80 yards out at scene start. She is not yet aware of the ATA team. She is carrying a sublimation kit — sealed container designed to look like a medical bag.
- A timing puzzle: Earhart's injuries are stabilizable but Noonan is bleeding internally. Extracting both requires either splitting the team (reducing combat effectiveness for the next map) or making a doctrinal choice to leave Noonan. Either is a valid play.

**Pillar.** Puzzle. Timing, forensic cleanup choices, the Noonan question, deciding whether to engage the EPOCH operator quietly or let her arrive and ambush her.

### Map 3: EPOCH Camp (5-ft scale, tactical)

The EPOCH camp is dug into a hollow behind the central dune line, ~400 yards inland. The map shows the camp and its immediate perimeter.

**Features.**
- Three tents in a triangle formation, central fire pit (unlit), supply cache.
- A pre-deployed EPOCH anchor — small obelisk-form device half-buried near the central fire pit. Disabling it costs the team a round but prevents EPOCH reinforcements from arriving via gradient transit.
- Three EPOCH operators present at scene start: a sniper (overwatch, NE dune), a medic (in the central tent with sublimation prep gear), and a heavy (patrolling perimeter). The fourth operator is the one from Map 2 — if she was killed quietly, she does not arrive; if she was let through, she arrives at this map as a fourth combatant.
- A captured EPOCH operations log (in the medic's tent) — recoverable intel about EPOCH's broader Pacific operations. Bonus objective.

**Pillar.** Combat. Tactical engagement with mixed-role EPOCH team, optional stealth-first approach (taking out the sniper opens the rest of the camp to ATA fire), the anchor decision (disable now or extract intel first).

## Branching Outcomes

- **Mercury extracted, Noonan left.** Doctrinal optimal. Mercury enters ATA service. Historical record intact. Slight personal weight on the team carrying her — she knows.
- **Mercury extracted, Noonan extracted.** Possible but costly. Noonan is a documented secondary disappearance — extracting him creates a small downstream anomaly the agency can absorb. Noonan becomes a junior NPC and a moral asset; player team earns a permanent +1 to morale-class checks. ATA leadership is privately displeased.
- **Mercury extracted, EPOCH team killed cleanly.** Standard win. EPOCH loses a sublimation specialist and a forward anchor.
- **Mercury extracted, EPOCH team escapes with intel.** EPOCH learns the Reichenbach Protocol is operational and accelerates its own sublimation timeline. Downstream missions get harder.
- **Mercury killed in extraction.** Failure state. The historical record is preserved (Earhart "disappeared") but ATA loses the asset. Mission counted as a failure.
- **Mercury sublimated by EPOCH.** Catastrophic failure. Mercury becomes an EPOCH operator. Encounters with her are scheduled for later in the campaign at boss tier.

## NPCs Present

- **Amelia Earhart** — injured, conscious, cooperative once she understands the situation. Will not leave Noonan if she has any say. See [Mercury.md](../Embedded-Agents/Mercury.md) for post-extraction characterization.
- **Fred Noonan** — bleeding out, semi-conscious. Speaks little. Player choice asset.
- **EPOCH Sublimation Specialist** — Grade-II operator. Field name unknown. Equipped with sealed sublimation kit.
- **EPOCH Sniper** — Grade-I operator. Bolt-action precision rifle.
- **EPOCH Medic** — Grade-II operator. Prep gear, light sidearm.
- **EPOCH Heavy** — Grade-II operator. Submachine gun.

## Music Suite

Four Suno prompts forming the mission's audio identity. See [Music-Conventions.md](../Design-Notes/Music-Conventions.md) for the four-tier structure and prompt format.

**Shared palette.** 1930s Pacific-theater instrumentation — muted trumpet, brushed snare, upright bass, warm strings, with a low ukulele drone reinforcing the equatorial setting. Shared mode: **A Aeolian**, which lets the Civilization tier pivot toward C major (Mercury's home key) for the wreck scene without breaking the palette. Boss tier layers the EPOCH faction signature (sub-bass pulse, granular pad) underneath.

### Tier 1 — Exploration (Insertion Approach)

> 1930s Pacific-theater wilderness theme, sparse muted trumpet over distant brushed snare, low ukulele drone, soft sustained strings, A minor Aeolian, 75 bpm. Recurring two-note breathing motif. Instrumental, atmospheric and exposed.

### Tier 2 — Civilization (The Wreck Scene)

The Civilization tier substitutes for a populated zone here — this is the social/character moment of the mission, the cockpit conversation with Earhart and Noonan. Quotes [Mercury's rising-fifth motif](../Design-Notes/Character-Themes.md#mercury) without resolving it.

> 1930s torch-song fragment, solo muted trumpet over warm strings and brushed snare, hinting at a rising-fifth motif (Mercury's theme) that does not quite resolve, A minor turning toward C major, 70 bpm. Instrumental, tender and dignified.

### Tier 3 — Combat (Crash Site Skirmish)

Used for the EPOCH sublimation specialist's arrival at the wreck and any flush-out engagements before the camp finale.

> 1930s Pacific-theater combat cue, urgent brushed snare with low brass stabs, muted trumpet figure, upright bass walking line, A minor Aeolian, 130 bpm. Recurring driving four-note motif. Instrumental, tense and tactical.

### Tier 4 — Boss (EPOCH Camp)

Full intensity, with the EPOCH faction signature layered underneath — sub-bass pulse and granular pad seeping in beneath the 1930s palette to signal the mission's true antagonist.

> 1930s Pacific brass-and-strings climax layered over an EPOCH sub-bass pulse and granular pad, full muted-trumpet melody, urgent strings, driving brushed snare, A minor Aeolian, 140 bpm. Recurring full-intensity descending motif. Instrumental, climactic and contested.
