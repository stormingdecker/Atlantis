# Historical Anomalies (Mission Seeds)

> **Status:** Draft. Real unexplained events reframed as shard-falls, extractions, or EPOCH ops — a holding page of "appeared-from-nowhere" hooks that seed self-contained missions and feed the larger arc threads. See the [Missions Overview](Overview.md) and [The Origin § 4D Fragmentation](../Core-Setting/The-Origin.md#4d-fragmentation).

The [artifact-reframing principle](../Artifacts/Candidates.md) has a sibling: not every anomaly is an *object* to recover. Some are *events* — a town that danced itself to death, a colony that vanished overnight, a civilization that fell in a generation. Mundane history files these under "unexplained," because the mundane record can only see the surface of an agency operation. This page collects the best of them and reframes each as ATA/EPOCH activity: a shard arriving trajectory-less "from nowhere," an embed community pulled out ahead of a threat, a leaked psi field, or — worst of all — EPOCH reaching into ATA's *completed* history and unwinding it (see the [Reality Log](../Core-Setting/Reality-Log.md)).

Each entry gives the real historical account first, then the in-setting truth, the agency angle, and how it plays. Where a reframe leans on soft canon, it's flagged inline — treat those as GM-tunable, not settled.

## How to Read This Page

- **What really happened** is the factual historical record — no reframe, so the GM knows what the players can actually research.
- **True nature** is the in-setting reframe.
- **Agency angle** is who did it (ATA, EPOCH, or a botched op) and why.
- **Mission hook** is how it comes to the table.

## Shard-Falls & EPOCH Origins

### The Tarim Mummies (excavated 1900s onward; burials ~2000 BCE–200 CE)

- **What really happened:** In the Tarim Basin — the Taklamakan desert of what is now Xinjiang, northwest China — the arid, salty ground has naturally desiccated hundreds of Bronze- and Iron-Age burials into some of the best-preserved mummies on Earth. The oldest cemeteries, **Xiaohe** ("Small River") and **Loulan**, hold individuals with West-Eurasian ("Caucasoid") features, felt hats, tartan-style woven wool, and cheese and wheat grave goods — a cultural toolkit that looked wildly *out of place* so far east, and for a century made the Tarim people an archaeological puzzle: who were they, and how did they get there? (Ancient-DNA work published in 2021 complicated the migration story — the earliest Tarim population was genetically a local, long-isolated Ancient North Eurasian group that was *culturally* cosmopolitan rather than a wave of newcomers — but the "why here, why like this" strangeness remains.)
- **True nature:** EPOCH's earliest foothold in history. The Kunlun shard is spatially *there* — high on the Tibetan plateau whose northern wall, the Kunlun range, drops into the Tarim Basin — even though the shard does not "arrive" until the ~2200s (see [The Kunlun Shard](../Core-Setting/The-Kunlun-Shard.md)). To build a home ground at their own anchor's landing coordinate, EPOCH pushed [far-traveler](../Core-Setting/Temporal-Gradient.md#personal-transit-tolerance--the-far-traveler-advantage) embeds and a support population *back* to the habitable ground nearest that point — the basin below the mountains — and let them settle, marry, and die there across centuries. The mummies are that support population: the cosmopolitan material culture is the residue of people equipped from a future they never named. This makes the Tarim Basin EPOCH's cross-time home turf, and this entry the most direct EPOCH-origin tie on the page. *(Geography flag: the shard's exact 2200s impact point is Tibetan-plateau per canon; siting the embed community in the adjoining Tarim Basin is the reframe's one spatial liberty — the plateau proper is brutal to settle, the basin is where people actually lived.)*
- **Agency angle:** EPOCH, deliberate and foundational. Reaching the deep Bronze Age is enormously expensive from a future anchor — so this was not a raid but a *colonization*, an investment of scarce far-traveler capacity to plant roots at the one coordinate that matters most to them. Anything ATA learns here is a look at EPOCH before EPOCH technically exists.
- **Mission hook:** Two windows. **(A) Early-20th-century archaeology insert** — as the first European expeditions (Hedin, Stein) open the basin, ATA embeds among the diggers to read the burials before the mundane record locks in an interpretation, and to grasp *why* the finds itch at the Chronograph. **(B) Bronze-Age insert** — a costly, deep-drop expedition into ATA's own exclusive era to investigate a living EPOCH founding-community on the ground: the dread of finding the enemy already *at home* in the deep past. Ties naturally into [The Long 1910s](../Campaigns/The-Long-1910s.md) via window (A).
- **→ Full mission:** built out as **[The Tarim Foothold](Tarim-Foothold.md)**.

## Unwindings

### The Bronze Age Collapse (~1177 BCE)

- **What really happened:** Within roughly two generations around 1200–1150 BCE, the interlocking civilizations of the eastern Mediterranean and Near East came apart almost at once — Mycenaean Greece, the Hittite Empire, the great trading city of Ugarit, and more collapsed or shrank drastically; cities burned, palace economies and long-distance trade broke down, literacy contracted, and a centuries-long "dark age" followed. Egyptian records under Ramesses III describe waves of raiders they called the **"Sea Peoples,"** repelled in a great battle dated to 1177 BCE. Historians still debate the mix of causes: prolonged drought, earthquakes, migration, and the fragility of over-connected systems all feature — no single trigger is agreed.
- **True nature:** EPOCH **unwinding an entire region of ATA's completed history.** The collapse is not a natural cascade — it's the *symptom* of a large EPOCH operation aimed at erasing ATA's Bronze-Age power base, the Mediterranean home ground where ATA is strongest. Systems don't all fail at once by accident; they fail at once when someone reaches into finished history and pulls the load-bearing threads. The "Sea Peoples" are the operation's cover and vector — the visible edge of a deletion, mythologized by survivors into a horde of raiders.
- **Agency angle:** EPOCH, arc-scale. This is the same terrifying capability the [Knossos Anteroom](Knossos-Anteroom.md) mission first exposes on a small scale — EPOCH reaching into ATA's *completed* past — but applied to a whole civilization. It directly escalates [Arc 01](../Campaigns/Arc-01.md)'s second-tier truth into a strategic threat: ATA is not just fighting for the future, it is fighting to keep its own foundation from being edited out from under it. 1177 BCE sits at the far, expensive edge of EPOCH's reach — so the operation is slow, indirect, and enormous, which is exactly why it *reads* as impersonal collapse. See [History § Bronze Age](../History/Bronze-Age.md) and [Factions/EPOCH.md](../Factions/EPOCH.md).
- **Mission hook:** Not a one-shot — an arc thread. ATA detects the collapse *happening in its own logs*, entries it filed centuries ago beginning to fray. Missions run interleaved through the collapse window: identify which "Sea Peoples" landfalls are EPOCH vectors versus mundane refugees, protect specific anchor sites and embed lineages, and race to shore up the foundation before the unwind propagates downstream into ATA's present. The horror beat: some of what's already gone can't be gotten back.

## Psi Leaks & Broadcasts

### The Dancing Plague of 1518 (Strasbourg)

- **What really happened:** In July 1518, in Strasbourg (then a free city of the Holy Roman Empire), a woman remembered as Frau Troffea began to dance in the street and could not stop. Within weeks as many as **~400 people** were dancing uncontrollably, day and night; contemporary chronicles and city records report that some collapsed and a number *died* — from exhaustion, strokes, or heart failure. Baffled authorities first prescribed *more* dancing (hiring musicians and clearing halls) before reversing course. The episode is well documented and still genuinely unexplained; leading theories range from ergot poisoning to stress-driven mass psychogenic illness, none fully satisfying.
- **True nature:** A [glamour-psi](../Embedded-Agents/Helen.md) field **leak.** A malfunctioning or overloaded psi asset — a burnt-out engineered-charisma operative, or a Mark psi-emitter left running and degrading — is contaminating the town, driving involuntary entrainment in everyone inside its radius. The dancing is the crude motor expression of a compulsion field with no operator steering it. (See [Psionics](../Core-Setting/Psionics.md) for the ability framework; glamour-psi as a *broadcast* rather than a targeted effect is the soft-canon lever this entry turns.)
- **Agency angle:** Ambiguous by design — ATA *or* EPOCH. The source could be a stranded ATA asset the team must recover, or discarded EPOCH materiel the team must deny. Either way the priority is containment without exposure: a 16th-century city cannot be allowed to learn what's actually happening, and the toll climbs every day the source runs.
- **Mission hook:** An eerie, near-zero-combat **containment** one-shot. The pillars come from investigation, not a firefight: triangulate the leak through the pattern of afflicted (exploration), diagnose and safely shut down or extract the source without becoming entrained yourselves (puzzle), and handle the one hard confrontation only if the source is a *person* who doesn't want to be switched off. Thematic sibling to the Fátima broadcast in [The Long 1910s](../Campaigns/The-Long-1910s.md).

## Vanishings & Extractions

### Roanoke (1590) & the Mary Celeste (1872)

- **What really happened:** **Roanoke** — in 1587 a group of ~115 English colonists settled on Roanoke Island (in present-day North Carolina). Governor John White sailed to England for supplies; delayed by war, he returned in 1590 to find the settlement empty, no sign of struggle, and the word **"CROATOAN"** carved into a post (and "CRO" on a nearby tree) — an agreed signal pointing to a nearby island, yet the colonists were never found. **The Mary Celeste** — in December 1872 the American brigantine was discovered adrift in the Atlantic near the Azores, **seaworthy and largely provisioned**, cargo mostly intact, but completely abandoned: the captain, his wife and young daughter, and the crew — around ten people — were gone, along with the ship's single lifeboat. No trace of them was ever found, and no accepted explanation exists.
- **True nature:** Botched or emergency **era-extractions.** *Roanoke:* an embed community pulled out ahead of an incoming threat — a hurried evacuation, not a disappearance; "CROATOAN" is the last human-legible marker of an extraction rendezvous, a message left for a pickup that had to move. *Mary Celeste:* a crew caught inside an extraction or a transit anomaly — either bystanders swept up when an operation went off next to them, or an embed pickup that took the whole deck by accident, the missing lifeboat the sign of a panicked, only-partly-successful pull.
- **Agency angle:** Either faction, depending on the table — an ATA embed community running for cover, or an EPOCH pickup that fractured. What both leave behind is an **extraction signature**: the transit residue, timing, and physical tells that let a trained team read *how* people left rather than *that* they left.
- **Mission hook:** Self-contained investigation **one-shots**, ideal as introductory or palate-cleanser sessions. Insert after the fact, read the extraction signature against the [Reality Log](../Core-Setting/Reality-Log.md), recover whatever was left, and decide what the mundane record is allowed to keep. Low combat, high dread; two clean, portable mysteries with a shared underlying mechanic.

## Open Design Questions

- **How much EPOCH-origin lore to spend on the Tarim entry.** It's the biggest lore payoff here — do players get the full "EPOCH was in the deep past before it existed" reveal in one mission, or is it seeded across both the archaeology window and a later Bronze-Age drop?
- **Bronze Age Collapse as playable vs. backdrop.** An unwind that big may be more powerful as a looming strategic threat than as a map the team stands on. Where's the line between "arc thread the players feel" and "arc thread the players fight"?
- **Attribution ambiguity.** The Dancing Plague and the Vanishings are deliberately faction-agnostic. Is that flexibility worth keeping, or does the campaign want each event pinned to a specific side for continuity?
- **Which anomalies get real mission pages.** Tarim is now promoted ([The Tarim Foothold](Tarim-Foothold.md)); the Bronze Age Collapse is the next candidate. Leave the rest as GM-tunable hooks.
- **Signature consistency.** If "extraction signature" and "psi-field leak" become recurring diagnostic beats, they need shared mechanics so players learn to read them across missions. Define once, reuse.
