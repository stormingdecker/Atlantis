# Atlantean Transit Authority (ATA)

The keepers of the Origin.

## Identity

The ATA presents to the modern public as a multinational regulatory body — ostensibly governing maritime transit infrastructure in the eastern Mediterranean. In truth, they govern transit through *time*. They hold (or claim to hold) sovereignty over the Atlantean site and the meteor itself.

## Posture

- **Preservationist (officially).** Their stated mission is to stabilize the timeline, clean up anomalies, and protect history from interference.
- **Surgical.** ATA agents prefer one operative in one place at one moment to nudge a single decision. Subtlety as doctrine.
- **Resource-rich.** They own the Origin meteor — the largest known gradient anchor. They can afford patience, redundancy, and long embeds. They also exclusively control the deep Bronze Age (4000–2300 BCE), an era [EPOCH](EPOCH.md) cannot physically reach.
- **Ideologically committed to democratic institutional capacity.** ATA fights to preserve the historical chain that produces modern democracy as a globally distributed achievement — free elections, free press, protected dissent, rule of law, political pluralism, civil society. Modern democracies live in Japan, India, South Korea, Taiwan, post-Soviet Eastern Europe, the Americas, and increasingly across Africa, not only in Western Europe and North America. ATA preserves the *democratic chain* as a multi-civilizational achievement, not a Western tribal one.
- **Existentially motivated.** ATA fights for the historical conditions that produced the modern democratic order. If EPOCH succeeds in disrupting those conditions — preventing democratic consolidation, replacing pluralism with hegemonic consolidation — then the Atlantean site eventually falls under different control, the modern ATA never forms, and every ATA agent ceases to have existed. Bootstrap survival is the deepest stake. This is not an abstract concern: it is the *reason* ATA fights the war as a war at all.

## Anchor Network

ATA's embed network is densest in the Mediterranean, Western Europe, and North Africa — regions spatially adjacent to the Origin and culturally central to ATA's modern institutional reach. East Asia, India, sub-Saharan Africa, and the Americas are sparsely anchored: each operation in those regions pays a high spatial-cost component, and establishing new anchors is a long-arc strategic investment. See [../Core-Setting/Temporal-Gradient.md](../Core-Setting/Temporal-Gradient.md#anchor-network-density) for the full anchor density breakdown and what it means for mission planning.

This asymmetry means East Asian campaigns are *contested with EPOCH home-field advantage* — ATA teams insert as the away team, paying more cost, with fewer local embeds, against an EPOCH-dense regional network. Mediterranean campaigns are the mirror image.

## Doctrinal Self-Image

ATA's internal culture frames its mission as guardianship, not aggression. The agents see themselves as preventing the *worst* historical outcomes — a 20th century without the Allied victories, a 21st century without democratic consolidation, a 2200s in which the planetary hegemony EPOCH defends is consolidated *earlier* and *deeper.*

This self-image is not entirely accurate. ATA does aggressive things — it installs preferred outcomes, suppresses inconvenient figures, manipulates institutions for strategic ends. It is not morally pristine. But it is *ideologically coherent*: agents broadly believe that the system they're defending is materially better than the alternative, and they're willing to do compromising work to preserve it.

The campaign should leave room for player characters to question this. Some ATA agents are true believers; some are pragmatists; some are cynics who have come to see the mission as a job. All three coexist inside the agency, and the philosophical tension is genuine. See [EPOCH.md](EPOCH.md) for the symmetric account of the other side.

## Recruitment

ATA recruits from the modern day: historians, soldiers, polyglots, athletes, scientists, occasionally civilians who stumbled into something they shouldn't have. Player characters are almost certainly ATA recruits, or starting that way.

Recruitment is staged. New agents are told a sanitized version — usually some "deep-state archaeological intelligence" cover — and only learn the truth of the meteor after passing several missions.

### The Reichenbach Protocol

ATA's standing doctrine for recruiting **historical figures** is named after [Sigerson](../Embedded-Agents/Sigerson.md)'s 1891 extraction at Reichenbach Falls. The principle: when a figure of exceptional capability is approaching a canonical death event, ATA extracts them at the last possible moment, preserving them for revival, infusion, and integration as a senior agent. The historical record is left intact — the public believes the death happened — and the agency gains an operator whose skills would otherwise be lost.

**Selection criteria.** ATA's Reality Analysis division maintains a standing roster of candidates. A figure earns Protocol consideration when:

1. They are *exceptional* in a capability the agency values (natural psi, rare technical skill, period-irreplaceable expertise, historical access to a specific era or institution).
2. Their canonical death event is *clean* — forensically unrecoverable, isolated, or already mysterious in the historical record. Ocean ditchings, fatal falls, fires, single-witness disappearances, and unwitnessed solo events are all ideal.
3. Recovery of remains in the historical record is *known to have failed*. ATA does not extract figures whose bodies were later identified, autopsied, or buried; the forensic record cannot be retroactively altered without rippling history.
4. The extraction window is operationally accessible — an anchor can be placed within range, a transit team can be deployed, and the death event itself can be timed precisely.

**Why the doctrine is conservative.** Every visible-historical-figure extraction creates downstream causality management problems. The extracted agent is now a known face in modern records; their pre-extraction history is documented and findable; the operational tradecraft of *hiding them in plain sight* is expensive. ATA's Reality Analysis division rejects most Protocol candidates on grounds that the visibility-vs-capability tradeoff doesn't pencil. The two known successes — Sigerson (1891) and [Mercury](../Embedded-Agents/Mercury.md) (1937) — both passed unusually clean tests on all four criteria.

**What the Protocol is *not*.** ATA does not engineer the death event. ATA does not extract figures *before* their canonical death (which would leave a forensic anomaly the historical record cannot absorb). ATA does not extract figures whose deaths are politically or culturally load-bearing — Lincoln's assassination, Caesar's, JFK's, the deaths whose downstream consequences shape the world that ATA is preserving. The Protocol operates only at the seams of history, never at its load-bearing joints.

**Tells and constraints in play.** Reichenbach-extracted agents share recognizable patterns: known faces, documented biographies, dangerous public exposure risks, the psychological weight of having been *recorded as dead* by everyone they loved. They are also the agency's most-recognizable senior operatives, with all the operational complications that implies. The agency's tradecraft for managing them is one of its core competencies.

See [../Embedded-Agents/Candidates.md#extraction-roster](../Embedded-Agents/Candidates.md#extraction-roster) for the speculative roster of additional candidates whose death profiles fit the Protocol — and EPOCH's parallel doctrine of [Asset Sublimation](EPOCH.md#asset-sublimation).

## Open Questions

- Public preservationist face vs. real mission — are they actually preserving, or actively re-shaping under cover?
- Internal politics — reformist wing vs. orthodoxy? A "Curator" caste vs. operational agents? Doves vs. hawks on EPOCH? Realists vs. ideologues on the democratic-preservation framing?
- Leadership structure — anonymous council, a single figure, a committee, an AI?
- What's the *real* reveal moment for new agents, and what tests gate it?
- Do ATA leadership themselves know what the meteor IS, or are they also in the dark?
- Are there ATA agents who have *defected* to EPOCH after coming to see the hegemonic philosophy as preferable? What happened to them?
- Does the rank-and-file know that EPOCH's 2200s base society is, by its own internal measures, prosperous and stable? Or is that fact restricted to senior intelligence?
