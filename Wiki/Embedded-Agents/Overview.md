## Two Categories

### Undercover Embeds

Agents living quiet lives — merchants, scribes, courtesans, soldiers — embedded as ordinary people of their era. Their purpose is *infrastructure*: maintaining extraction anchors, gathering local intelligence, supporting transient operations, occasionally executing low-visibility nudges to history.

Mundane humans cannot detect them. Other infused agents can — see [Agent-Sense](../Core-Setting/Orichalcum.md#agent-sense). The classic ATA-vs-EPOCH "cocktail party" tension applies: undercover embeds from rival agencies who share an era often know about one another and tacitly avoid open conflict to preserve both their covers.

### Visible Embeds (Named Historical Figures)

Agents whose mission *requires* visibility — pharaohs, queens, generals, oracles, philosophers, religious founders. They operate as recognizable named figures of history. The cover is the role itself.

Visible embeds are rarer, more expensive to insert, and far more politically delicate than undercover embeds. They are also dramatically more impactful: a properly placed pharaoh shapes the next two thousand years of downstream history.

For mission-design purposes, **visible embeds function as boss-tier antagonists or quest-givers** depending on faction alignment. They are not encounters players can avoid by stealth; they are the encounters players have *come for*.

## The EPOCH Power Curve

EPOCH's visible embeds are, in the early campaign, dramatically more dangerous than ATA's. Three reasons:

1. **Technological asymmetry.** EPOCH agents come from 150+ years in ATA's future. Their genetic engineering, psi-induction protocols, and equipment all reflect 2200s technology. ATA's Mark IV foundry is generations behind EPOCH's Mark VI+.
2. **Doctrinal aggressiveness.** EPOCH builds embeds for *maximum impact*. Where ATA installs a quiet pharaoh, EPOCH installs a charismatic god-king with deliberate psi-augmentation, engineered beauty, and a private army.
3. **Infusion saturation.** Senior EPOCH embeds carry far heavier infusion loads than ATA standard. Agent-sense reads them as *dense* presences. Their psi capabilities are amplified accordingly.

**This is intentional campaign shape.** Early-game EPOCH embeds are *overpowered relative to a starting ATA team*. The team's job in those early arcs is usually *not* to defeat them in direct confrontation — it is to investigate, contain, evade, or extract while taking losses. Defeating an EPOCH embed in the early campaign is a *major* achievement and a turning-point reward.

As the campaign progresses and ATA's foundry mark expands (through player-recovered tech and captured EPOCH biotech), the asymmetry closes. By the mid-late campaign, a well-equipped ATA team can credibly hunt EPOCH embeds head-on.

This is the campaign's macro-difficulty curve: not "monsters get bigger" but "you stop being outclassed."

## Mechanical Notes

For encounter and mission design:

- **Visible embeds are always Agent-Sense detectable.** Players cannot stealth their way past one infused agent into a room with another. Once both sides are in range, both sides *know*.
- **Their psi capability is usually their primary threat vector**, not direct combat. Players who try to fight an EPOCH embed head-on without first neutralizing or interrupting their psi are making a strategic error.
- **Capturing an embed is far more valuable than killing one.** A captured EPOCH embed is intelligence, biotech sample, and political leverage. Live captures are processed at the [Atlantis Genetics Lab](../Core-Setting/Atlantis-Facility.md) (I-6) for biotech analysis — each one accelerates ATA's research stream by years, and at higher Lab tiers unlocks Research Upgrades the agency could not otherwise acquire. ATA central command will frequently reward capture-and-extract missions over kill missions, even when killing is easier.
- **Extraction is hard but possible.** Per the gradient mechanic, an embed's drop home is cheap — which means once you've engaged one, you have a limited window before they can simply fall up the well. Anchor-jamming or fuel-dampening tech (where available) extends the window. Without it, the team must complete the engagement very quickly.

See [../Design-Notes/Mission-Design.md](../Design-Notes/Mission-Design.md) for general mission structure; embed-targeting missions are a recurring high-stakes mission type that crosses the Investigate/Restore/Exploit categories.
