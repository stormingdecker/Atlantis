# Candidate Embedded Agents & Mission Hooks

> **Speculative roster.** None of these attributions are canon. Brainstorm material — figures whose historical profile *looks like* an agent operation, plus the missions their lives suggest. Lock in as canon when a specific arc needs them.

## How to Read This Page

- **Faction guess** is my best read on which agency *would* run them. `?` means contested or undecided.
- **The tell** is what makes them suspicious — anomalous capability, lucky timing, weird gaps in the historical record, results too clean to be organic.
- **Mission angle** is the player-facing hook.

Some figures only make sense if their *adversary* is also an agent — those are flagged as pairs.

## Antiquity (3000 BCE – 500 CE)

### Akhenaten (Egypt, ~1350 BCE)

- **Faction guess:** ATA.
- **The tell:** Imposed monotheism on a 2000-year-old polytheistic state, more or less by personal decree. Built an entire new capital from nothing. The reform died with him and was deliberately erased from the record by successors. The whole episode reads like a *failed* preview operation — ATA testing whether monotheism could be installed early.
- **Mission angle:** Investigate the failure. Was Akhenaten ATA going off-script? Was the erasure an EPOCH counter-op? Did the project leak something Egyptian priesthood couldn't tolerate?

### Xerxes & Leonidas (Persian Wars, ~480 BCE) — pair

- **Faction guess:** Xerxes — EPOCH. Leonidas — ATA, possibly with the 300 as an embed cadre.
- **The tell:** EPOCH wants Persian dominance to consolidate eastward earlier (per the EPOCH doctrine in [../Factions/EPOCH.md](../Factions/EPOCH.md)). Persian conquest of Greece would have prevented Greek philosophical-scientific tradition from forming, kneecapping Western institutional development by 2000 years. ATA *had* to make Thermopylae and Salamis happen.
- **Mission angle:** Players insert during the Persian Wars to support the Greek defense without revealing themselves. Thermopylae is a fixed point; the question is whether the players can keep it that way against EPOCH operatives trying to sabotage Greek coordination.

### Alexander the Great (~336–323 BCE)

- **Faction guess:** ATA (with significant doubt).
- **The tell:** Conquered the known world before age 33, then died suddenly under unclear circumstances. The Hellenization of the Near East that resulted is one of the most consequential cultural transmissions in history — it laid the literate Greek infrastructure that Rome inherited and Christianity rode. The early death is suspicious; was it ATA pulling him before he could go off-mission, or EPOCH succeeding in an assassination ATA couldn't prevent?
- **Mission angle:** Babylon, 323 BCE. Alexander is dying. Is this his extraction window, an EPOCH kill, or a mundane fever? Players have hours to find out and act.

### Hannibal & Scipio (Punic Wars, 264–146 BCE) — pair

- **Faction guess:** Hannibal — EPOCH. Scipio — ATA.
- **The tell:** Hannibal was a brilliant general from a culture that wasn't supposed to be able to challenge Rome. Scipio reversed it. Carthage's destruction set the geopolitical shape of the Mediterranean for the next 600 years. EPOCH would *love* a Carthaginian-dominant Mediterranean (Phoenician maritime culture has different downstream consequences than Latin-Roman); ATA needs Rome to consolidate the West.
- **Mission angle:** A multi-arc campaign in itself. The three Punic Wars as a slow-burn agency war. Crossing the Alps with the elephants is one mission. The Battle of Zama is another. The salting of Carthage is the dark capstone.

### Julius Caesar & Brutus (Rome, ~100–44 BCE) — pair

- **Faction guess:** Caesar — ATA. Brutus — EPOCH.
- **The tell:** Caesar's rise from a moderately distinguished family to dictator-for-life in a single generation is anomalous even for the late Republic. His cultural and administrative reforms (the Julian calendar, citizenship reform, Roman provincial integration) are *exactly* the kind of system-level changes that propagate forward across the entire Empire. The assassination derailed the trajectory ATA was building. Brutus's role as the conspiracy's moral cover suggests an EPOCH operative carefully constructed to give the assassination ideological legitimacy.
- **Mission angle:** **The Ides of March Arc.** Players insert in 45 BCE knowing the assassination is coming. They cannot prevent it — that would catastrophically ripple the timeline ATA itself is built on. But they can investigate *who*, find evidence Brutus is EPOCH, and prevent the same operatives from doing it again with the next reformer. The mission's reward is intelligence, not prevention.

### Cleopatra VII (Egypt, ~69–30 BCE)

- **Faction guess:** Long-embed. Faction contested — possibly ATA early, defected or replaced by EPOCH late.
- **The tell:** Unusually competent diplomat, polyglot (reputed to speak 9 languages — flag for engineered cognitive enhancement), played Rome's most powerful men against each other for 20 years. The "asp" suicide is suspicious — could be an extraction cover story.
- **Mission angle:** Did she die? Was the suicide an extraction? If she was extracted, where to and by whom? Modern-day investigation arc: ATA discovers Cleopatra signature in 1st-century-CE archives elsewhere. Did she just go home?

### Archimedes & the Siege of Syracuse (212 BCE)

- **Faction guess:** Archimedes — ATA, proto-Mark-III tech leak.
- **The tell:** His engineering designs (the Claw, the heat ray, advanced compound machines) are *centuries* ahead of his era. The historical record explicitly notes Roman soldiers were terrified of his weapons. He was killed by a Roman soldier despite Marcellus ordering him spared. The killing reads like an EPOCH counter-op — ATA's tech was leaking *too fast*, accelerating Roman engineering capability beyond EPOCH's tolerable bounds.
- **Mission angle:** **The Siege of Syracuse Arc.** ATA wants Archimedes extracted before the city falls (he's leaked enough; further leak is destabilizing). EPOCH wants him dead. Players insert during the siege with hours to find him and get him out. The fall of the city is the mission clock.

### Hypatia (Alexandria, ~370–415 CE)

- **Faction guess:** ATA. Already canon-adjacent — see [../Core-Setting/Reality-Log.md](../Core-Setting/Reality-Log.md), where her extraction is one of the example Reality Log deltas.
- **The tell:** Brilliant Neoplatonist mathematician and philosopher in a city where her demographic, profession, and religion all made her vulnerable. Murdered by a mob in the orthodox record. ATA's intervention extracted her in 414 CE, replacing the public narrative with a retirement-and-natural-death story.
- **Mission angle:** Already the canonical Reality Log example. Could anchor a "what happens to ATA-extracted historical figures *after* extraction" arc — where do they go, who handles them, what's the long-tail support look like?

## Late Antiquity & Medieval (500 – 1400 CE)

### Justinian & Theodora (Byzantium, ~530s CE) — pair

- **Faction guess:** ATA.
- **The tell:** Massive legal codification (the Justinian Code propagated forward for 1500 years), Hagia Sophia engineering, Theodora's anomalous rise from actress to empress and her stabilizing role during the Nika riots. The pair operated as a unit. Codification is a *classic* ATA preservation move — lock institutional memory into a transmissible document.
- **Mission angle:** Constantinople, 532 CE. Nika riots in progress. ATA needs Theodora to stay calm and convince Justinian not to flee. EPOCH operatives are working the rioters to make sure he *does*. Players insert as palace staff during the worst of it.

### Charlemagne (~768–814 CE)

- **Faction guess:** ATA.
- **The tell:** Unified post-Roman Europe under a literate, Christianized administration after 300 years of fragmentation. The Carolingian Renaissance preserved classical texts that would otherwise have been lost. His coronation as Holy Roman Emperor in 800 CE founded a political tradition that lasted a millennium.
- **Mission angle:** Aachen, court intrigue, manuscript preservation operations. Lower-stakes era for ATA — players might be embedded scribes salvaging texts before they're destroyed in monastery raids.

### Genghis Khan (~1200 CE)

- **Faction guess:** EPOCH (cautious).
- **The tell:** The Mongol conquests reshaped Eurasian power for 200+ years and devastated the Islamic Golden Age (Baghdad 1258). Pulling Islamic intellectual centers offline during the Crusades is *plausibly* an EPOCH operation to slow institutional development across Eurasia.
- **Mission angle:** This is dangerous territory — making a real historical conqueror "the villain" via faction attribution needs careful handling. Maybe better as a *natural* historical force ATA and EPOCH both react to, rather than as an embed. Open for discussion.

## Renaissance & Early Modern (1400 – 1800 CE)

### Leonardo da Vinci (~1452–1519 CE)

- **Faction guess:** ATA, almost certainly.
- **The tell:** *Everything*. Flying machines, anatomical sketches centuries ahead of their era, hydraulic engineering, optics, the systematic notebook-keeping style that resembles modern engineering documentation more than any contemporary practice. His notebooks read like an embed agent who *kept forgetting to dial it down*. The unfinished commissions read like an agent constantly being pulled to other operations.
- **Mission angle:** Florence, 1490s. Leonardo is leaking too fast — his designs are being seen by patrons. Players are sent to either suppress specific notebooks before they circulate, or to *recover* lost notebooks before EPOCH gets them. The Tank, the Helicopter, the Self-Propelled Cart — each is a potential mark-leak ATA needs contained.

### Galileo (~1564–1642 CE)

- **Faction guess:** ATA observer, possibly not a full agent.
- **The tell:** His telescope-based observations are real natural genius, not necessarily tech leak. But the *publication strategy* and the Inquisition confrontation read like managed exposure — ATA testing how much heliocentrism the institutional Church could absorb. The recantation was the survival outcome; the slow institutional shift over the following century was the real win.
- **Mission angle:** Rome, 1633. Players are present at the Inquisition trial as observers. Their job: ensure Galileo recants (live to fight another day) without ATA exposure. Complications: EPOCH wants him executed (to discredit early scientific revolution); a sympathetic Cardinal wants to help him escape (which would actually be worse for ATA's long game).

### Isaac Newton (~1643–1727 CE)

- **Faction guess:** ATA, but a problematic one.
- **The tell:** Calculus, optics, gravitation — all natural genius probably. But his alchemy work and his obsession with Biblical chronology suggest *something else* was going on. Working hypothesis: Newton was an ATA agent given partial information about the Origin meteor and spent decades trying to reverse-engineer parachronics from theological hints. He never quite got there. ATA suppressed his alchemy notebooks after his death.
- **Mission angle:** Modern-era arc. Newton's missing alchemy notebooks have surfaced at a Cambridge auction. EPOCH wants them. ATA wants them destroyed or recovered. Players run a contemporary intelligence op.

### Catherine the Great (~1762–1796 CE)

- **Faction guess:** ATA.
- **The tell:** German-born princess who took the Russian throne in a coup, ran the empire competently for 34 years, and corresponded extensively with Enlightenment philosophes. Russia's modernization on her watch is anomalously rapid for the era. The fact that EPOCH's home base is *future Russia* makes ATA's interest in Catherine's-era Russian institutional development particularly pointed.
- **Mission angle:** St. Petersburg, late 1700s. ATA-EPOCH proxy struggle for the trajectory of Russian institutional culture. Catherine herself is the operation; players are her staff, her ambassadors, her trusted couriers.

## Industrial & Modern (1800 – 1950 CE)

### Napoleon (~1769–1821 CE)

- **Faction guess:** Contested — possibly EPOCH initially, then a defection or replacement during the Hundred Days.
- **The tell:** Anomalous rise, anomalous reach, anomalous collapse. The Napoleonic Code echoes Justinian as a preservation-by-codification move (ATA-style), but the imperial overreach and the Russian invasion are catastrophic strategic errors that *feel* like EPOCH-driven self-sabotage. Both agencies probably had hands on him at different points.
- **Mission angle:** Multiple. Egyptian campaign (Rosetta Stone — ATA artifact recovery), Waterloo (extraction-or-kill window), the Hundred Days (which agency is running him *now*?).

### Tesla & Edison (~1856–1943 CE) — pair, sort of

- **Faction guess:** Tesla — ATA controlled-leak experiment. Edison — mundane, but EPOCH-influenced via patent suppression.
- **The tell:** Tesla's wireless power, his radio prior art, his "death ray" notebooks — all suggest someone with future-tech intuition trying to selectively introduce capabilities. His personal eccentricities and poverty in old age fit the profile of an agent whose handlers cut him loose. Edison's role in *suppressing* Tesla's most disruptive ideas (AC vs DC, patent wars) could be EPOCH manipulating mundane institutional channels.
- **Mission angle:** Modern era, FBI seizure of Tesla's papers after his 1943 death. Players are present at the seizure — ATA wants the dangerous notebooks recovered before they're filed away in government archives EPOCH might reach.

### The 1957 "Defector" (Moscow)

- **Faction guess:** EPOCH. Already canon — see [../Factions/EPOCH.md](../Factions/EPOCH.md).
- **The tell:** The entire ATA narrative of EPOCH's origins is fabricated around him.
- **Mission angle:** Modern-day investigation — locate him if still alive, capture for interrogation, or confirm his fate.

## East Asian Theaters

The Kunlun Shard's spatial location in Tibet makes East Asia EPOCH's regional home turf — dense embed network, lower spatial-cost insertions. ATA operates there as the away team. This makes East Asian campaigns structurally different from Mediterranean ops: more reliance on field improvisation, less local-embed support, more direct contact with EPOCH-aligned regional powers.

Most of these candidates are EPOCH-attributed or contested, reflecting EPOCH's regional dominance. ATA's East Asian operations are rarer, more expensive, and historically tactical rather than transformative.

### Qin Shi Huang (China, 259–210 BCE)

- **Faction guess:** EPOCH.
- **The tell:** Unified China by force in a single generation, standardized writing, weights, measures, currency, and axle widths across the conquered territories. Built the first Great Wall. Burned competing schools of philosophy. The pattern is *EPOCH ideal* — autocratic unification, coordinated central governance, suppression of pluralism, durable consolidation. The standardization templates propagated forward across 2200 years of Chinese civilization. His paranoid obsession with personal immortality (sending Xu Fu on his expedition, ingesting mercury) suggests he knew something most rulers don't.
- **Mission angle:** **The First Emperor's Mausoleum.** Sealed since 210 BCE, the inner chamber has *never been excavated* in real history. Archaeological surveys confirm rivers of mercury inside (consistent with the *Records of the Grand Historian*). What's in there? EPOCH equipment cache? A preserved EPOCH operative? A working anchor station EPOCH established to bootstrap their East Asian network? Modern-era ATA recovery op with serious political complications (the Chinese government has refused excavation for decades). Or historical-era insertion at the moment of his death — race to be inside the chamber before sealing.

### Xu Fu's Expedition (China, ~219 BCE)

- **Faction guess:** EPOCH extraction, or ATA disruption.
- **The tell:** Qin Shi Huang sent Xu Fu east with a fleet of "three thousand virgin boys and girls" seeking the elixir of immortality. They never returned. Historical record offers no satisfying explanation for what happened to a fleet that size.
- **Mission angle:** Two readings. **(A) EPOCH extraction:** Xu Fu was an EPOCH embed sent to *exfiltrate* a major resource (people, samples, or an artifact) from Qin China at the moment Qin Shi Huang's tightening surveillance made internal operations risky. The fleet "disappeared" because EPOCH dropped it forward through time. **(B) ATA disruption:** Xu Fu was an ATA agent attempting to disrupt the First Emperor's consolidation by extracting key cultural carriers (educated youths) from the empire at scale. Either way, modern-era investigation arc: where did 3000+ people actually go? Japanese folklore claims Xu Fu landed in Honshu and became a local king — is that the truth, an ATA cover, or an EPOCH-seeded mythology?

### Wu Zetian (China, 624–705 CE)

- **Faction guess:** ATA.
- **The tell:** Only female emperor in Chinese history. Rose from concubine to consort to regent to emperor in her own name. Administrative reformer who expanded the imperial examination system (a meritocratic check on aristocratic power — a classic ATA institution-building move). Patronized Buddhism as a counterweight to Confucian institutional inertia. Tang dynasty's commercial and cultural florescence on her watch.
- **Mission angle:** Chang'an, late 7th century. ATA-EPOCH proxy struggle over Tang institutional trajectory. Wu's policies need to succeed for downstream Tang prosperity; EPOCH operatives are working palace factions to undermine her. Players are her staff, her advisors, her chamberlains. Pairs as a contrast with Catherine the Great in Russia 1000 years later — the same ATA pattern of female-ruler reform-and-stabilize in regions ATA needs to influence.

### Tang Taizong (China, 599–649 CE)

- **Faction guess:** ATA.
- **The tell:** Codified the Tang Code, which became the institutional template for legal systems across Korea (Goryeo Code), Japan (Taihō Code, then Yōrō Code), and Vietnam. One Tang emperor's legal reform propagated as the foundational legal framework of half of Asia for the next millennium. *Codification-as-preservation* is the defining ATA pattern — see Justinian, Napoleon, Sejong.
- **Mission angle:** Lower-stakes than Wu Zetian. Players might be embedded clerks or scribes in the early Tang court, working manuscript preservation operations to ensure the Tang Code survives subsequent dynastic transitions intact.

### Zhuge Liang (China, 181–234 CE)

- **Faction guess:** ATA tech-leak.
- **The tell:** Three Kingdoms-era strategist whose engineering inventions — the repeating crossbow, the "Wooden Ox" cargo system, anomalous siege weapons — are centuries ahead of their era. His military strategies are still studied in modern staff colleges. The historical record reveres him as an almost supernaturally capable advisor. Pattern matches Archimedes and Leonardo: ATA embed leaking tech faster than the era can absorb.
- **Mission angle:** Sichuan, 220s CE. Zhuge Liang's notebooks are circulating in copy form across the warring kingdoms. Players are sent to either suppress specific designs (the repeating crossbow's military application is too destabilizing) or recover lost technical writings before EPOCH operatives in the rival kingdoms can acquire them.

### Zheng He (China, 1371–1433 CE)

- **Faction guess:** ATA aborted op, or EPOCH disruption.
- **The tell:** Ming admiral whose treasure fleets reached Africa, Arabia, and Southeast Asia in seven voyages between 1405 and 1433 — a scale of maritime exploration that would not be matched by any other power for centuries. Then the program ended abruptly in 1433 and the Ming retreated into deliberate isolation. China had the capability for sustained naval expansion and *chose not to use it*. The decision shaped half a millennium of global power balance.
- **Mission angle:** **Was Zheng He ATA?** Did ATA briefly attempt to seed Chinese maritime expansion as a counterweight to coming European naval dominance, then abandon the program when EPOCH counter-ops at the Ming court turned the dynasty isolationist? Multi-arc campaign possibility: each of the seven voyages as a mission, with the final post-1433 "who killed the program" arc as the culmination.

### Shotoku Taishi (Japan, 574–622 CE)

- **Faction guess:** ATA.
- **The tell:** Prince Regent who imported Buddhism, codified the 17-Article Constitution, and established the administrative template for the Japanese imperial state. Classic ATA codification + institution-building pattern, this time in an EPOCH-favored region (which makes it bolder — ATA was operating deep in EPOCH home turf).
- **Mission angle:** Asuka period court, late 6th / early 7th century. ATA support operation. Players are embedded as Buddhist scholars or court advisors, working to ensure Shotoku's reforms institutionalize before he dies.

### The Mongol Invasions of Japan (1274 and 1281)

- **Faction guess:** Contested — the typhoons (kamikaze, "divine winds") that destroyed both invasion fleets are the central mystery.
- **The tell:** Twice, Mongol fleets approaching Japan were destroyed by typhoons at precisely the right moment for Japanese survival. The probability of *both* invasions being defeated by weather is statistically anomalous. Three readings: (A) natural — the East Asian typhoon season is genuinely treacherous; this is just lucky. (B) ATA weather op — some kind of meteor-radiance climate-influence capability ATA has never publicly acknowledged. (C) Oracle Program precognition — an embedded Japanese precog warned the Kamakura shogunate when to deploy and where to retreat.
- **Mission angle:** Players insert in 1274 or 1281, monitor the typhoon arrivals, attempt to determine which reading is correct. If ATA-engineered, the climate capability is a *major* worldbuilding reveal that resets player assumptions about ATA's toolkit. If Oracle, who was the precog and where are their descendants? Either resolution shifts the campaign's geographic strategic balance.

### Tokugawa Ieyasu (Japan, 1543–1616 CE)

- **Faction guess:** Contested.
- **The tell:** Established the Tokugawa shogunate after Sekigahara (1600), which produced 250 years of internal Japanese stability *and* deliberate isolation from European contact (sakoku, sealed-country policy, 1635). Either reading works: (A) ATA op — long-duration stability preserves Japan's institutional capacity for the Meiji modernization that follows. (B) EPOCH op — sealing Japan off from Western contact during the European Age of Discovery prevents the Western intellectual and economic exchange that might have produced an earlier Japanese democratic-republican experiment. Both readings have evidence; the truth may involve a faction switch mid-tenure.
- **Mission angle:** Multiple. Sekigahara (1600) for the initial installation. The Sakoku Edict (1635) for the isolation decision — was Ieyasu's grandson (Tokugawa Iemitsu) following the original ATA plan, or was the isolation policy EPOCH counter-influence after Ieyasu's death?

### King Sejong (Korea, 1397–1450 CE)

- **Faction guess:** ATA.
- **The tell:** Created Hangul, a phonetic writing system designed to be learnable in a day, explicitly to enable mass literacy outside the Confucian scholarly elite. The single most egalitarian institutional move in pre-modern East Asian history. *Cultural-preservation through accessibility* is a deep ATA pattern — information that propagates broadly is harder for any future agency to suppress.
- **Mission angle:** Mid-15th century Joseon court. ATA support op while the new script is being institutionalized over scholarly-class opposition. Lower-key intrigue mission, but the long-arc payoff (Hangul propagates as cultural-preservation infrastructure ATA can rely on through later centuries) is significant.

### Ashoka the Great (India, ~268–232 BCE)

- **Faction guess:** ATA.
- **The tell:** Mauryan emperor who, after a particularly brutal conquest (the Kalinga war), converted to Buddhism and inscribed his Edicts on rock pillars across the subcontinent — a multi-language, multi-script institutional-memory operation that explicitly tried to make his policies *outlast him*. Promoted religious tolerance, animal welfare, social welfare programs that were anomalously modern for the era. The codification-into-stone pattern is *quintessential* ATA preservation work.
- **Mission angle:** Patna, mid-3rd century BCE. ATA infrastructure operation. Players are embedded scribes or court attendants, working to ensure the Edicts get carved as planned despite EPOCH operatives in rival kingdoms working to disrupt the Mauryan religious-tolerance message before it propagates.

## Extraction Roster

> Candidates for the **[Reichenbach Protocol](../Factions/ATA.md#the-reichenbach-protocol)** (ATA) and **[Asset Sublimation](../Factions/EPOCH.md#asset-sublimation)** (EPOCH). All are historical figures whose canonical deaths fit the extraction pattern: clean enough for ATA's conservative protocol, or ambiguous enough for EPOCH's aggressive one.
>
> Confirmed canon: **[Sigerson](Sigerson.md)** (ATA, 1891 Reichenbach), **[Mercury](Mercury.md)** (ATA, 1937 Pacific), **Moriarty** (EPOCH, 1891 Reichenbach — see Sigerson file), **[Rasputin](Rasputin.md)** (EPOCH, 1916 Petrograd — boss-tier field asset).

### ATA Reichenbach Candidates

ATA's doctrine is conservative — these are figures whose deaths were genuinely unrecoverable, *not* mysterious or controversial in the historical record. Forensic cleanliness is the gate.

#### Antoine de Saint-Exupéry (vanished July 31, 1944)

- **Capability:** Pioneering aviator, philosopher, and author. Operationally: rare combination of high pilot skill, multi-era cultural fluency (his writing crossed French, Arabic, and Spanish literary traditions), and demonstrated capacity for clandestine work (he flew reconnaissance for the Free French).
- **Extraction window:** Lockheed P-38 vanished over the Mediterranean off Marseille. No wreckage found until 2000 (and even then partial). Body never recovered.
- **The pattern:** Direct parallel to Mercury — aviator lost over deep water, no forensic recovery, clean for the Protocol. A natural second pilot/diplomat to pair with her.
- **Mission angle:** ATA extraction op, 1944 Mediterranean. EPOCH may be watching the same window. Pairs interestingly with the agency's Free France-era operational presence.

#### Percy Fawcett (vanished 1925, Amazon)

- **Capability:** British explorer and surveyor with rare deep-jungle operational experience. His mapping skills and indigenous-language fluency are functionally irreplaceable for ATA missions in Amazon-basin operational theaters.
- **Extraction window:** Vanished with his son Jack and Jack's friend Raleigh Rimell while searching for "the Lost City of Z." No bodies ever recovered. Decades of subsequent expeditions found nothing.
- **The pattern:** Forensically perfect — three people simply vanished into one of the largest unmapped territories on Earth. ATA had operational cover (his own expedition was the cover; he was *already* lost) and zero recovery risk.
- **Mission angle:** Three-person extraction; complications around the son and the friend (do all three get pulled? Just Percy? Was Jack already infused per his father's pre-recruitment?). Interesting parallel to the Noonan thread on Mercury's file.

#### Marie Curie (died July 4, 1934, aplastic anemia from radiation exposure)

- **Capability:** Two Nobel Prizes. The only person ever to win Nobels in two different sciences. Pioneer of radiation chemistry. Operationally: unparalleled foundational physics intuition, plus direct hands-on experience with the materials that anchor ATA's own mark-leak history.
- **Extraction window:** Death from radiation poisoning was prolonged and clinically anticipated. Forensic narrative was settled and accepted. Body was buried, exhumed in 1995 for transfer to the Panthéon — making this a *failed* Protocol candidate if anyone reads it carefully. ATA's doctrine excludes her on these grounds.
- **The pattern:** *Counter-example.* Curie demonstrates the Protocol's limit: even an exceptional figure cannot be Protocol-extracted if the historical record will later inspect the body. The agency's Reality Analysis division uses Curie as a teaching case in why the criteria are strict.
- **Mission angle:** None directly, but the *case for and against* her extraction is a sharp internal-doctrine debate worth roleplaying if the players ever encounter ATA's recruitment committee.

#### Alan Turing (died June 7, 1954, cyanide poisoning ruled suicide)

- **Capability:** Foundational computer science, cryptanalysis, mathematical biology. Operationally: irreplaceable for any campaign arc touching 20th-century computing development or wartime intelligence.
- **Extraction window:** Single-witness death at his Wilmslow home. Cyanide on a half-eaten apple. The "suicide" ruling has been historically contested; some scholars argue accidental poisoning. Body was cremated. No surviving forensic record.
- **The pattern:** Marginal Protocol candidate. The death was witnessed (his housekeeper found him); the cremation eliminated forensic re-examination; the *politically charged* nature of his persecution and death makes the extraction operationally delicate. ATA's debate: extract him and risk the political-historical disruption, or leave him as one of the 20th century's central moral failures? Currently *flagged for further analysis* in agency records.
- **Mission angle:** Wilmslow, June 1954. Players insert hours before the death. The mission *cannot* prevent the documented historical event — Turing's death is culturally load-bearing in ways Earhart's is not. The question is whether Sublimation by EPOCH is already underway and ATA must counter-extract, or whether the doctrinal calculus pushes ATA to leave him to history.

#### Sappho (~570 BCE, traditional account)

- **Capability:** Foundational Greek lyric poet, hugely culturally consequential, only ~10% of her work survives.
- **Extraction window:** Traditional account has her leaping from a cliff over unrequited love — a story most modern scholars consider apocryphal. *Actual* death undocumented.
- **The pattern:** Deep-antiquity Protocol candidate — and uniquely in ATA's exclusive operational zone (pre-2300 BCE only for the deep era, but Sappho's era is technically within both agencies' reach; the exclusivity claim is weaker here). ATA's interest: preserve a major poet whose work is otherwise lost.
- **Mission angle:** Lesbos, 6th century BCE. Long-arc cultural preservation op — recover her unpublished work along with the poet herself.

### EPOCH Sublimation Candidates

EPOCH's doctrine is aggressive — these are figures whose deaths are mysterious, suspicious, or whose bodies were never found. EPOCH may have engineered some of these events directly.

#### Glenn Miller (vanished December 15, 1944)

- **Capability:** The most popular bandleader of the swing era. Operationally: high public-figure familiarity (cover identity work; can plausibly appear in many 20th-century social settings), musician's social access (entertainment-industry cover networks), demonstrated wartime intelligence-adjacent service.
- **Extraction window:** Flew from England to Paris on a UC-64 Norseman. Aircraft vanished over the English Channel. No wreckage, no bodies, no forensic record. The mystery has spawned conspiracy theories for 80 years.
- **The pattern:** Mirror image of Mercury — aviator lost over deep water in WWII, same year window. EPOCH's perfect parallel sublimation: Miller deployed to entertainment-industry cover roles across the second half of the 20th century, his public-figure familiarity an operational asset rather than a liability.
- **Mission angle:** If Miller is operational at Shambhala, his presence at any agency-relevant event in the 1950s-onward is a counter-intelligence opportunity. Modern-era: agency archives include grainy photos from 1960s-70s social events showing a man who looks unsettlingly like an aged Miller. EPOCH denial; ATA suspicion.

#### Christopher Marlowe (died May 30, 1593, tavern brawl in Deptford)

- **Capability:** Major Elizabethan playwright, scholar, suspected intelligence operative (he likely worked for Walsingham). Operationally: unique 16th-century English literary and intelligence access.
- **Extraction window:** Killed in a tavern fight at age 29 in suspicious circumstances. The witnesses were all government-connected. The inquest accepted a self-defense story most modern scholars find dubious. Body was buried in an unmarked grave at St. Nicholas, Deptford.
- **The pattern:** Classic EPOCH sublimation — convenient death, witness testimony EPOCH could have shaped (Walsingham's network was reachable), unmarked burial means no later forensic verification, *and* the historical-record anomaly seeds the long-running "Marlowe wrote Shakespeare" conspiracy theory which EPOCH may have cultivated as a stability-erosion tool.
- **Mission angle:** Deptford, May 30, 1593. ATA inserts to witness the killing and determine: mundane tavern murder, EPOCH sublimation, or something else? Marlowe-as-EPOCH-asset is a major reveal that complicates any subsequent Shakespeare-era operations.

#### Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart (died December 5, 1791, age 35)

- **Capability:** Composer of foundational importance to Western classical music. Operationally: high-level European court access, polyglot, exceptional pattern-recognition (musical-mathematical talent maps unusually well to cryptographic work).
- **Extraction window:** Died young of disputed causes. Buried in a common grave at Sankt Marx Cemetery, exact location never identified. "Poisoning" rumors (including the Salieri theory popularized by *Amadeus*) circulated for decades.
- **The pattern:** EPOCH sublimation with engineered death event — induced illness, common-grave burial guaranteeing no forensic recovery, cultural mystery cultivated as a side effect. The Mozart case is the agency's textbook example of an *aggressive* sublimation: ATA would not have run this operation; EPOCH did, and the destabilizing cultural mythology around his death is *part* of the value.
- **Mission angle:** Vienna, 1791. Players can investigate the death event, attempt to confirm or refute EPOCH involvement, and potentially counter-extract if Mozart is still in transit. Long-arc: Mozart-as-EPOCH-asset operating in subsequent centuries is a major potential late-campaign reveal.

#### Grigori Rasputin (1869–nominally 1916) — **CANON: see [Rasputin.md](Rasputin.md)**

- **Capability:** Natural Grade II generalist psi — empathic suggestion, bio-tactile healing, anomalous combat resilience, limited tactical precognition. EPOCH-enhanced post-sublimation to Grade III. Currently EPOCH's most-feared field-deployable senior asset.
- **Extraction window:** The Yusupov Palace assassination, December 16/30 1916, served as the extraction cover. Yusupov was an *unwitting* cover-provider: EPOCH manipulated the conspiracy timing, substituted a pre-prepared body during a brief unattended window in the basement, and extracted Rasputin alive. The autopsied/burned body was the substitute; the burning destroyed substitution evidence.
- **The pattern:** Aggressive sublimation in textbook form. EPOCH did not engineer the death event itself but *used* a conspiracy already in motion as operational cover. The historical mystery (the impossible-to-kill mystic, the disputed autopsy findings, the inconsistent conspirator accounts) is the EPOCH cover narrative — and it has held for over a century.
- **Mission angles:**
  - **1916 Petrograd:** ATA insert. Multiple options — disrupt the conspiracy to deny EPOCH the extraction cover, attempt counter-extraction during the chaos, or surveil to gather intelligence on EPOCH tradecraft for the historical record.
  - **Modern era:** any encounter with Rasputin in 20th-21st century operations is a boss-tier engagement. See [Rasputin.md](Rasputin.md) for engagement doctrine.
  - **Multi-mission capture arc:** a campaign-shaping centerpiece. Capturing Rasputin would be ATA's single largest intelligence haul. Worth pre-planning.

#### The Romanov Family (executed July 17, 1918, Yekaterinburg)

- **Capability:** Limited individual operational value (the children were children), but the *political* sublimation value is enormous — the Romanov line preserved in EPOCH's care is a permanent legitimacy reservoir against any post-revolutionary Russian government.
- **Extraction window:** Family was executed in a basement at the Ipatiev House. Bodies were burned and buried in two unmarked pits. The remains were not discovered until 1991 (most) and 2007 (the missing two). The Anastasia legend — that one daughter survived — persisted for nearly a century.
- **The pattern:** Marginal sublimation candidate, complicated by the eventual forensic recovery. *But* the 70-year gap before the bones were found gave EPOCH a long operational window to deploy any successfully extracted family member as a "lost Romanov" claimant, destabilizing post-Soviet politics. The Anna Anderson case (1920s-1970s) is one possible artifact of this operation.
- **Mission angle:** Either historical-era (Yekaterinburg July 1918, brief extraction window in the chaos) or modern-era (Anna Anderson identity verification — was she EPOCH's failed Anastasia substitute, a mundane impostor, or genuine?).

#### D.B. Cooper (vanished November 24, 1971, Pacific Northwest)

- **Capability:** Skilled in aviation, parachute operations, social engineering — and demonstrably willing to attempt an operationally absurd act of theatre.
- **Extraction window:** Hijacked Northwest Orient Flight 305, extorted $200,000, parachuted from the aircraft over the Pacific Northwest. Never identified, never found. The FBI closed the case in 2016 without identifying him.
- **The pattern:** Modern-era EPOCH recruitment-and-extraction op. The hijacking itself was cover for the extraction (the agency needed a reason for him to be in mid-air over wilderness with no possibility of body recovery). The cash was discarded or buried as evidence-staging.
- **Mission angle:** Modern-era investigation. ATA archives suggest Cooper has been operational at Shambhala since 1971 and may have deployed back to 20th-century North America for specific operations. Modern-era hunt for his current cover identity.

### Cross-Pattern Notes

- **EPOCH outnumbers ATA on this roster** roughly 6:5 in the candidates listed, which roughly tracks ATA's intelligence estimate of the actual ratio (EPOCH runs ~5-10x ATA's sublimation rate). For a full roster the EPOCH side would have many more names.
- **Both rosters skew modern-era** (late 19th–20th century). This is partly availability bias (documentation density increases over time) and partly real (modern transportation makes "vanished without trace" events more common — ocean crossings, remote-jungle expeditions, aviation accidents).
- **Pre-modern candidates are rarer and more contested.** Sappho (ATA) and Marlowe (EPOCH) are the only pre-19th-century entries; both rest on historical-record gaps that the modern era documents more cleanly. The deeper the era, the more *every* death is ambiguous, but the harder it is to verify operational viability.
- **Pairs and counter-ops:** several of these candidates pair naturally (Mercury / Glenn Miller; Saint-Exupéry / Glenn Miller; Sigerson / Moriarty; Rasputin's failed extraction / a successful Moriarty extraction). The Protocol/Sublimation rivalry is a recurring mission template — both agencies racing to extract or counter-extract the same historical death event.

## How to Use This Page

- When the campaign needs a new arc, pick a row from the Mission Hook Index and build it out.
- When a single arc resolves, the figures involved get *promoted* from this page to their own files under `Embedded-Agents/`, with the speculative attribution becoming canon (or being explicitly inverted, which is also fine — surprising the GM's own brainstorm is a feature).
- When players ask "is X an agent?", the answer can be drawn from this page — but only commit when an arc actually uses them. Premature commitment is the enemy of flexibility.

## Open Questions

- **Remaining coverage gaps.** East Asian coverage is now solid; India has Ashoka but the Mughal era is unsketched; the Islamic Golden Age, sub-Saharan Africa (Mali, Songhai, Great Zimbabwe), and pre-Columbian Mesoamerica/Andes are still light. Mughal candidates worth flagging: Akbar the Great (religious tolerance, administrative reform — strong ATA pattern). Islamic Golden Age: al-Khwarizmi (mathematical foundations propagating forward — possible ATA tech-leak), the Bayt al-Hikma scholars in Baghdad before 1258 (ATA preservation operation about to be devastated by EPOCH-attributed Mongol conquest — see Genghis Khan candidate).
- **Female agents.** Now Wu Zetian + Hypatia + Hatshepsut + Cleopatra + Theodora + Catherine + Helen — seven named, better. Boudica, Joan of Arc, Elizabeth I, Razia Sultan, Mama Ocllo are still obvious additions.
- **"Mundane" historical figures.** Are there figures explicitly *confirmed mundane* by agency archives, used as benchmarks? Working assumption: 90%+ of historical figures are mundane. Agents are the exception, not the rule. "Socrates was just a guy" might be a deliberately preserved benchmark for what natural genius looks like.
- **The "natural" pattern.** Most historical change happens without agency intervention. Agents target *high-leverage* moments — codification events, regime transitions, pivotal battles, founding-figure decisions — not the everyday churn of history.
