# The Disruption (and the Plato Myth)

> GM-ONLY. The narrative around what *really* happened at the end of the Bronze Age era of Atlantis surface operations, and how the Plato myth came to exist as cover.

The current canonical framing (per [../Core-Setting/Atlantis.md](../Core-Setting/Atlantis.md)): no native Atlantean civilization. The surface population around the Bronze Age crater was the agency's earliest era of operations, posing as a coastal civilization for cover. Something happened around ~1200 BCE that ended the surface-cover phase and pushed the facility fully underwater-only. That something is the **Disruption Event**.

Pick one of the hypotheses below, blend, or hold ambiguous until late campaign.

## Disruption Hypotheses

### Hypothesis A — The Bronze Age Collapse Was The Disruption

The ~1200 BCE Mediterranean collapse and the end of the agency's surface-cover era are the *same event*. Something destabilized the crater site catastrophically — possibly an EPOCH predecessor agency from an earlier future-era, possibly a third party with their own gradient, possibly the meteor itself. The shockwave devastated Bronze Age civilizations across the region, and the agency's surface settlements were among the casualties.

Aftermath: surviving staff retreated underwater. Operations continued at reduced scale for centuries. The Plato myth eventually grew out of the cultural memory of the abandoned surface-cover sites.

**Implication:** modern ATA is a *restoration* agency, not a fully continuous one — but the discontinuity is operational (capabilities lost and rebuilt), not institutional (the agency itself never died).

### Hypothesis B — Deliberate Withdrawal

There was no catastrophe. The agency leadership, observing patterns the modern Reality Log doesn't preserve, *chose* to end surface operations and withdraw fully underwater. The Bronze Age Collapse correlates because the agency's withdrawal removed a major regional economic and technical engine, triggering instability that cascaded across the eastern Mediterranean.

Aftermath: the agency continued in reduced form. The decision-making rationale is preserved in the Reality Log Archive, but the specifics are restricted to senior leadership (and possibly to [Chief's](../Embedded-Agents/Chief.md) founder cohort).

**Implication:** ATA leadership has historically made *very large* unilateral decisions affecting global history, and may do so again.

### Hypothesis C — EPOCH (Predecessor Form) Did It

A pre-EPOCH agency from an earlier future-era attacked the crater. Their fuel reserves and capabilities were limited, but enough to do major damage to the surface cover. The attack failed to seize the meteor but succeeded in ending the surface era.

Aftermath: the agency rivalry is *much older* than the modern Cold War paperwork suggests. EPOCH (in its current form) may have inherited some of the institutional memory of this earlier opposing agency, and EPOCH leadership may know things about pre-modern parachronic conflict that ATA cannot easily reconstruct.

**Implication:** there is a fourth agency in the campaign's history, now defunct or absorbed, whose archives might still exist somewhere as a late-campaign recovery target.

### Hypothesis D — The Meteor Did It

The meteor is doing something slowly — growing, communicating, gestating. At some point in the Bronze Age it *pulsed*, and the pulse damaged the surface infrastructure. It has been quiet since. It may not stay quiet.

Aftermath: the campaign has an unspoken ticking clock. Future pulses may be coming.

**Implication:** the meteor is an active worldbuilding force, not inert matter. This deepens the mystery of [The Origin](../Core-Setting/The-Origin.md).

### Hypothesis E — A Time-Travel Paradox

Some future operation (possibly one the *players* will run in late campaign) causes the Bronze Age surface phase to end. The Disruption is something the players themselves cause — and have to decide whether to accept, undo, or contain.

Aftermath: late-campaign player agency tied to one of the setting's biggest mysteries. Highest narrative payoff.

**Implication:** the bootstrap-paradox themes already present in the facility's construction history extend to its disruption.

## The Plato Myth

Regardless of which Disruption hypothesis is canon for your campaign, the Plato myth is *agency-fabricated cover*. By Plato's era (~360 BCE), 800+ years had passed since the surface-cover phase ended. Rumors persisted; the agency curated those rumors into a containment narrative.

The seeded story (transmitted through Egyptian priests at Saïs → Solon → Plato) contains three deliberate misdirections:

1. **Wrong location.** Placed beyond the Pillars of Heracles (outside the Mediterranean), pointing investigators away from the real crater.
2. **Wrong timeline.** Placed 9000 years before Solon's era — impossibly deep prehistory, before any plausible civilization could have existed at that scale. Any historian doing the math dismisses it as legend.
3. **Wrong moral framing.** A civilization destroyed by hubris and the wrath of the gods. This shapes any modern recovery of fragmentary truth as "lost-civilization romance" rather than "active operational site."

The Plato cover is one of ATA's longest-running and most successful cultural-engineering operations. Renewals over subsequent eras (medieval mappa mundi placements, Renaissance esoteric tradition, 19th-century occult revival, 20th-century pseudoscientific Atlantis literature) have kept the cover refreshed without ever revealing the underlying truth.

Modern Atlantis-hunters who pursue Plato's coordinates find nothing. The myth's misdirection is *the point*. The cover absorbs leaks better than suppression ever could have.

### Operational Note for the GM

If players in their modern-era operations brush up against Atlantis cover-story leaks — a researcher who's found something, a private collector with anomalous Bronze Age artifacts, a cult that knows too much — the standard ATA response is *not* suppression by force. It is *re-shaping the leak into the existing myth*. The collector's anomalous artifact gets quietly authenticated as a fake, then publicly displayed as such. The researcher's paper gets peer-reviewed into mainstream Atlantis-pseudoscience venues, where it becomes indistinguishable from the genre. The cult gets a piece of seeded "evidence" that confirms their existing myth-framing and discourages them from looking elsewhere.

This is a mission shape worth its own arc: **leak containment via narrative redirection.** Players don't shoot anyone. They just publish the right book at the right time.
