# The Chronograph

The agencies' temporal seismograph. The early-warning system that makes ATA and EPOCH plausible institutions.

## What It Is

An instrument tuned to the resonance of the Origin meteor. It detects **ripples** — perturbations in the temporal field that propagate outward from significant historical changes. The Chronograph cannot travel through time, alter time, or *predict* the future (see [Psionics.md](Psionics.md) for that). It does one thing: it tells the agencies *that something has changed*, and gives them enough data to dispatch a response team.

Without the Chronograph, ATA and EPOCH would have no way to know what the rival had done, or even that the timeline had been edited. With it, they have an intelligence apparatus.

## Where It Lives

- **ATA's Chronograph** is built into the floor of the crater facility, directly above the meteor. It is the original instrument, the most sensitive ever constructed, and the one with the broadest detection range. ATA central command monitors it continuously.
- **EPOCH's Chronograph** is a smaller, jury-rigged instrument built from reverse-engineered designs and stolen meteor fragments. Its range is narrower, its resolution is coarser, and its operators have learned to interpret noisy data with more aggressive heuristics. Its physical location is one of EPOCH's most closely held secrets.
- Field-portable miniature Chronographs exist but have extremely short range — useful only for confirming a ripple is still active, not for detecting new ones.

## How a Reading Works

When the Chronograph detects a ripple, command receives a **reading** with the following measurable properties:

| Property | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Epicenter** | Date and geographic location where the change originated. Tells the mission team *where and when* to insert. |
| **Magnitude** | Richter-style scale. M1.0 = a single life altered; M5.0 = a city's outcome reshaped; M10.0 = a civilization rerouted. Drives mission urgency. |
| **Bearing / Signature** | The "shape" of the ripple. ATA ops have a recognizable signature (surgical, low-magnitude, narrow-focus). EPOCH ops have a different one (higher-magnitude, often violent, wider focus). Natural historical drift has a third. An **unknown signature** is the most alarming reading — someone else is in play. |
| **Aftershocks** | Secondary ripples that arrive after the main one, as downstream consequences propagate. Sometimes the aftershocks are larger than the original event. |
| **Propagation delay** | The Chronograph does not detect ripples instantly. A change near the Origin registers within hours. A change at the far end of the gradient may take days. This creates a *response window* before the new reality hardens. Miss the window and the change is permanent. |

## Asymmetric Instruments

| | ATA Chronograph | EPOCH Chronograph |
|---|---|---|
| Range | Wide (effectively unlimited within reachable gradient) | Narrow (~3000-year window typical) |
| Resolution | High | Medium-low |
| False positives | Rare | Common |
| Interpretive culture | Cautious, preservationist | Aggressive, opportunistic |
| Predictive use | Reactive only | Reactive only |

This asymmetry means **each agency frequently spots ripples the other missed**. ATA may detect a subtle Bronze Age shift EPOCH cannot resolve. EPOCH may interpret an ambiguous reading as exploitable opportunity while ATA dismisses it as drift. Both agencies race to anomalies the other side either doesn't see yet or is also racing toward — which is the basic engine of inter-agency competition.

## Spoofing and Counter-Intelligence

**Ripples can be faked.** A sufficiently skilled operation can deliberately generate a false ripple — an intervention crafted to produce a specific Chronograph signature, designed to deceive the rival agency's analysis. Use cases:

- **Decoy.** Draw the rival's attention to a non-significant event so a real operation can run undetected elsewhere.
- **False flag.** Fabricate a ripple with the rival agency's signature, to provoke their command into reacting to "their own" action.
- **Trap.** Plant a ripple whose epicenter is a kill zone — the rival's response team walks into prepared opposition.
- **Burn.** Fake a high-magnitude EPOCH-signature ripple to force EPOCH to spend fuel on a damage-control mission that was never necessary.

Spoofing is **expensive**. Fabricating a false ripple burns real meteor-fuel charge. Decoys are not free — every decoy is intelligence in itself, because the rival just spent budget on misdirection. Detection of spoofing is a skill check on the Chronograph reading itself: trained analysts can flag anomalies in the aftershock pattern that hint at fabrication. Detection is never automatic — sometimes you act on suspicion you're wrong about.

## Mission Types

The Chronograph drives mission generation. Every mission falls into one of these archetypes:

| Type | Trigger | Goal | Reward profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Investigate** | Anomaly detected, cause unknown | Identify who or what caused the ripple | Intel-heavy, modest XP, sometimes no combat |
| **Restore** | Ripple alters something we wanted preserved | Counter-edit; undo the change | Highest payoff on success; major timeline cost on failure |
| **Exploit** | Anomaly creates a strategic opportunity | Capitalize before the response window closes | Moderate immediate payoff, may create future complications |
| **Cover** | Ripple too visible to mundane history | Damp downstream effects so public records absorb the change quietly | Low risk, low payoff, faction-reputation builder |
| **Preempt** | Predictive analysis (psi or extrapolation) flags incoming anomaly | Beat the rival to the epicenter | High variance — first-mover advantage but often poor information |

See [../Design-Notes/Mission-Design.md](../Design-Notes/Mission-Design.md) for how mission types translate to player rewards, XP, and Reality Log entries.

## In-Agency Slang

- **ATA:** the Chronograph is formally called *the Chronograph*. Field agents call it **"the Watch"** ("we've got a Watch hit at M4.2 in Lyon, 1188 CE").
- **EPOCH:** formally called *Chronograf* (slight transliteration tic). Agents call it **"the Loom"** — partly because of its woven-pattern output display, partly because of the implication that EPOCH is *weaving* the timeline rather than just observing it.

- What does a Chronograph reading physically look like? Spectrograph? 3D harmonic waveform? Strip chart?
- How long does it take for a trained analyst to interpret a complex reading?
- Can the rival's Chronograph be *jammed* — produce noise that obscures real ripples? If yes, this is a major operational asset and a tempting target.
- Are there any natural sources of false ripples (cosmic events, geomagnetic storms, the meteor itself doing something)?
