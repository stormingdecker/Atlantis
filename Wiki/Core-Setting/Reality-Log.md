# The Reality Log

The campaign's persistent memory.

## What It Is, In Fiction

Every ATA facility (and EPOCH's equivalent) maintains a **Reality Log** — the cumulative record of timeline states, ripple readings, agency interventions, and observed deltas. It is the only durable record of "what reality used to be" — because outside the meteor's memory-privilege field, no such record can exist.

The Log is:

- Composed primarily of Chronograph readings, time-stamped and annotated.
- Cross-referenced against agency mission reports — "ripple M3.4 in Alexandria 415 CE → ATA operation OPHIR-12 → outcome: Hypatia extracted, public death narrative installed."
- Annotated by Reality Analysts who flag inconsistencies, possible spoofing, predicted aftershocks, and recommended responses.
- Stored in the crater facility (ATA) and in EPOCH's central operations site, in instruments that are themselves shielded by direct meteor fuel exposure. Reality Log records are *also* memory-privileged — they cannot be retroactively altered by timeline mutation.

## What It Is, As Campaign Artifact

The Log is also a **literal campaign artifact** the GM maintains and the players can review.

Treat it as a living document, updated session by session. Players watching their own interventions accumulate in the Log — and watching the world change around the entries — is one of the most satisfying long-arc payoffs of the campaign.

## Recommended Format

A simple chronological table, with three views:

### 1. The Ripple Ledger (Chronograph readings)

| Entry # | Date Detected | Epicenter (Date / Place) | Magnitude | Signature | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0001 | 2026-03-12 | ~1450 BCE, Knossos | M3.8 | Unknown → confirmed EPOCH | Resolved (Arc 01) | Minotaur Program interference. EPOCH operative captured. |
| 0002 | 2026-03-14 | ~30 BCE, Alexandria | M5.1 | ATA-attributable | Active | Library scroll inventory shifting — possible aftershock from 0001 |
| 0003 | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |

### 2. The Operations Log (Agency missions)

| Mission # | Codename | Era | Type | Outcome | Players Involved | Reality Delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 001 | KNOSSOS-VERIFY | Bronze Age | Investigate | Resolved | Team Alpha | Confirmed EPOCH presence. Minotaur asset preserved. EPOCH operative killed. |
| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |

### 3. The Deltas (what changed in the world)

This is the one players care about most. A running list of "things that are different now than they used to be." Format: date the change took effect (from the player POV — when they noticed), what was true before, what is true now.

Examples:

- **Session 3.** *Before:* Hypatia of Alexandria is documented as killed by a mob in 415 CE. *After:* Hypatia of Alexandria is documented as having retired to a private estate in 414 CE and died of natural causes in 432 CE. Public record now reflects the new history; only memory-privileged personnel remember the old version. Agent Reyes (PC) — whose grandmother was a Hypatia scholar — finds her grandmother's published works now include three books that never existed before.

That last detail — *the personal cost or benefit to a specific PC* — is what makes the Log emotionally weighty.

## Maintaining the Log

GM responsibilities each session:

1. **Open** by reviewing relevant prior entries with the players (e.g., "remember the Hypatia delta from Session 3 — there's now a new ripple connected to it.")
2. **Update** during play as missions resolve. Don't wait for end of session — call out deltas in real time when an intervention concludes.
3. **Close** by writing the new entries: ripple ledger row, mission row, delta row. Keep it brief — the Log is a working document, not a novel.

Player responsibilities:

- Track *their own* PC's personal deltas — relationships, family, possessions, reputation that have shifted. The Log is institutional; each PC also has a personal accumulation.

## Cross-Reference: EPOCH's Log

EPOCH maintains a symmetric Log. It diverges from ATA's in three ways:

1. Different annotations — EPOCH analysts emphasize *opportunity* readings; ATA emphasizes *threat* readings.
2. EPOCH's Log has gaps where their Chronograph couldn't resolve — entries marked "M?, signature ambiguous."
3. EPOCH's Log includes operations ATA has no record of, and vice versa.

Late-campaign discovery of fragments of the rival's Log is a powerful narrative payoff.
