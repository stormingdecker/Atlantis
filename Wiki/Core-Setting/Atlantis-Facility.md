# The Atlantis Facility

The crater base. Physical architecture, construction history, and sector layout.

See [Atlantis.md](Atlantis.md) for the high-level overview of the city as institution and cover; this page is the *physical* facility plan.

## The Three Rings

Plato got the structure right — three concentric rings of construction inside the meteor crater, with the meteor itself at the center. He got the diameter wrong (the real facility is much smaller than legendary Atlantis), the population wrong (a few thousand permanent, not millions), and the location wrong (or deliberately misdirected). Everything else in the legend is close enough that ATA suspects Plato had partial-knowledge sources.

### Inner Wall — The Meteor Ring

Surrounds the meteor itself at the crater's center. The wall is constructed of Atlantean-era reinforced ceramic with **transparent hexagonal panels** of the same composite later used in the dome — purposely designed to let the meteor's radiance pass through into the city.

- **Lights the city.** The meteor's glow provides ambient illumination across all sectors. No exterior sunlight reaches the underwater facility, so the meteor *is* the sun. Day and night cycles inside Atlantis are maintained by adjustable hex panels that polarize to dim, scheduled to a 24-hour rhythm so residents don't develop circadian disorders.
- **Heats the city.** Same path — radiant heat through the inner wall warms the inner sectors directly and the outer sectors via convection through the dome air volume.
- **Anchors the Chronograph.** The Chronograph instrument ([Chronograph.md](Chronograph.md)) sits in the inner ring's command sector, with its sensor array passing through the inner wall into direct meteor contact.

The inner sectors get the most radiance and the most heat. Long-duration shifts in the inner ring accelerate infusion-related effects in agents stationed there (faster psi activation, faster slow-aging onset). Inner-ring postings are prestigious *and* a known career-risk tradeoff.

### Middle Wall — The Train Ring

The middle of the three walls, structurally and operationally the spine of the city. Carries the **Atlantean rail** — a circular tram running continuously in both directions around the city's full circumference, with stops at every sector boundary.

- **The rail is how anyone gets anywhere.** Walking across radial sectors is possible but slow; the train turns a 30-minute walk into a 90-second ride.
- The rail is **the boundary** between inner and outer sectors. Each sector slice is divided by this ring into an inner-side and outer-side workspace.
- Mechanically: low-frequency induction propulsion (modern Mark IV ATA tech retrofitted into the original Atlantean track bed in the 20th century — the original Atlantean train was steam-driven from the geothermal plant).

### Outer Wall — The Crater Rim

Built directly into the crater rim itself. Holds the city's **exits**: pressurized airlocks for outbound transit, dock chambers for underwater vehicles, surfacing tubes for occasional clandestine boat operations, and the modern facility's hidden submarine pen.

- **Exits are tightly controlled.** Surface emergence is a logistical operation, not a casual commute. Most facility residents never leave Atlantis at all.
- **Defensive bulwark.** In any plausible attack scenario the outer wall is the agency's last hard line.
- **Where boost/drop transit chambers sit** is a design tension — see *Transit Hub* below in the sector list. Some doctrine puts them in the inner ring (closest to meteor = cheapest charges); current ATA practice puts them in the outer ring (containment if a transit goes wrong).

## The Dome

The entire facility is enclosed in a **dome of hexagonal glass tiles** — Atlantean-engineered transparent composite, sealed airtight, structurally rated to the crater's hydrostatic pressure with substantial safety margin.

- The hex-tile geometry is structural (load distribution) and was later mirrored in the inner wall's transparent panels for material consistency.
- Faintly luminescent under direct meteor radiance — the dome glows softly from the inside, giving the entire facility a perpetual twilight ambiance even when the inner panels are darkened.
- The exterior, viewed from outside, is the source of every "lights beneath the sea" mariner's report in eastern Mediterranean folklore for four millennia.
- Tile replacement is a routine maintenance task — individual tiles can be swapped without depressurizing the dome. The replacement tiles are still produced from the original Bronze-era glass-foundry recipe, partially lost during the Disruption (see [../GM-Secrets/Atlantis-Fall.md](../GM-Secrets/Atlantis-Fall.md)) and never fully recovered or improved upon.

## Construction History

The facility was built on the *surface*, in pieces, by ATA agents working from a nearby pre-Bronze coastal settlement (see *Open Questions* — which exact city is undecided; candidates include early Byblos, pre-dynastic Buto, the Cycladic coastal proto-cities, or one of the now-submerged Levantine sites).

The build process is one of the campaign's most striking time-loop curiosities:

1. **Surface fabrication.** Each ring section, each dome panel, each piece of internal structure was manufactured on land using whatever materials and labor the era could supply (early bronze tools, ceramic firing, primitive glassworks scaled up dramatically by ATA technical guidance).
2. **Sea transport.** Wooden vessels — period-appropriate Bronze Age boats, often Phoenician precursors — ferried components to the impact site and dropped them into the water above the crater. Sites of these drops show up in modern marine archaeology as inexplicable concentrations of finished masonry and ceramic on the seabed, often misattributed to shipwrecks.
3. **Underwater assembly.** Here is the closed temporal loop: assembly required scuba equipment, which the early Atlanteans did not have. The required gear (rebreather rigs, dive suits, undersea welding tools, eventually full submersibles) was **sent back from the modern era** to the construction crews, who used it to assemble the facility, then sent it back forward to be built in the future they were assembling the facility *to enable.*
4. **Sealing and pumping.** Once the dome was sealed against the crater rim, the interior was pumped out. The seawater extraction was driven by the same geothermal-steam infrastructure that still powers the facility — the meteor's heat ran the pumps from the very first moment.

### The Bootstrap Paradox

This construction sequence is the campaign's clearest example of **bootstrap causality**: the facility exists because modern scuba existed; modern scuba existed because the facility existed (and ATA's modern continuity, its R&D, its institutional capacity to develop scuba — all of it descends from the facility's existence).

The paradox is *stable* under the setting's physics (the Origin meteor's memory-privilege field permits closed loops to exist as long as no external observer can hold both ends simultaneously), but it is also *visible*. Trained ATA personnel know about it. Most find it disquieting. Some find it spiritually significant. A small contingent inside the agency treats it as evidence the meteor itself is *participating* in its own emplacement.

This is one of the campaign's deepest mysteries to leave ambiguous — see [../GM-Secrets/](../GM-Secrets/).

## Sector Layout

The facility is divided into **8 radial slices**, each slice split by the middle wall into an inner-side and outer-side workspace. **16 sectors total.**

Inner sectors are radiance-heavy and house the most sensitive infrastructure. Outer sectors house daily-life functions and surface-facing logistics.

### Sector-Slice Alignment

Each radial slice pairs an inner and outer sector with related functions:

- **Slice 1** (I-1 Command + O-1 Docks) — the agency's nerve and the agency's gate, on the same radial axis.
- **Slice 2** (I-2 Refinery + O-2 Residential-N) — the families closest to the radiance source, by deliberate posting.
- **Slice 3** (I-3 Foundry + O-3 Factory) — material flow in one slice: infused metal moves from the inner Foundry to the outer Factory without crossing the train ring.
- **Slice 4** (I-4 Geothermal + O-4 Farm) — power and food production share waste-heat and steam infrastructure.
- **Slice 5** (I-5 Reality Log + O-5 Commons) — memory and community; an intentional pairing for off-shift agents to debrief informally.
- **Slice 6** (I-6 Genetics Lab + O-6 Medical Center) — bio-research and clinical care; recovered biotech samples walk down the same corridor as recovering agents.
- **Slice 7** (I-7 Vault + O-7 Museum) — recovered-artifact storage hierarchy. When an artifact is downgraded from sensitive to displayable (after analysis and political clearance), it moves from inner vault to outer museum without leaving the slice.
- **Slice 8** (I-8 Transit Hub + O-8 Residential-S) — agents arrive from extraction and bunk immediately. The two sectors share a prep-and-deployment corridor.

## The Train, In Detail

The Atlantean rail is a continuous loop on the middle wall, running both directions on parallel tracks. Stations at every sector boundary (16 total — one between each pair of adjacent sectors).

- **Frequency:** trains every 60 seconds in both directions. Full circuit time ~12 minutes.
- **Capacity:** standard car holds ~40 people; coupled into 3-car trains during shift change.
- **Boarding:** open platforms, no fare control — Atlantis is small enough that everyone knows everyone, and access to the facility *itself* is the only meaningful gate.
- **Originally Atlantean:** Bronze-era steam propulsion powered by the geothermal plant. Modernized in the 20th century to induction propulsion using ATA Mark IV tech. The original bronze-era rolling stock is preserved in O-7 Museum.

## Life In Atlantis

A facility of a few thousand people, isolated under the sea, fed by an in-house farm, lit by a glowing meteor, with the only window to outside being a hex-glass dome that shows nothing but dark water.

- **Day cycles** are artificial, polarized through the inner wall panels.
- **Children** are rare but not unheard of — multi-generational ATA families exist in the North Residential sector. Children of agents are raised with awareness of the agency from infancy and either follow into service or are quietly resettled in the modern world at adulthood (their memory of Atlantis is intact but suppressed by social conditioning, not by any technical erasure).
- **Death rituals** are private to the facility. Most permanent residents are eventually buried in the inner walls themselves — Atlantis is, slowly and continuously, becoming a tomb of its own people.
- **Visitors from the surface world** are essentially nonexistent. Bringing a non-agent into Atlantis is a major political event requiring central command approval.

## Open Questions

- **Which surface settlement was the construction staging site?** Candidates: pre-dynastic Buto (Nile Delta), early Byblos (Levant), a Cycladic proto-city, or a now-submerged Levantine site. The choice has downstream implications for early ATA culture and language.
- **Exact crater diameter and facility scale.** Big enough for a few thousand people and 16 sectors plus a circular tram is non-trivial. Need to lock a number for player-facing distances and timing.
- **Population breakdown.** How many active agents, support staff, embedded researchers, residents-by-birth, retirees, prisoners?
- **What happens when the dome cracks?** Maintenance protocols, evacuation routes, emergency drill cadence.
- **Are there sub-levels?** Storage, deeper utility, hidden command bunkers below the visible 16-sector ring layout?
- **EPOCH equivalent.** Shambhala has its own architecture — high-altitude rather than submarine, but with analogous functions. Worth sketching as a parallel doc someday.
- **Acoustics.** A sealed dome facility lit by a glowing meteor — does it *sound* like anything? Constant hum from the geothermal plant? The train rumble? A subaudible resonance from the meteor itself?
- **Where did the dedicated Training sector go?** Eliminated in the current layout. Its functions are absorbed: combat sims + physical conditioning into Medical Center (O-6, rehab pairs naturally with conditioning), era-immersion language and tradecraft into Museum & Library (O-7, surrounded by primary sources), recreation/morale into Commons (O-5). Worth revisiting if the campaign's player-development tier curve ends up undernourished by this redistribution.
