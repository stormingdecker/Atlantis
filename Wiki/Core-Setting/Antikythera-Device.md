# The Antikythera Device

Every agent's drop-transit trigger. The single most universally-carried piece of agency equipment.

## What It Is

A handheld instrument, roughly the size and weight of a pocket watch (~150g), warm to the touch. Looks like a finely-worked brass mechanism — gears visible through a glass face, calibrated dials around the perimeter, classical-Hellenistic engraved decoration. It is not brass. It is **orichalcum**, infused at saturated grade, with a small piece of the Origin meteor embedded at its core.

The device is the *agent-side trigger* for a drop transit back to Atlantis. Every active ATA field agent carries one. EPOCH agents have their own equivalent, equally personal, equally essential to their operational profile.

## How It Works

### Power

The embedded Origin fragment emits the same characteristic radiance and decay heat as the parent meteor, at micro-scale. The decay produces enough thermal energy to power the device's internal mechanisms indefinitely without external fuel — a self-contained parachronic battery. The device is therefore *always warm*, a constant ~38–40°C (slightly above body temperature). Agents can feel its presence in a pocket or against the skin without looking. A device that loses warmth is a device that has failed — and an agent who notices the warmth gone has *seconds* to react before their drop-home capability is gone with it.

### Function

The device performs three intertwined functions:

1. **Coordinate computation.** Internal gear-trains continuously compute the agent's current spacetime position relative to the Atlantean anchor. This is the live readout that lets command know where each deployed agent is at any moment (the device transmits low-amplitude parachronic signals readable by the Chronograph at home).
2. **Return-coordinate storage.** Pre-loaded with the agent's specific assigned return position (spacetime coordinates and arrival chamber within the [I-8 Transit Hub](Atlantis-Facility.md)). This is reprogrammed before every mission.
3. **Drop transit trigger.** When activated (typically by a specific dial-rotation sequence — different per agent, biometrically gated), the device initiates an immediate drop transit. The agent's mass + nearby infused matter falls along the gradient toward the meteor anchor and rematerializes at the programmed return coordinates.

### First-Issue and the Buried Chamber Tradition

New ATA agents receive their first device not from base requisition but through a ritual the agency calls **First Recovery**: a small chamber, sealed millennia ago somewhere geographically appropriate to the agent's recruitment location, contains the agent's first device, pre-engraved with their assigned agency alias and pre-programmed to their specific biology. The agent (or, more commonly, a team of incoming recruits) hikes to the chamber, opens it via a multi-input puzzle, and recovers the devices.

The First Recovery serves multiple purposes:

- **It is the first time the recruit experiences the agency's temporal capability directly** — holding a device sealed in their name for thousands of years before they were born is *not deniable* the way a verbal briefing about time travel would be.
- **It is the first time the recruit sees their agency alias.** The alias is *engraved on the device*. New agents discover their codename by reading it off the brass.
- **It demonstrates ATA's confidence in the recruit.** Someone, in the deep past, placed *this device specifically for this agent*. The agency has committed to the recruit's identity, role, and mission before the recruit themselves did.

The chambers are typically Bronze-Age-era placements (~3500–1500 BCE), in geographic locations that were undisturbed during the intervening millennia. The chamber for the campaign's prologue team is in the [Carpathian Mountains](../Campaigns/Arc-00-The-Cryo-Train.md#session-1--the-chamber); other chambers exist worldwide, placed for other recruit cohorts across ATA's modern recruitment history. The locations are agency-secret, the placements are coordinated by Reality Analysis to ensure undisturbed survival, and the recovery rituals are sufficiently impressive that virtually no recruit who completes a First Recovery ever defects.

Replacement devices, after the first, are issued from Atlantis's I-8 Transit Hub via normal Outfitting channels. Only the *first* device per agent uses the Buried Chamber Tradition.

### Programming

A device's return coordinates are set by Outfitting before each mission deployment. The settings include:

- **Return spacetime point.** Always a future moment relative to mission departure (see [Return-Time Mechanics](#return-time-mechanics) below). Usually a specific date and hour at Atlantis.
- **Arrival chamber.** One of the six transit chambers in [I-8 Transit Hub](Atlantis-Facility.md) (at T2 facility; three at T1, more at T3). Each agent in a team is assigned a different chamber to avoid spatial conflict.
- **Arrival posture orientation.** Whether the agent rematerializes standing, kneeling, or prone — relevant for tactical extractions where the agent might be injured or unconscious during return.
- **Biometric lock.** The device is keyed to the assigned agent's infused biology. Only that specific agent (or another agent with matching biometric override credentials) can trigger it. A stolen device is a brick to the thief.

The Outfitting Department's pre-mission programming session typically takes 10–15 minutes per agent. Reprogramming in the field is theoretically possible but practically very difficult — the dials and adjustment screws are precise enough that most agents wouldn't trust their own field-modifications without explicit Outfitting consultation.

## Appearance and Disguise

The device's default appearance is **Hellenistic brass instrument**. This is excellent cover for missions to any era from roughly 500 BCE forward where a complex mechanical object might plausibly be carried by a traveler:

- **Greek and Roman eras:** the device looks like a personal astrolabe or astronomical curiosity. Carried openly without suspicion.
- **Medieval Europe:** can be reframed as a reliquary, a personal devotional object, or a scholar's clock — though the gear visibility might attract attention.
- **Renaissance forward:** looks like an antique navigational instrument or scientific curiosity.
- **Modern era:** an antique pocket-watch or steampunk-curiosity, suitable for any vintage-aesthetic cover identity.

For Bronze Age missions, the brass-Hellenistic styling is *anachronistic* (Bronze Age cultures don't have the metallurgical or design vocabulary to recognize it as a plausible local object). Agents on Bronze Age deployments either:

- Hide the device entirely (concealed in clothing or in a hidden satchel pocket)
- Disguise it with overlay materials (wrapped in cloth, sealed in a gold-leafed reliquary cap, hidden inside a hollow stone)
- Use a specialized **Bronze Age variant** with different styling (kept stockpiled at Atlantis for Bronze Age deployments — looks like an oddly-warm carved stone or a wrought-bronze ceremonial object)

The brass-Hellenistic styling is itself partly a *cover artifact*. ATA designed the device's appearance specifically so that pieces of the technology, if ever lost in-era, would be interpreted by later historians as *primitive astronomical curiosities* rather than as advanced functional devices. The success of this cover strategy is documented in [The Antikythera Shipwreck](#the-antikythera-shipwreck) below.

## Return-Time Mechanics

Drop transit does not return the agent to the moment they left.

Every device's return programming includes a **minimum forward offset** of approximately 30 days, with typical offsets ranging from 1 month to 4+ months depending on mission planning. The offset exists for several reasons:

- **Gradient physics:** the meteor's parachronic field cannot route mass to a return point arbitrarily close to its departure point. There is a minimum 4D distance the system enforces. Practical floor: approximately 30 days.
- **Operational tempo:** if agents could return at the exact moment of departure, ATA could run infinite missions per real-time minute. The forward offset enforces a *real elapsed time at base* between missions, preserving the agency's operational pacing.
- **Coordination:** the offset gives Outfitting, Reality Analysis, and Medical time to process each returning team before the next mission. Without it, base infrastructure would be permanently in chaos.

### Connection to Base Upgrades

The return-time mechanism is the operational vehicle for the [base-upgrade cycle](../Design-Notes/Base-Upgrades.md). When players commit a base upgrade before deployment, the upgrade's build duration extends the mission's programmed return-time offset by the appropriate number of months. Examples:

- Standard mission, no upgrades in progress: return-time offset ~30 days
- Mission with one 2-month upgrade in progress: return-time offset ~2 months
- Mission with two parallel upgrades (one at 1 month, one at 4 months): return-time offset is set to the longer of the two — 4 months — so the upgrades are complete by the time the team gets back

This is what produces the in-fiction effect of agents arriving home to a *changed Atlantis* — sectors that were Tier 1 when they left are now Tier 2 when they return; new equipment is on the shelves; rooms have been reconfigured.

It also creates a subtle pressure: agents are away from their personal lives at Atlantis for *real elapsed time*. Relationships, friendships, ongoing projects at base all advance without them. An agent who has been deploying continuously for two years of campaign-time may have lost contact with the cohort of recruits who joined alongside them. This is part of the *cost* of being a field operative.

## The Antikythera Shipwreck

The single most consequential historical leak of the device, and the source of its name.

In approximately **60 BCE**, an ATA agent operating in the Aegean lost a device during a maritime extraction. The device fell into the sea aboard a sinking Roman merchant ship near the island of Antikythera. The agent survived; the device did not.

The wreck lay undisturbed for ~1960 years. In **1900 CE**, Greek sponge divers discovered the wreck and recovered, among other artifacts, the fragmented remains of a complex bronze geared mechanism. The find made international news. Greek archaeologists named it the **Antikythera mechanism** after the find-site.

Modern researchers spent decades reconstructing the device's function. They concluded it was an *astronomical calculator* — a Hellenistic-era analog computer for predicting eclipses, lunar phases, and planetary positions. The reconstruction is *almost correct*. The mechanism's surviving gear-trains do compute astronomical positions. They also compute the parachronic-coordinate functions that the missing internal sections (the orichalcum core, the meteor fragment, the biometric lock) needed for actual drop-transit triggering. Without those missing parts, modern archaeologists see only the astronomical portion of a device that did far more.

ATA's response to the 1900 discovery was to *not respond*. The leak was already too public to suppress. Instead, the agency let the academic interpretation run its course — "advanced ancient Greek astronomical instrument" — which became the standard reading. The device's true function remains unknown to modern mainstream archaeology, even as the public's familiarity with the name "Antikythera mechanism" gives ATA an excellent cultural cover. Modern ATA agents who lose their devices in-era can rely on the fact that any recovery will be interpreted as "another piece of the famous Greek instrument" rather than as a working time-travel artifact.

The agent who lost the original device was disciplined but not severely. The leak's manageable framing made it one of the agency's most successful long-term cover absorptions.

## Loss, Capture, and Field Considerations

### Loss

An agent who loses their device in the field is *not immediately stranded* (drop transit is downhill — any other agent's device can extract them as a passenger if the carrier permits it). But they have lost their *personal* extraction capability and become dependent on teammates or follow-up rescue.

ATA's standard doctrine: lost devices must be reported immediately upon return, with the loss circumstances documented for Reality Analysis to track. Pre-modern losses (Bronze Age, classical era) are often *recoverable* via follow-up missions — see Sigerson's [original cryo-facility-era operations](../Embedded-Agents/Sigerson.md) and other artifact recoveries.

### Capture

A captured device is a *brick to the captor* due to the biometric lock — EPOCH cannot trigger it. But the meteor fragment inside is *not* worthless to them; properly extracted, the fragment can be added to EPOCH's fuel reserves. The orichalcum-infused gear-trains are also valuable as biotech samples.

When EPOCH captures an ATA device, the standard ATA counter-protocol is:

1. **Locate via Chronograph.** The device's parachronic signature is trackable.
2. **Race to recover or destroy.** Mission-tier recovery operation. Sometimes destruction (forcing the device's meteor fragment to escape containment, rendering the device uninstructable) is preferable to recovery if the geographic location is too distant.
3. **Re-issue.** The agent whose device was lost receives a replacement. Replacement Antikytheras are stockpiled at Atlantis but each one represents weeks of foundry work to produce.

### Damage

Physical damage to a device is rare (the orichalcum shell is hardy) but not impossible. A cracked device that still computes can sometimes be used safely; a device with damage to the meteor-fragment containment is *dangerous* (raw meteor exposure to the agent's body would over-saturate them and probably kill them). Standard doctrine: a visibly damaged device is *abandoned in the field* rather than carried home, with a recovery flag for later retrieval by a specialist team.

### Field Repair

Field repair is generally not feasible. The gear-trains are precision-machined to tolerances no field tool can address. The orichalcum housing requires foundry-grade work to reshape. Agents are trained on basic cleaning and lubrication but not on internal repair.

## Mission Angles

The device generates several recurring mission types:

- **Lost-device recovery.** A historical record indicates a device was lost in a specific era. The team is dispatched to recover it before it can be discovered by mundane archaeology or claimed by EPOCH.
- **Captured-device recovery.** EPOCH has acquired a device. Race against EPOCH's containment timeline to either recover or destroy it.
- **Sabotage prevention.** Intelligence suggests EPOCH is attempting to interfere with ATA's foundry production of replacement devices. Investigate, disrupt the EPOCH operation.
- **Cover artifact management.** A historical leak (a fictional Antikythera-like discovery in a different era) needs to be managed by the agency. Players run a Plato-myth-style cover-shaping operation. (See [GM-Secrets/Atlantis-Fall.md](../GM-Secrets/Atlantis-Fall.md#the-plato-myth) for the pattern.)
- **Field deprivation.** A mission goes wrong; the team's devices are confiscated, jammed, or otherwise rendered inoperable. They must complete extraction via secondary means — find another agent in-era, reach a hidden cache, signal for an emergency anchor pickup, or survive long enough to be retrieved.

## Open Questions

- **The Bronze Age variant** styling. Some sketch of what these look like (carved stone? wrought-bronze pendant? wooden case?) would be useful for in-fiction descriptions.
- **EPOCH's equivalent device** — what does it look like, what's it called, what are its tells? Probably more compact / more advanced given EPOCH's foundry mark. Possibly more *visually* future-coded, which is itself a tell. Worth a small section in [EPOCH.md](../Factions/EPOCH.md) at some point.
- **Multi-agent return coordination.** When a team of 4–5 agents triggers drop transit simultaneously, are they returning as a *group* or as *individuals* arriving in different chambers within seconds of each other? The latter is more likely given the per-device programming model, but worth deciding.
- **Self-destruct features.** Does a captured device have a self-destruct option that the agent could trigger remotely (via the Chronograph signal channel)? Currently no, but it might be useful campaign material.
- **The original device's history before the Antikythera shipwreck.** Where was the agent operating? What was the mission? Possibly material for a sub-arc.
- **Heritage devices.** Are there devices that have been continuously in service for *decades* or *centuries*? The orichalcum core doesn't degrade meaningfully on those timescales. The mechanical components might. Worth considering whether senior agents carry old devices with sentimental/historical value.
