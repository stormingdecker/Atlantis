# The Origin

The fixed point in spacetime that anchors all time travel.

## The Event

**Date of impact: approximately 4000 BCE** — within the late Ubaid period in Mesopotamia, before pre-dynastic Egypt's unification, before any recognizable Mediterranean civilization. The world had villages, agriculture, and proto-cities. It did not yet have empires, writing, or organized history.

A multi-dimensional meteor pierced the atmosphere and **fragmented on contact**; its largest fragment impacted the eastern Mediterranean seabed (see [4D Fragmentation](#4d-fragmentation) below). The impact crater is vast, deep, and — crucially — still warm. That fragment — the Origin shard — sits at the crater's heart, glowing softly.

It emits constant heat and a faint, characteristic radiance from a form of radioactive decay that has never been replicated, never been depleted, and has never harmed living tissue. It simply *gives*.

## What It Is, Mechanically

- **The Origin** is the spacetime coordinate of the impact: a fixed point in both space *and* time, anchoring a temporal gradient that extends bidirectionally with finite reach.
- All temporal travel from the Origin is measured as distance along the gradient from this point. See [Temporal-Gradient.md](Temporal-Gradient.md).
- The meteor is one of two known fuel sources for temporal displacement — the larger of the two. The other is the [Kunlun Shard](The-Kunlun-Shard.md), located in the future, anchoring EPOCH's gradient. Whoever holds the larger shard holds the deeper past.

## 4D Fragmentation

The source meteor was not ordinary rock — it was a **four-dimensional object**, extended through time as well as space. When it struck the atmosphere it **fragmented in 4D**. A normal meteor shatters in three dimensions: the chunks fly apart *spatially* and rain down within minutes of one another. This one's fragments took new velocity vectors with a **temporal component** — so they flew apart across *time* as well as space, and came to rest at spacetime coordinates scattered across millennia.

This single mechanism explains everything downstream:

- **The Origin** — the largest fragment — settled into the eastern Mediterranean seabed at **~4000 BCE**, anchoring ATA's gradient.
- **The [Kunlun Shard](The-Kunlun-Shard.md)** flew "forward," landing in Tibet at **~2200 CE** (~6200 years later), anchoring EPOCH's gradient. Its future arrival is no mystery — it is simply a fragment whose 4D vector pointed downstream in time.
- **Other shards** could be *anywhere* — deep prehistory, the far future, the open ocean, unexcavated ground. Any one of them could anchor a third party. See [../GM-Secrets/Other-Shards.md](../GM-Secrets/Other-Shards.md).

**Why there is no record of "the meteor arriving."** There was never a single witnessed impact to record. The parent object's atmospheric-contact and fragmentation event sits at its *own* spacetime coordinate — plausibly **pre-civilization, or the distant future** — unrecorded either way. What history *does* occasionally record is a **shard striking ground or ocean seemingly from nowhere**: no fireball streaking across the sky first, no incoming trajectory, just a sudden impact — because the shard arrived along a vector with a time component rather than a clean ballistic arc. Those abrupt, trajectory-less "impacts from nowhere" are the campaign's tell that a new shard has landed somewhere in history.
