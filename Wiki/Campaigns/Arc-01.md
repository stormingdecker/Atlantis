# Arc 01 — Working Title: "The Knossos Anomaly"

The first post-prologue arc. Goal: take the freshly-read-in players and give them their first time-travel mission. Introduce the gradient, the era, the myth-engineering reality — now that they know what they're dealing with from [Arc 00](Arc-00-The-Cryo-Train.md).

## Prerequisite

PCs have completed [Arc 00 — The Cryo Train](Arc-00-The-Cryo-Train.md) and have been read into ATA. They have begun (or completed) the [infusion protocol](../Core-Setting/Orichalcum.md#infusion-protocol). They know about EPOCH, time travel, and the Origin. They have already been to Atlantis — they arrived there at the climax of [Arc 00](Arc-00-The-Cryo-Train.md#session-5--the-escape-and-the-reveal) (the escape-by-transit), and their onboarding tour was [The Origin Vault](../Missions/Origin-Vault.md).

## Pitch

The PCs are recently-infused ATA agents on their first time-travel mission. Routine retrieval op in Bronze Age Crete — something went wrong with the [Minotaur Program](../Myth-Engineering/Minotaur-Program.md) and a sanitization team is needed.

What they find: the Minotaur asset is still alive. Theseus never arrived. Something — or someone — intercepted the extraction.

**Mission page:** [Knossos Anteroom, ~1450 BCE](../Missions/Knossos-Anteroom.md) — the playable location for Acts II–III (currently a stub to build out).

## Acts

### Act I — Briefing & Insertion

- PCs meet at the ATA modern-day staging facility (cover identity: a marine research station off the Greek coast).
- Briefing officer (possibly the same recurring contact from Arc 00) walks the team through the mission specifics openly — they're read in now, so no more deliberate vagueness.
- First gradient jump. PCs experience the temporal slide. One of them has a *strange* reaction — flag for later. (GM: pick a PC for the long-arc reveal.)

### Act II — Knossos, ~1450 BCE

- Arrive in the palace district. Period-accurate cover identities pre-arranged by ATA's local embed.
- The local embed is *dead*. Not "extracted" — dead. Body recently cold.
- Investigation reveals the labyrinth has been entered by an outside party. Period-inappropriate weapon scoring on the walls.

### Act III — The Labyrinth

- Players descend. Encounter the still-living Minotaur asset, which behaves oddly — it has been *fed* recently by someone who knew how to handle it.
- Discover an EPOCH operative was here. Recently. Possibly still.
- Choice: kill the asset, capture the asset, or *talk to it* (it has limited cognition but is not fully nonverbal — GM call on what it knows).

### Act IV — Extraction & Debrief

- Return to modern day. Debrief.
- The mission was supposed to be routine — because in ATA's records it *was* done: Theseus disposed of the Minotaur asset millennia ago, a closed operation in a settled past. It is not closed anymore.
- **Second-tier truth (the arc's reveal).** EPOCH — which the team already knows exists (Arc 00) — does not merely run its own operations in parallel to ATA's. It reaches into ATA's *completed* history and unwinds it. Theseus never arrived because EPOCH intercepted him; the asset still lives because EPOCH kept it alive. The past the agency believed was finished can be reopened and overwritten by the enemy. (Consistent with [Localized Memory Privilege](../Core-Setting/Timeline-Model.md): ATA's people still *remember* the version of history the enemy just edited out — which is how they know it changed at all.) This reframes the war from "two agencies operating across time" into "a tug-of-war over the same edits, in both directions." Nothing ATA has 'already done' is safe.
- **The lingering question (seed for later arcs).** How did EPOCH know to be at *this* operation, in *this* obscure corner of ATA's home turf? Either ATA is more predictable than it believes, or the enemy is reading moves it should not be able to see. Left unanswered — it becomes a recurring dread, and a hook for a later mole/leak or precog-countermeasure arc.
- Deeper truths about the Origin/meteor's *true nature* (the [4D-fragmentation](../Core-Setting/The-Origin.md#4d-fragmentation) cosmology, what the meteor ultimately is) remain for later arcs.

## Open Design Questions

- How many sessions should this arc run? (My guess: 4–6.)
- Player count and party composition assumptions — combat-capable? Social-heavy? Mixed?
- Do PCs have *one* shared backstory hook (recruited together, e.g. shared incident) or independent recruitment paths?
- What is the "strange reaction" during the first jump, and which PC gets it? (Recommend: the PC with the most player investment in long-arc mystery.)
- Combat system / game system? This is the single biggest undecided variable and shapes every mission design downstream.
