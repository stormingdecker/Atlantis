# Arc 00 — The Cryo Train

**Working title:** *The Prologue*

The campaign-opening mission. Modern era (2020s). The players are recruits who think they have signed up for an elite search-and-rescue force. They have not. They are about to learn what they have actually joined, and the lesson begins with a train heist across Eastern Europe.

## Pitch

A high-value medical asset has been stolen from a secured facility and is being moved east. The players are dispatched to recover it. They board a chartered freight train somewhere in Eastern Europe and conduct a moving heist against well-equipped hostiles. They succeed (or partially succeed) — and then escape the only way left to them, by a means none of them yet understand.

The asset turns out to be a man in cryogenic stasis, frozen since 1891. The hostiles are from 2200 CE. The agency the players just joined operates from a base under the eastern Mediterranean and has been at war with the future for over a century.

By the end of the prologue, the players know what they have actually been recruited into. The campaign proper begins.

## When in Campaign Sequence

This is **Arc 00** — the literal first sessions. Before any infusion, before any meta-knowledge, before any contact with the meteor.

PCs are *uninfused humans* with elite training: former military, police, intelligence, specialized professionals. They have been recruited individually over the past few months by what they were told was an elite private search-and-rescue contractor. They have been through some onboarding (physical training, background checks, basic team integration) but they have not been briefed on anything strange.

[Doc](../Embedded-Agents/Doc.md) is on the team from session one as the medical specialist. **Her player is the only one who is in on more than a fraction of the truth.** She knows she has been infused; she knows the agency has unusual capabilities; she does not yet know about the time-travel-anchor structure or the meteor. (GM may calibrate Doc's pre-knowledge as needed. Recommended: she knows there is something *very strange* about ATA and has been told she will be read in further after a successful first mission.)

## What the Players Are Told (Pre-Mission Brief)

The full mission unfolds in two phases. The first phase is *equipment recovery* — the team is told they will retrieve specialized equipment from a classified cache before the actual operational mission begins. The second phase, which the team learns about from the cache itself, is the train heist.

Initial briefing (at the staging facility):

> Your first task is equipment recovery. We have a classified cache in the Carpathian Mountains, southern Slovakia / northern Romania border region. The cache contains personal equipment assigned to each of you specifically — equipment we cannot transport via conventional means and cannot reproduce on short notice. You will hike to the cache site, recover your assigned items, and return to staging. Mission instructions for the operational phase are inside the cache.
>
> Hike duration: approximately 4 hours from the trailhead. Terrain is moderate-difficult, requiring climbing in two sections. The cache entrance is sealed; you will need to identify and operate the unlock mechanism. Specific training was unnecessary — the mechanism is intended to be solvable by a team with your composition.
>
> What we are not telling you: what is in the cache, what the cache is, and why the cache exists. You will figure most of it out yourselves when you get there. Don't bring back questions. Bring back the equipment and the next set of orders.

This briefing is *deliberately* incomplete. The players should feel that something significant is being held back. The briefing officer (probably an ATA mid-rank under cover as a private-security operations director) is sympathetic but firm: trust us, do the job, you will understand soon. **Doc remains at the staging facility while the team goes to the chamber** — she is already equipped, and the briefing officer wants someone medical-grade at staging during the recovery.

## What the Players Don't Know

The cryogenic pod contains [**Sigerson**](../Embedded-Agents/Sigerson.md) — formerly known to literature as Sherlock Holmes, preserved since 1891 to await medical advances that could safely cure his addiction damage and complete his infusion. He is one of the highest-value precognitive assets ATA has ever identified, and the agency has been waiting 130+ years for the modern era to arrive so [Doc](../Embedded-Agents/Doc.md) could finally complete the protocol.

The hostiles are **EPOCH** agents. EPOCH discovered the cryo facility's location via still-unidentified intelligence channels (possibly a captured ATA agent under interrogation, possibly a Chronograph reading of the pod's faint orichalcum signature). They raided the facility, captured the pod intact, and are moving it to a Russian EPOCH-aligned facility for either (a) extraction of Sigerson as a future EPOCH precog asset or (b) destruction, if extraction proves impossible.

If EPOCH succeeds in either outcome, ATA loses its single most valuable modern-era infusion target. Modern ATA's precognitive analysis capability — already thin — would be permanently degraded.

## Setting: Eastern Europe, 2020s

The train is a chartered freight service moving through the Eastern European rail network. The exact route depends on the cryo facility's location (GM's call). Suggested:

- **Origin:** ATA's hidden cryo facility in **northern Norway** (Senja or Lofoten archipelago — fjords, cold-stable infrastructure, low population density, plausibly hidden underground in old mining works).
- **Route:** Ferry to Sweden → south through Sweden by rail → east through Finland or via Baltic ferry to Estonia → through Russia toward St. Petersburg or onward to Moscow.
- **Mission window:** EPOCH has had ~18 hours since the raid. The pod is currently somewhere in the Baltic states or western Russia, moving east on a chartered freight train. ATA has approximately 24–36 hours to intercept before the train reaches St. Petersburg, where EPOCH's local infrastructure makes recovery operationally infeasible.

Modern logistics: 2020s technology. PCs have access to:
- Modern communications, body armor, small arms
- Limited vehicle support (a recovery helicopter, a road vehicle if useful)
- A small team of ATA local-handlers providing on-the-ground intel
- **No infused gear, no orichalcum, no time-travel anything.** This is a mundane high-tech heist. The PCs are at full standard-human capability.

## Pre-Mission Recruitment

Optional Session 0 material — useful but skippable if you want to start in media res.

Each PC has their own recruitment thread:

- Approached privately by a quiet, professional ATA recruiter
- Offered well-paying contract work with a "private search-and-rescue and security contractor specializing in cross-border medical extractions"
- Background-checked thoroughly
- Brought to an introductory facility (could be a private security training site in Eastern Europe, an unmarked office building, etc.)
- Met other team members
- Did basic exercises together as a team-building period
- Was told nothing strange yet

Doc's recruitment is materially different: her ATA-recruiter approached her after her real-world career collapse (see [../Embedded-Agents/Doc.md](../Embedded-Agents/Doc.md)). She has been with the agency for ~18 months by the time the prologue starts. She has completed infusion. The other PCs do not know this; they think she is just the team's contracted medical specialist.

## Mission Structure (4–5 sessions)

### Session 1 — The Chamber

> **Playable location:** [The Carpathian Cache](../Missions/Carpathian-Cache-Cave.md) — full map tiles, encounter detail, music suite, and map-image prompts.

The first session is a *physical-and-puzzle adventure* with a single simple combat and a quietly devastating reveal at the end. The one fight — driving a bear from the cave — is the gentlest possible introduction to the combat system, waged by unaugmented humans with what they carried in. The rest is the team's first experience of the agency's actual nature, delivered via environmental discovery rather than exposition.

**The hike.** PCs are dropped at a trailhead in the southern Carpathians. They proceed on foot through highland karst terrain. The route is described in their briefing materials: a 4-hour hike including two technical climbing sections (the second of which requires the team to coordinate — anchoring, belaying, helping each other up). The hike establishes physical capability, team coordination, and lets the GM introduce the PCs' interpersonal dynamics naturally.

Weather should probably be cooperative-but-cold. Late autumn in the Carpathian highlands. The team is well-dressed, well-equipped, well-briefed. They are *not* in any meaningful danger from the terrain. The hike is establishing tone, not stakes.

**The cache entrance.** The cache is in a natural cave system high on the mountainside, reachable only via the technical climb the team just completed. There is no door, no signage, nothing that reads as a *site* at all — that is the point. What marks it is a set of **innocent-looking scratches on the rock beside the cave mouth**: a shallow, weathered pattern that looks like glacial scoring or idle graffiti to anyone who doesn't know better. The team was briefed to look for exactly this mark. Spotting it (a Per check, or simply recalling the briefing) confirms they've found the place.

It has clearly been here a very long time. So has whatever it guards.

**The bear.** The cave is occupied. A brown bear has denned in the entrance chamber for the season and does not welcome company. This is the prologue's one combat encounter, and the gentlest possible introduction to the system: unaugmented humans, mundane gear, one large animal that would rather leave than fight to the death. The team can drive it off with noise, fire, and coordinated pressure; corner it or panic, and it turns genuinely dangerous. The beat teaches initiative, positioning, and cover at survivable stakes — and, crucially, establishes what these people can do as *ordinary* humans, before anything strange happens, so the later contrast lands.

**The boulder (the puzzle).** Past the den, the passage ends at what looks like a dead end: a boulder taller than a person, wedged against the rock as though it rolled there and stuck millennia ago. It looks far too heavy to move. It isn't — not quite. The stone has been **hollowed and balanced** so that two strong people, pushing together at the right point, can roll it aside; a second set of the same briefed scratches, low on its face, marks where to push. Anyone who doesn't know the trick sees an immovable rock and turns back. Solving it is the puzzle — recognize the signal, trust it, and commit two people to a shove that looks futile. The briefing hinted only that "the way in looks like no way at all." Behind the boulder: the chamber.

**The chamber.** Behind the door is a small underground chamber. Approximately 4m square, 2.5m ceiling, dry, dust-thick on every surface. A single stone bench in the center. On the bench: **a dark wooden tray bearing six brass-looking devices** (one per PC). Each device has a name engraved on its face — a name that none of the PCs recognize at first.

The team looks at each other. The names are *not their names*.

*(Each device, on closer inspection, has the engraved name of its intended bearer's **agency alias**. The players don't have aliases yet — they have been recruited under their real names. The chamber is the moment they discover the codenames they will be known by, going forward. GM coordinates names with each player ahead of time — the engraved names should be ones each player has agreed will be their PC's operational identity.)*

On the tray, next to the devices, is a small folded sheet of cream-colored paper. The paper is old in a way that doesn't quite scan — worn at the edges, slightly yellowed, but somehow not as old as it should be given the dust.

The note reads (or close to):

> You will be wondering how these devices came to be here, marked with names you do not yet know are yours. That answer comes later. For now: trust the markings, take your assigned device, and keep it close. They will keep you warm. They will not let you down.
>
> Your mission has compressed. At the coordinates below, at the hour given, a freight train will pass carrying a person who must not reach the place it is taking them. Intercept. Recover. Return to staging.
>
> Do not lose your device.
>
> — A.

(Coordinates: somewhere in eastern Poland or Belarus, on the freight route. Time: approximately 48 hours after the chamber-opening, giving the team time to return to staging and prepare.)

**The marvel.** Let the players sit with this. The chamber is millennia old (the dust, the door's wear patterns, the script's age). The devices and the note are *for them specifically*. The names on the devices are names they did not know were theirs.

No NPC is here to explain. The players are alone in the chamber with the evidence. They will speculate, theorize, refuse to believe, argue. *Let them.* This is the moment the campaign's foundational mystery lands. It should land slowly.

**Soft tells (in addition to the chamber itself).** The devices are *warm to the touch* — noticeably so, even through the chamber's cold air. The note's paper, on close examination, has fiber composition that doesn't match anything modern (Doc, if consulted on the return trip, can confirm: "this isn't a modern paper. I can't tell what it is."). The chamber's stone walls have very faint orichalcum-radiance markings near the bench, invisible to the eye but visible to anyone using a UV lamp — if a PC thinks to bring one or check, the markings appear as flowing decorative patterns that *look like* they're tracking the orbits of stars.

**Return to staging.** The team hikes back, devices in hand, mission compressed. Doc is at staging when they return, calm, having clearly *known something* without saying. The briefing officer is also there, equally calm. "You have your devices. Good. Now — to the train."

### Session 2 — Briefing and Approach

**Refined briefing.** Now that the team has their devices and the note's instructions, the briefing officer can be more specific. The chamber's note gave coordinates and a timeframe; staging adds operational intel. The asset is a cryogenic preservation pod containing a single human occupant. The hostiles transporting it are armed and dangerous. The pod must be recovered. Time pressure: ~24 hours to intercept.

**The questions.** The team will *of course* be asking about the chamber. The briefing officer deflects firmly but not unkindly: "You will be briefed on the cache and what it means after this mission. Right now, focus on the train. Trust your devices, even if you don't yet know what they are. They will not let you down."

**Approach planning.** PCs travel from staging to the GPS coordinates given in the note. They study the train's likely composition, the route, the stops it will make. They develop an insertion plan. Multiple options:

- **Station boarding.** The train will stop at a small freight hub during fueling/crew-change. Board there. Risk: easier to detect during stationary periods.
- **Track-side intercept.** Use a vehicle to match train speed on a parallel road, jump onto an exterior service ladder. Risk: high physical danger; specialized skills.
- **Helicopter insertion.** Rope down from above onto the moving train. Risk: noisy, visible from a distance.
- **Sabotage to force stop.** Cause a track issue that requires the train to halt at a specific isolated location. Risk: alerts EPOCH that an intercept is incoming.

The GM should let the players pick. This session is preparation: planning, recon, gear-checks, last-night-before conversations among the team.

**Soft tells continue.** The briefing officer slips a phrase in 2200s-creole accent for a moment, catches herself, moves on. The recovery helicopter has subtle modifications that don't match civilian or standard military spec. One of the operations-center staff says "this is what the cache was for" with reverent satisfaction. Players notice. They don't yet understand.

### Session 3 — The Heist (Outer Cars)

**Onto the train.** PCs execute their insertion plan. Variable depending on choice. Action sequence — combat possible against a small advance-guard of EPOCH security in the outer cars.

**The hostile force.** ~12 EPOCH operatives spread across the train's length. They are well-equipped but not obviously *non-modern* — they look like professional military contractors. They have modern body armor, modern weapons, modern radios. The illusion holds for now.

**Working inward.** The train is composed of ~12 cars. The cryo pod is in a refrigerated car near the middle. PCs must move through several cars to reach it, encountering EPOCH security en route. Cars include:

- The locomotive (3 EPOCH operatives — the train crew, all hostile)
- A passenger car serving as an ad-hoc command post (3 EPOCH operatives, including a senior coordinator)
- Several cargo cars (largely empty, used as buffers and ambush sites)
- The refrigerated car containing the pod (4 EPOCH operatives standing guard)
- A trailing security car (2 EPOCH operatives, communications and surveillance)

**The first impossible moment.** During an engagement in the cargo cars, one of the EPOCH operatives does something that physics says is impossible:

- *Option A:* A combatant pulls a weapon from a holster that should not contain something that big — the gear seems to compress or fold from somewhere it was not stored
- *Option B:* A combatant takes a wound that should be incapacitating and continues fighting, healing visibly within minutes
- *Option C:* A combatant *flickers out of existence* mid-fight — drop-extracted to safety before the players can capture them

The players see it. They do not understand what they saw. They will remember it.

### Session 4 — The Pod (and the Reveal Mid-Heist)

**Reaching the pod.** The refrigerated car is where the pod sits, surrounded by power infrastructure (industrial generators, coolant systems). The pod itself is roughly coffin-shaped, made of an unfamiliar dark material, with a small viewing port at one end. Through the port, players can see — for the first time — *who they are rescuing*. A man's face, perfectly still, eyes closed, in the pod's faintly-lit interior. He is dressed in clothing that is not modern. His features look 19th-century.

The players will *react*. The GM should let that reaction land. This is the *first* moment when something fundamental about the mission becomes visibly strange.

**The pod's documentation.** A clipboard or digital tablet attached to the pod contains technical notes. Some are in modern English. Some are in older script. One legible line: "Subject patient since 4 May 1891. Last vital check: stable at -196°C." Players can read this. They will read it.

**Combat in the refrigerated car.** The 4 EPOCH operatives guarding the pod fight hard. They cannot afford to let the pod be retaken. The car is a confined space with industrial machinery as cover. One of them, dying, will say something in a language that sounds *almost* like Russian or Mandarin but has unfamiliar grammatical structures. (Subtle: this is 2200s Russian-Chinese creole. Players won't recognize it as such, but it will register as *wrong*.)

**Securing the pod.** PCs must disconnect the pod from the train's power supply and connect it to portable power (provided by the recovery team). The transfer is a tense ~15-minute procedure where the pod is on a brief independent battery. If they fumble it, Sigerson is in real danger.

### Session 5 — The Escape (and the Reveal)

There is no quiet ride home. The escape *is* the reveal.

**The countdown.** With the pod secured, the team's [Antikythera devices](../Core-Setting/Antikythera-Device.md) — the warm brass instruments from the chamber — begin to pulse and heat in earnest. A handler's voice (or the devices themselves) makes it plain: a drop-transit is arming, and everything inside its field when it fires goes with it. A **countdown** begins — a fixed number of rounds. The team must hold their ground, keep the pod inside the field, and survive to zero. This is the combat climax, with a clock.

**The two agents.** EPOCH's counter-attack narrows to **two elite operatives** who arrive to stop the recovery at any cost — and who, in these final minutes, stop hiding what they are. One takes a killing wound and keeps fighting; one moves in a way bodies should not. The "private military contractor" illusion collapses entirely. The team cannot win a clean fight in the time they have — the objective is to *survive the countdown* and keep the pod in the field, not to clear the car.

**The prisoner.** With focus, the team can **disable one of the two** before the transit fires — beat them down, pin them, drag them inside the field. When the countdown hits zero, everything within it goes: the team, the pod, and the disabled EPOCH operative. (The other agent is left behind, staring at the space where the enemy simply *stopped being there*.)

**The arrival — the reveal.** The world drops out and reassembles. The team is no longer on a train in Eastern Europe, no longer in the 2020s. They are standing in a vast, warm, softly-lit space under an *impossible* sky, in a place and time that cannot be — the **[Atlantis facility](../Core-Setting/Atlantis-Facility.md)**, in the deep past. They have just traveled through time *and* space, and they felt it. No briefing-room exposition delivers this; they are *standing in it.* This is the home-base reveal and the first visceral lesson in the campaign's time mechanics.

**Who meets them.** [Chief](../Embedded-Agents/Chief.md) is there (with [Doc](../Embedded-Agents/Doc.md), who transited separately and arrived ahead of them). The staged reveal happens *here* now — every claim confirmed by the floor under their feet:

1. **The asset.** "His name was Sherlock Holmes. The Conan Doyle stories are real, mostly. He has been in stasis since 1891. He is one of the most valuable assets we have ever recovered."
2. **The enemy.** "The people you fought are called EPOCH. They are not from Russia. They are from the year 2200." (The prisoner, secured nearby, is the proof.)
3. **The agency, and where you are.** "This is the Atlantean Transit Authority. You are standing in our home — and it is roughly four thousand years before you were born. We have been at war with the future for over a century, and that war is fought across all of human history."
4. **The choice.** "Everything you just did, you did with a body we quietly prepared for it — enough to survive one transit. What comes next, the full infusion, is yours to accept or refuse. Stay, and you learn things you cannot un-learn. Take the night."

The players take the night. They come back. They stay. (Or one of them leaves, and the campaign uses that as a thread.) Their onboarding into the facility — the guided descent that ends before the Origin shard — is the next mission: **[The Origin Vault](../Missions/Origin-Vault.md)**.

The campaign proper begins.

## After the Prologue

The PCs are now at Atlantis and read into ATA. Their onboarding tour is [The Origin Vault](../Missions/Origin-Vault.md); their training and the full [infusion protocol](../Core-Setting/Orichalcum.md#infusion-protocol) begin here (multi-month; Doc's modern protocol cuts it to ~3 months, building on the *seed infusion* she gave them unknowing during recruitment — the same preparation that let them survive the escape transit).

**Antikythera device explanation.** The team already *used* their [Antikythera devices](../Core-Setting/Antikythera-Device.md) to escape — so the explanation lands on people who have felt what these things do. They are personal drop-transit instruments, keyed to each bearer, programmed to return them to Atlantis. The devices the team pulled from behind the Carpathian boulder are *their own*, cached there millennia ago by an ATA operative who knew their aliases and knew this mission would unfold this way. Standing in the deep-past facility that placed them, the bootstrap-paradox finally sinks in. The devices accompany every subsequent mission; losing or compromising one is a campaign-significant event.

**The prisoner — the first research asset.** The disabled EPOCH operative swept into the transit is the players' first captured asset, and the seed of the base's [research / upgrade loop](../Design-Notes/Base-Upgrades.md). Interrogation and analysis over the following downtime yields the campaign's first real intelligence dividend:

- **Who the enemy is.** Confirmation and detail on EPOCH — that they are a *future* agency, how they operate, how they treat their own people.
- **Technology to reverse-engineer.** The operative's gear and augmentations surface concrete research leads — the first upgrade options the players can choose to pursue.
- **A location.** Buried in the prisoner's kit and cited coordinates: a hint at the spacetime position of the **[Kunlun Shard](../Core-Setting/The-Kunlun-Shard.md)**, EPOCH's anchor. The players don't yet grasp its significance — it is the first thread toward understanding *where, and when,* the enemy actually lives.

**Sigerson** spends the next ~6 in-game months being revived by Doc. By the time he is awake and stable, the players are fresh agents on their first post-infusion missions. He becomes a recurring NPC ally: senior precog consultant, available for mission briefings, slowly adjusting to the modern era and to time-travel reality, occasionally cryptic, occasionally devastating in his deductions. See [../Embedded-Agents/Sigerson.md](../Embedded-Agents/Sigerson.md).

**The Moriarty thread** is opened but not yet activated. Players don't learn about Moriarty's situation (real EPOCH operative, extracted to 2200s, still alive) until much later. Sigerson, once awake, will eventually want to know what happened to his old adversary. The campaign answers that in a late arc.

## Mission Outcomes

### Decisive Win

- Pod recovered intact, Sigerson stable
- All EPOCH operatives killed or captured (captures are rare — drop-transit extraction is cheap for EPOCH; most who survive engagement simply *vanish*)
- No PC casualties
- Players exit the prologue informed, intact, and ready to commit

**Reward profile:** Standard XP for a difficult action mission. Recruitment to ATA proper. Sigerson is alive to become an asset.

### Win with Cost

- Pod recovered but the cryo unit was disrupted during recovery, requiring extended emergency stabilization by Doc
- One or more PCs wounded (recovery from wounds during infusion period)
- Possibly one PC dead or unable to continue — replaced by a new recruit at the start of Arc 01

**Reward profile:** Mission succeeds; minor narrative complications carry forward.

### Failure with Survival

- Players intercept but cannot fully secure the pod — they extract themselves but the pod reaches EPOCH
- Or: pod recovered but Sigerson dies during stabilization due to mishandling

**This is a campaign-breaking outcome and the GM should pull punches to avoid it.** The campaign's mid- and late-arcs depend on Sigerson being alive. The prologue should be tuned for player success even with sub-optimal play. If the players genuinely fail despite good play, the GM has options:

- Reframe: Sigerson was extracted by ATA in a follow-up operation the players don't see (deus ex but acceptable for prologue stakes)
- Reframe: Sigerson is in EPOCH's hands now; mid-campaign arc involves rescuing him from Shambhala
- Or simply: the campaign continues with Sigerson dead; the precog capability they would have had is permanently lost; ATA's prognostic edge is reduced for the rest of the campaign

GM's call. Sigerson alive is preferred.

### Disaster

- Total mission failure, possibly multiple PC deaths

This should be near-impossible to achieve at the prologue level. The GM should not run this mission with disaster-level lethality. The prologue exists to introduce the world; killing PCs in session 2 is a bad shape for the campaign. Players should *feel* the danger; they should not actually all die.

## Key Reveal Beats — A Checklist for the GM

These are the moments the prologue should hit, in order:

1. ☐ The hidden entrance — the briefed scratch-signal and the deceptively-immovable boulder (Session 1: the way in is placed for them, hidden in plain sight)
2. ☐ The devices' name engravings (Session 1: each PC's device bears the agency alias they didn't know they had)
3. ☐ The note's deliberate cryptic tone (Session 1: "the names you do not yet know are yours")
4. ☐ The devices' warmth (Session 1–2 ongoing: the constant tactile sensation of carrying something alive-but-not-alive)
5. ☐ Briefing officer's accent slip / unusual reverence about "the asset" (Session 2)
6. ☐ Mid-heist: first impossible moment (Session 3: one EPOCH agent does something the players cannot explain)
7. ☐ Discovery of the pod through its viewing port (Session 4: a 19th-century face)
8. ☐ Discovery of the pod's documentation (Session 4: the 1891 date)
9. ☐ Combat-language moment (Session 4: an EPOCH agent speaks 2200s creole, dying)
10. ☐ Escape-by-transit: the devices fire and the whole team vanishes from the train (Session 5: time travel *experienced*, not explained)
11. ☐ Arrival at Atlantis and the staged reveal — the asset, EPOCH, ATA, the deep past, *and the chamber's true nature* (Session 5)

If a session is dragging, the GM can move some of these forward. If a session is moving fast, hold some back for later in the session arc. The point is for the players to *gradually* feel that this is not the mission they thought it was.

## Key NPCs

### The Briefing Officer

Recommended: a recurring ATA mid-rank who handles new recruits. Calm, professional, slightly evasive in the briefing. After the prologue, becomes the players' regular briefing contact for early-campaign missions. Could be one of [Chief's founder cohort](../Embedded-Agents/Chief.md#the-founder-cohort) operating under a cover role.

Possible aliases: **Marshall** (ops director vibe), **Nest** (handler vibe), **Ledger** (mid-rank ATA accountant-of-secrets vibe). GM's pick.

### The EPOCH Train Commander

The senior EPOCH operative on the train. The PCs will probably fight him in the climax. Suggested:

- A 2200s EPOCH agent, ~80 subjective years old, served EPOCH for decades. Has slow-aging effects similar to ATA agents.
- Wears modern military gear over what is plausibly 2200s undergear (which would be visible if examined post-combat).
- If captured (rare), refuses to break alias. Will give a fake name and a fake organizational affiliation. His real identity is in EPOCH's archives; ATA cannot easily extract it.
- Possible alias: **Komandir** (Russian "commander" — fits the post-2200 creole), or whatever the GM wants.

### The Recovery Helicopter Pilot

A small character role but useful. Calm, competent, asks no questions about what's in the pod. Becomes a recurring figure for future ATA extractions. Knows nothing about ATA's deeper structure — he is mundane ground personnel under cover identity. Useful as a benchmark for the players' eventual realization that *they* know much more than this pilot ever will.

## Open Design Questions

- **The exact EPOCH intelligence channel.** How did EPOCH find the cryo facility? Worth pre-thinking because the answer affects what counter-intel ATA does in Arc 01. Suggested: a captured ATA agent under EPOCH interrogation revealed the location; that agent is dead now, but the leak is in the system somewhere.
- **The cryo facility's fate.** Burned by EPOCH? Or abandoned by ATA after the raid? Recoverable in a later arc?
- **Who the briefing officer is.** Recurring or one-off? If recurring, GM should sketch them as an NPC with their own arc.
- **Whether to seed Old Doc's presence at the debrief.** If yes, the Two-Versions reveal lands later when Young Doc realizes she's met "that other doctor." If no, save Old Doc for later.
- **Whether to do the prologue as 4 sessions or 5.** The 5-session version above gives the chamber its own session, which lets the WTF moment breathe. A faster GM can compress the chamber + briefing into a single session (Session 1 ends with the chamber, Session 2 is the heist start). GM's pacing call.
- **The Moriarty thread.** When does it first get hinted at? Probably mid-Arc-01 at earliest (Sigerson is awake, asks about Moriarty, gets a cryptic answer). Probably not before the players are read into ATA.
- **"— A." who signed the note.** Atlantis? Atlas? A specific founding agent? Should be left deliberately ambiguous through the prologue but may become a recurring signature on subsequent cryptic communications the players receive. Possibly Chief, possibly a long-dead founder, possibly the bronze-era agency operative who placed the chamber. GM's call.
- **Other content in the chamber.** Is there anything else there? Possibly: a small box of additional reading or instructions that the PCs only discover later (a hidden compartment that opens on a subsequent visit). The chamber as a *recurring resource* that the team can return to between arcs, finding new items their past selves — or someone — has left for them, would be a great recurring narrative device.
