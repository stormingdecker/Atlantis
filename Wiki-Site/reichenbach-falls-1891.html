Reichenbach Falls, May 1891

Status: Draft. The origin of the doctrine — ATA's first successful Reichenbach Protocol operation, the extraction of Sigerson under cover of Sherlock Holmes's "death." See also the Missions Overview.

Premise

The most famous fake death in fiction was a real one. In May 1891 an ATA team pulled a Grade-I natural precog — the man the record calls Sherlock Holmes — out of Victorian London under cover of a fatal plunge at the Reichenbach Falls. His fragile, addiction-wracked body could not have survived a boost forward, so the "extraction" was a cryogenic pause: he went into a smuggled stasis unit at the falls and slept toward a century when ATA medicine could safely revive him (see Sigerson). Arthur Conan Doyle published the cover — Watson's The Final Problem — at the agency's request, and "Sigerson" is the alias that papered over the gap.

This is the operation ATA later named the doctrine after: fake a historical death to cover an extraction, then let a period-adjacent chronicler fictionalize it. Howland Atoll was the second success and the one that formally codified the doctrine; Reichenbach is where it was first proven in the field.

The EPOCH presence is the deep-reach twist. Holmes's rival, Professor Moriarty, was EPOCH's own natural-precog asset, and an EPOCH team was at the falls to extract him the same night (see Sigerson § Moriarty). Reaching 1891 from EPOCH's future-dense home is a costly far-traveler job, so the enemy here is thin, careful, and quick to withdraw — a counter-extraction shadowing ATA's, not a firefight.

Map & Setting

Unmistakably Alpine, and unmistakably vertical — handled as three stacked, linked play-tiles (per top-down VTT conventions) rather than isometric. The team works down the mountain from tile to tile.

  • Upper — the Cliff Path. The narrow switchback trail above the gorge: pine, wet rock, a plank footbridge, sheer drops rendered as hard-outlined map edges. Exploration + social/period-cover pillar. Late-Victorian dress; the 1891 tourist season gives cover and witnesses. Ships lit (day) and dark (dusk, for the staged duel).
  • Middle — the Waterfall Ledge. The killing floor: the spray-slick shelf at the lip of the falls where the "duel" is staged and the cryo unit is cached. Puzzle + combat pillar. Deliberately cramped — chokepoints, a single safe line across the wet stone, the cache alcove behind the cataract.
  • Lower — the Lower Gorge & Mountain Lodge. The pool and boulder-field below, and the Englischer Hof-style lodge where the team stages, holds the asset, and manages witnesses. Exploration + fallback/extraction pillar. Warm interior tile (fireplace, guest register, back stairs) contrasts the cold cliff above.

The falls themselves earn a boss-sequence art treatment on the ledge tile — not because the fight is large, but because the moment is.

Objectives

  1. Sell the death. Stage the plunge so the public record — and Watson, the embed-adjacent chronicler — reads it as Holmes and Moriarty going over together. No recoverable body, no contradiction.
  2. Secure the asset. Get Holmes into the cached cryo unit intact; his health is the whole difficulty, and boosting him forward is off the table.
  3. Read the EPOCH play. Identify that a second extraction is under way, and who — the intel that Moriarty was theirs is worth more than stopping it.
  4. Manage witnesses & the chronicle. Set Watson's account, place the walking-stick and note, leave the "empty path" that Conan Doyle's story requires.
  5. Withdraw clean. Clear the site, leave the forged evidence, and hold the Sigerson cover for the fictional "Great Hiatus."

Encounters & Beats

Exploration — the Cliff Path. Arrival in period cover during tourist season. Track Holmes's ascent, plant the staged evidence (the alpenstock, the farewell note), and read the mountain for the EPOCH shadow: a second party moving with un-Victorian discipline, the tell that someone else is running an op on the same ledge.

Puzzle — the Waterfall Ledge. The real problem is choreography, not a lock. The team must arrange a fall that survives a coroner's fiction: sightlines managed so witnesses see two men grapple and vanish, the cryo cache reached and armed behind the cataract, timing threaded against the EPOCH extraction happening feet away. Getting greedy — trying to grab Moriarty too — is the tempting wrong turn.

Combat / Climax — the Staged Duel. A short, contained clash on the wet ledge, fought for appearances as much as for effect. The EPOCH detail is a lean far-traveler counter-extraction, most likely already withdrawing with Moriarty by the time ATA reaches the lip; if the GM wants a live encounter, it evades and drops home rather than trading blows. The danger is the ledge itself — spray, footing, the drop.

Reveal (at debrief — not on this map): the operation becomes the template. What the team did here, ATA will do again at Howland and formalize as the Reichenbach Protocol. Moriarty going the other way seeds the campaign's long Sigerson-vs-Moriarty thread.

Open Design Questions

  • Framing: flashback vs. re-visit. The stub offers both — a present-day anomaly investigation at the old extraction site, or a flashback that re-runs the 1891 extraction. Recommend the flashback: it earns the doctrine name that Howland references and lets players commit the founding act rather than dust it for prints. The re-visit works as a shorter B-side (an anomaly reading at the falls that pulls the flashback up as playable memory).
  • Moriarty's nature. The mission hook imagines Moriarty as an EPOCH far-traveler reaching into 1891; established canon (Sigerson § Moriarty) makes him a period-native precog EPOCH groomed from the 1870s, extracted forward that night. Recommend keeping canon and assigning the costly far-traveler footprint to the EPOCH handler/extraction team reaching back for him — same "subtle, rare, not a brawler" texture, no contradiction.
  • How present is Watson? Complicit embed who helped stage the cover, or an honest chronicler fed a story? Affects how much the team can rely on him on-map. (Cross-ref the open Watson question in Sigerson.)
  • Do players know the ending? If run as a flashback, the death is a foregone conclusion — lean into how, not whether. If run as a tutorial for the Protocol, consider hiding the cryo-vs-boost distinction as the puzzle's real reveal.
  • Timeline attention. Grabbing Moriarty, saving the "wrong" witness, or leaving one contradicting artifact should each register on the Reality Log as downstream ripple — how hard should the temptations bite?
