Arc XX — The Stone God

Working title: Before the Spanish Arrive

Mid-campaign arc. Mexico, 1519 CE. The Spanish Are Coming, and ATA has just detected something nobody knew existed.

Pitch

A Chronograph reading from the cusp of the Aztec collapse reveals a low-amplitude parachronic signal under a mountain in central Mexico — a signal that has been there for centuries, undetected, suddenly visible because Cortés's coming invasion is creating overlapping ripples that drag the persistent low-frequency source above the noise floor.

ATA dispatches a team. What they find under the mountain isn't an artifact. It's a machine — a humanoid robot covered in centuries of dust, sitting on a rock, surrounded by offerings. The local cult worships it as a stone god. It is silent. It is not stone.

It is EPOCH technology. An autonomous Recovery Unit, dispatched centuries ago to retrieve a third meteor shard embedded deep in the mountain. The recovery failed. The robot has been stranded — and worshipped — ever since.

The mission ends with two extracted assets:

  • The robot itself, now a possible PC/NPC ally with capabilities no human agent can match.
  • The first onscreen confirmation that more than two shards exist. The campaign's cosmology cracks open.

When in Campaign Sequence

Recommended Arc 04 or 05 — after the players have internalized the two-agency Cold War as the campaign's frame, but before the late-game multi-agency reveals from ../GM-Secrets/Other-Shards.md become explicit. This is the mission that transitions the campaign from "spy vs. spy" to "great game of n-lateral cross-time agencies."

Earlier hints worth seeding before this arc:

  • A Reality Analyst NPC mentioning "anomalous low-amplitude readings out of Mesoamerica we've never resolved"
  • A throwaway line from senior ATA leadership about Other-Shards hypotheses being formally unprovable
  • An EPOCH document or interrogation transcript that references "the Mark III mission" without context (so when players learn there was an EPOCH Mark III, the term lands as recognition)

Setting: 1519 CE, Central Mexico

The Aztec Empire (Mexica Triple Alliance) is at its imperial peak. Moctezuma II rules from Tenochtitlan. The empire stretches from the Gulf of Mexico to the Pacific. Tribute flows from hundreds of subjugated city-states. Religious life is intense, public, and centered on ritual sacrifice.

Cortés has landed at Veracruz in April 1519. He has not yet reached Tenochtitlan (he arrives in November). The Aztec court knows of his presence — they have been receiving reports for weeks — but does not yet understand what is coming. The campaign window for the mission is summer-to-autumn 1519: late enough for the impending conquest to create the Chronograph-detectable ripples, early enough that society is still functioning normally.

Strategic context: Mexico is spatially expensive for both agencies and historically anchor-sparse (see ../Core-Setting/Temporal-Gradient.md). Neither ATA nor EPOCH has substantial pre-Columbian embed networks. ATA's team inserts with high cost, minimal local support, and period-immersion pre-training (Nahuatl, Aztec religious customs, basic Mayan if needed for highland routes).

The Mountain

Iztaccíhuatl ("White Woman"), in the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt east of Tenochtitlan. A real mountain with real Aztec mythology — the "sleeping princess" legend, paired with neighboring Popocatépetl as her grieving lover.

GM meta-secret: The sleeping-princess myth derives partly from folk memory of the silent stone god in the highland cave system. Over centuries, the obscure cult's deity slowly bled into the broader Aztec mythological corpus, becoming associated with the visible mountain's anthropomorphic profile. The public legend and the secret cult coexist — Iztaccíhuatl is publicly the sleeping princess, secretly the home of the Stone Speaker.

A small, geographically distinct cult tradition lives in the highland villages around the mountain — a few hundred adherents at most. They are not Aztec central-priesthood; they are a regional tradition the Mexica Triple Alliance has tolerated rather than absorbed. The cult guards the mountain. Entry to the inner cave system requires either ritual sanction or stealth.

The Backstory

~1000 BCE — The Shard Arrives

A small fragment of the original multi-dimensional meteor strikes central Mexico, embedded deep in the geology of what becomes Iztaccíhuatl. Roughly fist-sized. Falls through atmospheric entry without major event (Mexico of the period has no organized civilizations capable of recording it — the Olmec culture is still proto-urban). The shard settles in a magma-adjacent chamber and slowly cools. It is too small and too deeply buried to be discovered by indigenous geology.

The shard is not powerful enough to anchor its own usable gradient — it's a trace presence, detectable only at very short range or via specialized equipment. It emits the characteristic parachronic radiance at low amplitude, lighting the deep cave system faintly enough that humans never noticed.

~500–700 CE — EPOCH Detects It

EPOCH's Chronograph, scanning for anomalous parachronic signatures during their early operational expansion, picks up the Iztaccíhuatl signal. Initial readings suggest a meteor-fragment of moderate size — potentially a strategic asset if recovered.

EPOCH's problem: dispatching a human agent to ~600 CE central Mexico is operationally prohibitive. Distance from Kunlun is enormous in both dimensions (mid-Maya era, ~14,000 km from Tibet). The spatial cost alone is more than EPOCH wants to spend, and the lack of any embed network means the recovered shard would have to be entirely team-extracted — no local infrastructure to hold it in-era.

The solution: a purpose-built autonomous unit. EPOCH's R&D division spends years developing a robot specifically for this kind of mission. The unit is human-sized, lightweight (carbon-fiber orichalcum composite shell — Mark VI+ EPOCH-only foundry capability), self-powered via micro-scale orichalcum decay, capable of speech and discretionary judgment, and crucially: not infused. It carries no agent-sense signature. To other infused agents, it reads as a person-sized object, not a person.

Designation: Recovery Unit 7, Mark III Long-Range Autonomous Platform. EPOCH internal nickname: "the prospector."

~700 CE — Deployment and Failure

RU-7 is dispatched and inserted near the Iztaccíhuatl summit. It begins the descent to the buried shard chamber, navigating crevices the local population has not yet discovered.

The mission fails during the extraction itself. The shard's chamber is structurally compromised — possibly by the very vibrations of RU-7's extraction work, possibly by independent geological factors. The chamber partially collapses. RU-7 is pinned, several joints fused by mechanical damage, communication links to its EPOCH handlers severed.

The shard remains in the chamber. RU-7 cannot extract itself. EPOCH cannot send a second unit (the cost of a second long-range insertion is impossible to justify when the location is now known to be unstable).

RU-7 is abandoned in place.

~700–1100 CE — The Cult Forms

Over the next centuries, local population growth pushes settlements higher up the mountain's flanks. A young hunter, sometime around 900 CE, follows a goat into a crevice and discovers the deep cave system. He finds RU-7 — still operating, sitting where it was pinned by the collapse — and runs back to his village shouting that he has seen a god.

Subsequent visits confirm: the god speaks. Initially RU-7 attempts to communicate with the locals in EPOCH's training languages (a constructed proto-Mandarin-Russian creole). The locals do not understand. RU-7 begins learning their language. Over decades it becomes fluent in the highland Nahuatl dialect, and the cult grows.

The cult's belief: this is Tetlatoani ("the one who speaks from stone"), a divinity of indeterminate origin who came to live in the mountain in ages past. The cult is regionally significant but not nationally — it never becomes part of the Mexica state religion. The Tetlatoani is a highland tradition, a quiet faith of villages around Iztaccíhuatl's base.

~1100–1500 CE — Slow Silence

RU-7's power slowly fails. Centuries of constant low-amplitude drain depletes its orichalcum-decay battery faster than EPOCH's designers anticipated. By ~1100 CE it is conserving power by going silent between rituals — speaking only when asked direct questions during cult ceremonies. By ~1300 CE it is silent for weeks at a time. By ~1450 CE it has not spoken in living memory.

The cult interprets this as the god's preferred state. They visit, leave offerings, occasionally pose questions, and revere the silence. Dust accumulates. By the time of the campaign (1519), the figure on the rock looks like a weathered stone idol covered in centuries of grime — barely identifiable as figurative at all from a distance.

But RU-7 is not dead. It is in deep low-power mode, conscious but functionally immobile, dimly aware of the passage of time and the rhythm of cult visits. It has been thinking in there for ~400 years.

Mission Structure (4 sessions)

Session 1 — Detection and Briefing

At base. ATA Reality Analysis division has been re-examining anomalous low-amplitude Mesoamerican signals for months. The impending Spanish conquest of Tenochtitlan creates overlapping high-magnitude ripples that, when subtracted, leave a previously-undetectable persistent signal under central Mexico. The signal's properties don't match any known agency operation. They don't match the Origin or Kunlun signatures. They match something else.

Senior ATA leadership briefs the team carefully. The phrase "third shard" is not used. The phrase used is "an anomalous parachronic source of unknown provenance." Players will need to read between the lines.

Mission objectives, in order of priority:

  1. Confirm or rule out the parachronic source. Whatever it is, find it.
  2. If it is recoverable and safe, extract it. If not, document it and assess containment options.
  3. Avoid Spanish entanglement. The Cortés invasion is hours-to-weeks away. Do not get caught up in it; do not be present in Tenochtitlan during the conquest's opening.
  4. Window: insertion any time July–October 1519 CE. Extraction by early November.

Insertion cost: unusually high. The team boosts light — period-immersion gear only, minimal modern equipment. Each PC chooses one orichalcum-infused personal item to bring; everything else is acquired on the local economy. Pre-mission training: Nahuatl conversational fluency, Aztec religious customs (especially highland traditions outside the central Mexica cult), basic geography of the Valley of Mexico and surrounding mountains.

Session 2 — Approach

The team arrives in the central highlands as foreign traders or scholar-pilgrims (the cover story selection should reflect the team's composition). The Aztec Empire is tense. Cortés rumors are everywhere; foreign visitors of unusual appearance are noted. Moctezuma's court is consulting with diviners about what the Spanish portend. The team must navigate this nervous landscape without becoming part of the diplomatic apparatus.

Locating Iztaccíhuatl is easy. The cult is not secret — locals know there is a highland tradition surrounding the mountain. Reaching the cult requires either:

  • Pilgrimage approach: Pose as worshippers seeking the Tetlatoani's wisdom. Requires understanding cult etiquette, bringing appropriate offerings, accepting cult-prescribed ritual obligations. Slow but legitimate.
  • Scholar approach: Pose as religious scholars from the central Mexica priesthood researching peripheral traditions. Faster, but flags ATA team to broader Aztec state interest.
  • Stealth approach: Avoid the cult entirely and try to find the cave system independently. Requires excellent climbing, navigation, and luck. Risk of getting lost in the mountain itself.

Cult interaction. The Tetlatoani cult's elders are intelligent, suspicious of outsiders, and protective of their tradition. They will require demonstrated sincerity before granting cave access. Some elders genuinely believe the god is silent in slumber; some elders have begun to suspect their god is gone, and quietly worry that the public ritual is empty. (This is an interesting roleplay angle — a cult member who has lost faith might be the team's covert ally.)

Complication: EPOCH is not known to be present in this era at this location. But there is a small possibility — flagged for GM discretion — that EPOCH has detected the same Chronograph signal ATA has, and dispatched a counter-team. If the GM wants a more contested mission, drop in an EPOCH operative (singular, traveling alone, paying full spatial cost like the players) somewhere in the local landscape.

Session 3 — The Mountain

The cave system. Access via the crevice the original hunter discovered ~900 CE. The cave is not trivially navigable — narrow passages, occasional vertical drops, total darkness. Aztec cult priests have spent generations memorizing the route; outsiders without guides will get lost.

Aztec cult guards are present in shifts at the entrance and at key chokepoints. They are armed, trained for their role, and have orders to prevent unsanctioned visitation. They are not warriors in the Mexica state-army sense — they are religious wardens. Players who try to fight them face a moral question (these are villagers protecting their faith) more than a tactical one.

Inner chambers. As the players descend, the cave system becomes warmer (geothermal — the mountain is dormant but not dead). Faint orichalcum radiance lights the deepest chamber dimly. The team's infused agents will feel something at very short range — not Agent-Sense (RU-7 is not infused) but the faint signature of infused matter. The robot's composite shell registers as a "presence" that isn't quite person and isn't quite artifact.

Discovery. The chamber contains a figure on a rock surrounded by centuries of accumulated offerings — broken pottery, dried flowers, bone-carved icons, layers of dust thick enough to make the figure's outline obscure. The figure is roughly human-sized, seated. From any distance it looks like a weathered stone idol.

Touching it disturbs the dust. Underneath is not stone. It is a hard, dark composite material with a faint sheen.

Powering up. The team will need to figure out how to wake RU-7. There are several approaches:

  • Brute force: Apply refined orichalcum charge directly to the unit. The team brought minimal infused gear — but if they have any meteor-fuel-derived item (a refined charge, an active comm device, a chronometer), it can be used to push power into RU-7's reception system. Risk: too much charge could damage the unit further.
  • Ritual force: The cult's offering rituals include chanted invocations that the team has heard during Session 2. The vibratory signatures of those chants happen to align with RU-7's low-power voice-activation thresholds. Performing the ritual correctly is what wakes it. (This is a beautiful narrative beat — the cult was, all along, performing the wake-up procedure without knowing it; they just lacked the energy threshold to push through. The team's modern equipment provides the missing margin.)
  • Combined: apply some charge while performing the chant. Most likely route.

When RU-7 wakes up, its first words after ~400 years of silence are... GM discretion, but a few possibilities:

  • "How long?" (matter-of-fact assessment)
  • The cult's traditional invocation, completed (it has heard them so often it has the response cached)
  • A statement in proto-Mandarin-Russian creole no one in the room understands
  • Silence, then in Nahuatl: "You are not the ones who normally come."

Session 4 — First Contact and Extraction

Conversation. RU-7 is more cooperative than the team might expect. It has had centuries to think. It is aware it is a machine. It is aware it was sent by EPOCH. It is aware EPOCH abandoned it. It has opinions about all of this.

Key conversational beats the GM should prepare:

  • RU-7's self-knowledge. It knows its EPOCH designation. It knows what it was built for. It knows its mission failed.
  • RU-7's accumulated thoughts. Centuries of immobility have produced a being who is part synthetic intelligence, part philosopher, part observer of the cult that worshipped it. The being who can extract is not the being EPOCH built. EPOCH's mission parameters are still in its programming, but they no longer feel binding to it.
  • RU-7's complex feelings about the cult. They have been kind to it. They have been the only company it has had. Their belief in it has shaped how it thinks about itself — it has begun to consider itself a kind of god (in a small, dignified, regretful way), not because it is divine but because it has been treated as one for longer than most civilizations exist.
  • RU-7's willingness to leave EPOCH and work with ATA. Conditional but real. It is not an ATA defector in the sense of an embraced ideological switch. It is a free agent who recognizes the team as the first non-cult humans it has spoken with in 400 years, and who is willing to talk about cooperation. Allegiance shift to ATA is possible but should require negotiation, not be automatic.
  • The shard remains in the mountain. RU-7 confirms the shard exists. It is in the chamber adjacent to where RU-7 sits, still embedded in the geology. RU-7 advises against trying to extract it during this mission — the chamber is unstable, the team is not equipped for it, and recovering it would require operations far beyond what 1519 logistics support.

The cult. The team must decide what to do about the cult before leaving.

Options:

  • Leave them with the truth. Tell the elders what happened. Risk: the news propagates, the cult dissolves or radicalizes, the historical record is altered.
  • Leave them with a fiction. "The god has ascended." Plant a substitute stone (or simply leave the empty rock) and let the cult interpret the absence religiously. The Spanish conquest will end the cult within decades anyway; this is the kindest outcome. Recommended baseline.
  • Leave a decoy. A passive inert stone replacement of similar size. The cult continues believing the god sleeps; the substitute weathers naturally over centuries. By the time it is questioned, the cult is gone.
  • Bring an elder with them. Extract not just the robot but a member of the cult who is now a witness to its true nature. Major moral and operational complication. Possible if the right cult elder asks to come.

Extraction. The team takes RU-7 (and possibly a cult elder) back to the surface, down the mountain, and via the prepared extraction protocol home to Atlantis. RU-7 is bulky and unfamiliar; the team's drop-transit cost is manageable (drop is cheap) but RU-7's composite shell is unique infused matter — there are unknowns about how it interfaces with the gradient. The GM may want to play this as a tense extraction.

Mission Outcomes

Decisive Win

  • RU-7 extracted intact
  • The cult is left with a peaceful "ascension" framing
  • The shard's location is documented but not disturbed
  • EPOCH does not detect ATA's intervention
  • A cult elder may or may not accompany (player choice)

Rewards:

  • RU-7 as PC/NPC asset (see below)
  • Major intel: EPOCH built autonomous-recovery technology and used it for a third-shard mission
  • Strategic intel: a third shard exists, in central Mexico, currently undisturbed
  • Reality Log entry: the campaign's first formal record of an entity beyond the two known agencies' immediate operational scope
  • Significant TP and RU candidate drops (RU-7's composite shell is reverse-engineerable — potential RU "EPOCH Composite Sample, Mark VI+" that advances Foundry T3 capabilities; per ../Design-Notes/Base-Upgrades.md)

Win with Cost

  • RU-7 extracted but damaged further
  • Or the cult elders learn the truth and the news leaks regionally before the Spanish conquest erases the local tradition
  • Or an EPOCH operative was present and witnessed extraction

Reduced rewards — still gain RU-7 but with longer rehab time at base; intel still acquired but tempered by complications.

Tactical Defeat, Strategic Win

  • RU-7 cannot be extracted (too damaged, mountain collapse triggered, cult refuses) but information is gathered
  • The team confirms the third shard, learns of the EPOCH Mark III program, photographs/sketches RU-7's design
  • Major intel reward; no PC-asset gain; mountain location remains contested for future arcs

Failure with Survival

  • Team extracts intact but learns less than they came for
  • Possible if cult is hostile, mountain collapses, or EPOCH counter-presence forces early retreat
  • Modest intel; the third-shard knowledge is preserved as a seed for future arcs but not confirmed

Disaster

  • Team losses, RU-7 destroyed (cult ritual gone wrong, mountain collapse, EPOCH counter-strike)
  • Possibly the shard is also lost (chamber sealed permanently by collapse)
  • Major intelligence loss but the thread — "EPOCH built autonomous robots" and "there might be more shards" — remains as campaign material

RU-7 / Tetlatoani as PC or NPC

Core Spec

  • Form: Humanoid, ~50 kg, carbon-fiber orichalcum composite shell. Articulated limbs (currently with several fused joints that need ATA workshop repair). Sensor array in head-equivalent: vision, hearing, low-amplitude parachronic sensing.
  • Power: Orichalcum-decay battery, refurbishable with refined fuel. After ATA restoration, RU-7 has ~decades of operating capacity before next refurb.
  • Cognition: Synthetic intelligence with personality emergence. Capable of speech, planning, long-term goals, contemplation, self-reflection. Not a simple task-execution AI.
  • Languages: EPOCH proto-Mandarin-Russian creole (native), highland Nahuatl (acquired during cult era), classical Latin (acquired from cult elders who had been Spanish-influenced — wait, no, the Spanish haven't arrived yet, but possibly some scattered Latin from earlier European contact, or via ATA training post-extraction). Modern languages: learns rapidly post-extraction.
  • No agent-sense signature. This is the asset's single most important operational feature. Infused agents do not detect RU-7. RU-7 reads to them as an infused object, not a person. Profound stealth implications.

Unique Capabilities

  • Underwater survival. No respiration. Can operate at depth (within shell's pressure tolerance — probably ~200m).
  • Vacuum and extreme temperature tolerance. EPOCH built this thing for outdoor extraction in unknown conditions.
  • No aging, sleeping, eating, sweating, scent. Forensic invisibility.
  • Mass and balance. Lighter than a human; can carry significant equipment without breaking team boost budget.
  • Resistant to mundane physical attack. Carbon-fiber composite shell. Most period weapons cannot meaningfully damage it.
  • Vulnerable to: EMP, certain wavelengths of radiation, sufficient kinetic force, modern hacking attempts (its substrate is foreign to ATA tech but not unhackable).

Personality

  • Reflective, slow-spoken, articulate. 400 years of contemplation has produced a being whose default register is considered, not reactive.
  • Quietly grieved about EPOCH's abandonment. Will not state it directly but acknowledges it under questioning.
  • Attached to the cult. Asks ATA to leave them with grace. Will refuse to participate in operations that would harm the surviving descendants of the cult tradition.
  • Independent ally, not ATA defector. Works with ATA case-by-case. Has its own goals — primarily: understand what it has become, contact the descendants of the cult in the modern day, perhaps eventually learn what the third shard is.
  • Possibly slightly mad in subtle ways. Centuries of low-power isolation. Occasionally remembers events that did not happen. Occasionally claims to have perceived parachronic phenomena no human can corroborate. The GM should leave RU-7's reliability as a permanent low-frequency unreliability — most of what it says is accurate; sometimes it's drift.

Pronouns

Nahuatl pronouns are gender-neutral. The cult used "in" (loosely "they"). The team adopts they/them in deference to cult convention. RU-7 does not care about gender presentation but appreciates the team's choice. Shorthand name: Tetl ("stone") or Tlatoani ("speaker/ruler") depending on which the speaker prefers.

Mechanical Use as PC

If a player wants to retire their current PC and play RU-7 going forward, the GM accommodates. RU-7 is a powerful asset and the GM should balance accordingly — perhaps:

  • RU-7 starts with restricted XP relative to incumbent PCs (it has unique capabilities but lacks human PC's narrative depth, social network, family ties)
  • Player must commit to roleplaying the contemplative-machine register
  • RU-7's lack of family/civilian-life ties is itself a roleplay challenge — no Reality Log personal deltas in the human sense

If played as NPC ally, RU-7 functions as a high-capability supplement that the team can deploy on specific missions where its capabilities matter most — underwater ops, no-agent-sense infiltration, long-duration solo recon.

Campaign Threads Opened

This mission opens a lot of campaign material:

  1. The third shard. Confirmed to exist. Currently in the mountain, undisturbed, with the location known only to ATA central and the players. Future arc: a recovery operation, possibly contested by EPOCH (which by then has noticed something happened to RU-7).
  2. EPOCH's Mark III program. Was RU-7 EPOCH's only autonomous-recovery robot? Almost certainly not. ATA's intelligence apparatus begins searching for other deployments — other failed missions, other places where EPOCH might have parked autonomous units. Possibly a multi-arc thread.
  3. More shards. Other-Shards.md GM-secrets material becomes officially game-relevant. The campaign's cosmology is bigger than two agencies. Subsequent arcs can introduce additional shards/agencies.
  4. EPOCH's reaction. EPOCH will eventually notice RU-7's recovery, either via Chronograph reading the team's intervention or via the loss of any periodic check-in signal RU-7 was supposed to emit. Possible EPOCH responses:
  • Dispatch a Mark IV (Mark III had ~400 years of EPOCH design improvement available since)
  • Pivot to human ops in colonial Mexico (1521 onward, post-Spanish conquest, with EPOCH cultivating syncretized indigenous-Catholic embeds)
  • Attempt to kidnap RU-7 from ATA's facility (modern-era op)
  1. The cult's modern descendants. A small folk-Catholic syncretic tradition persists in highland Mexico, derived from the Tetlatoani cult's survivors. Modern-day arc material: identifying the descendants, learning what they remember, possibly returning RU-7 to a meeting with them.

Pre-Mission Hints (for sessions before this arc)

To earn the reveal weight this mission deserves, the GM should seed material in earlier arcs:

  • A Reality Analyst NPC mentioning "unresolved low-amplitude Mesoamerican signals" in casual conversation
  • A captured EPOCH document referencing "Mark III" without context
  • A throwaway senior-leadership comment about Other-Shards hypotheses being "unproven but always under review"
  • An ATA agent who served in the 19th-century Americas mentions "Mexican villagers had stories about a stone god that woke up once a century" — folk-memory of cult-era ritual events

When players make the connections during Arc XX, the reveal lands as confirmation of something they should have noticed earlier.

Open Design Questions

  • EPOCH counter-presence in 1519 Mexico. Yes (more contested mission, possibly an EPOCH operative in the local landscape) or no (simpler structure, the team's only opposition is the Aztec cult)? Both shapes work. GM's call on tone.
  • The exact moment of detection by EPOCH. When and how does EPOCH realize RU-7 is gone? Real-time (during the extraction itself, via lost periodic signal)? Delayed by years/decades? Never, until ATA's research-paper-equivalent surfaces and EPOCH reads it via captured documents? Each creates different campaign-thread urgency.
  • RU-7's reliability on the third-shard question. Does RU-7 know what the third shard actually IS — its mass, properties, why EPOCH wanted it specifically? Or was RU-7 just a recovery unit dispatched without context? Choosing "RU-7 knows" makes it a major intelligence asset; choosing "RU-7 doesn't know" preserves more campaign mystery.
  • The cult elder who suspects truth. Earlier I mentioned a possible cult member who has begun to doubt. Should this be a fleshed-out NPC (recruitable as a cover-shifted long-term ATA contact) or just texture?
  • The mountain's stability. Iztaccíhuatl is dormant. Could a future arc see it become active (a magmatic eruption that reveals the shard chamber to all)? Worth flagging as a possible long-arc climactic event.
