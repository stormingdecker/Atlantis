The Yusupov Cellar, December 1916

Status: Draft. A Stage-3 urban infiltration set on the historical night of Rasputin's murder. See the Missions Overview, Rasputin, and the Temporal Gradient.

Premise

Petrograd, the night of 16–17 December 1916. EPOCH is executing the Rasputin sublimation tonight — converting the mad monk into an EPOCH operator inside the Yusupov Palace, under cover of the most famous political assassination of the age. ATA's window is the murder itself: history remembers Rasputin dying here, so the team has a fixed, unforgiving clock.

The tell is already in the record. Rasputin's death reads as impossible — poisoned with cyanide that didn't take, shot at point-blank range, beaten, still fighting, finally bound and drowned in the Moika. To ATA's analysts that isn't a hardy peasant; that's a target already partially infused, mid-sublimation, when the conspirators struck. The historical "murder" may be the aristocrats' plot and EPOCH's conversion colliding in the same cellar.

Petrograd 1916 is far from ATA's Bronze-Age Mediterranean strength and far from EPOCH's future base at Kunlun — a contested reach where both sides are transit-stretched. Neither can flood the site. This is a subtle op — social cover, timing, and nerve — not a slugfest.

Map & Setting

Three stacked tiles (per top-down VTT conventions); the team can move between them but the murder happens below.

  • Cellar — the Dining Room. The intimate basement salon Yusupov dressed for the evening: low vaulted ceiling, a laid table with the poisoned madeira and cakes, a fire, a gramophone, heavy drapes. Puzzle + combat pillar. Claustrophobic, warm lamplight, 5-ft grid. Ships lit (the staged party) and dark (after the shots, lamps knocked out). This is the clock's zero-hour room.
  • Ground Floor — the Courtyard & Halls. The palace above: reception rooms, servants' passages, the inner courtyard where the second shooting and the body-loading occur. Exploration + social/period-cover pillar. Aristocratic Petrograd — parquet, chandeliers, snow at the windows.
  • The Moika Embankment. The frozen river frontage and the bridge where history dumps the body. Extraction pillar. Open, exposed, snow and gaslight; the team's clean-drop-home point and EPOCH's likely exfil too.

Objectives

  1. Confirm the sublimation. Read Rasputin for infusion — is he a victim, a half-made EPOCH operator, or already turned? This decision gates everything.
  2. Choose the posture: interdict (stop the conversion) or witness & withdraw (document it and let history run). The briefing deliberately leaves this to the team.
  3. Hold the historical shape. Whatever you do, the record must still read as "Rasputin dies here tonight." Deviations spike Timeline Attention.
  4. Identify EPOCH's operator on site — the handler running the conversion under cover of the party.
  5. Extract clean via the Moika before the Petrograd police, the conspirators, or the dawn close the window.

Witness-and-withdraw is the low-ripple, high-intelligence play. Interdiction is louder and riskier — save a man history says must die and you may hand EPOCH a paradox lever.

Encounters & Beats

Exploration — the Party (social cover).

  • Insertion in period cover among staff or minor guests. The palace is full of watchers: Yusupov's conspirators, real servants, and at least one EPOCH plant.
  • Reading the room — who is mundane, who is agent, where Rasputin actually is — is the opening game. Agent-sense cuts both ways; the EPOCH handler is stretched thin but not blind.

Puzzle — the Impossible Death.

  • The cellar staged as a locked-room mystery in reverse: the poison should work and doesn't, because the target's infusion is metabolizing it.
  • Diagnose the sublimation from the evidence — untouched cyanide, wrong-century residue on the wine, the handler's kit — and decide how to intervene without breaking the death the record requires.
  • Timing windows are hard: the poisoning, the first shot, the courtyard shot, and the river each have historical timestamps to hit or fake.

Combat — the Cellar Turn.

  • If the team interdicts, or the sublimation completes, the fight is close, dark, and terrifying: a partially- or fully-converted Rasputin who will not go down on schedule, in a cramped room with drawn-in walls.
  • The EPOCH handler avoids the brawl — subtle, evasive, angling for a clean Moika drop rather than a duel. The challenge is winning quietly and still leaving a corpse in the river.

Open Design Questions

  • Rasputin's state on arrival. Victim / half-made / fully turned changes the mission from rescue to interrogation to boss-lite fight. Recommend: half-made and self-aware, so the team's choice carries the beat.
  • Interdict vs. witness — which does the campaign reward? Both should be viable. Recommend: witness-and-document is canon-safe and intel-rich; interdiction opens a later-arc paradox thread.
  • The conspirators. Are Yusupov, Purishkevich, and the Grand Duke pure mundanes, unwitting cover, or has EPOCH nudged them into position? Recommend: mundane and unwitting — their plot is EPOCH's cover, which is the elegant part.
  • What sublimation leaves behind. Does a converted Rasputin drop-home with the handler, or does EPOCH intend him to stay in 1916 as an embed? The answer sets whether this is a one-night op or the seed of a recurring foe.
  • Extraction friction. How hard is the Moika drop when both factions want the same exfil point in a transit-stretched era?
