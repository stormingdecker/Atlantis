The Core Mechanic (from GURPS Lite)

Everything in GURPS reduces to:

  • 3d6 roll-under. Roll three six-sided dice. If the total is ≤ your effective skill, you succeed. Lower roll = better margin. 3 or 4 is always a critical success, 17 or 18 is always a critical failure.
  • Effective skill = base skill ± modifiers. Difficulty, environment, equipment, etc. all stack as +/- modifiers to your target.
  • Three roll types:
  • Success rolls — for attempting actions.
  • Reaction rolls — for NPC dispositions toward the PCs (2d6 + modifiers, read on a table).
  • Damage rolls — "dice + adds" notation (e.g. 2d+3 = roll 2d6 and add 3).
  • Contests — opposed rolls. Margin of success matters: winner is the one with the higher margin (or the only one who succeeded).

Verdict for our distillation: keep 3d6 roll-under as the spine. It is elegant, fast, intuitive, and trivially modifiable. Most of what we'll cut from GURPS is the depth of advantage/skill granularity, not the core resolution.

Direct Analogs in GURPS Atlantis (3rd ed)

GURPS Atlantis offers three campaign frames; ours is essentially a fourth, time-travel-flavored variant that combines the conspiracy frame with cross-time mechanics from Infinite Worlds.

Chapter 5 — "The Heirs of Minos" (a conspiracy campaign)

This is the closest structural match. Key elements we can lift or learn from:

  • A modern-day Conspiracy Council of regional "Lords" descended from an ancient civilization (Minoan in the book, our Atlanteans in our setting).
  • The Minotaur is treated as a historical-mythological asset of the conspiracy — exactly the framing we already chose for the Minotaur Program.
  • Factions within the conspiracy: The Palatines, The Thalassocrats, The Bull-Dancers — three internal political wings with competing agendas. Useful template for ATA internal politics.
  • "Crystalline Matrices" as the operational tech basis — close in flavor to our meteor.
  • Psionics as the conspiracy's hidden capability layer.

What to steal: the structure of a multi-faction conspiracy with regional lieutenants, an inner council, and a hidden tech edge. What to skip: the specific Minoan-cultural detailing, the Crystalline Matrices system (we have the meteor), and most of the psionics (unless we decide myth-assets or temporal agents have psi flavor).

Chapter 4 — "The Orichalcum Age" (high-fantasy Atlantis)

A direct-play campaign set IN Atlantis at its height — Atlantean principalities, armed forces, magic, Atlantean wildlife, and the "Destined Disaster" finale with volcanoes.

What to steal: detail on what high-tech-Atlantean daily life might look like for PCs visiting via time travel. If players can jump to Atlantis's golden age, this chapter is the field guide. What to skip: the fantasy magic system (unless we want Atlanteans to have magic-flavored tech).

Chapter 3 — "Beneath the Waves" (underwater operations)

Underwater combat, vehicles (submarines TL6 and TL7), diving equipment, pressure rules. If our crater facility ops or Bronze Age sea missions need underwater action, this is where we look.

Direct Analogs in GURPS Infinite Worlds (4th ed)

This is the book we should lean on hardest. It is the rebuilt-for-4e successor to GURPS Time Travel and IS the cross-time agency campaign book.

The Setup (almost too on-the-nose)

  • Infinity Unlimited / Infinity Patrol — corporate-and-UN-chartered cross-time agency from "Homeline" (their Earth-prime). Direct analog to our ATA.
  • Centrum — a rival cross-time civilization with the same tech and an authoritarian "we'll civilize them all" agenda. Direct analog to our EPOCH.
  • Reich-5 — a Nazi-victory parallel that both agencies try to contain. Optional analog to a "bad outcome" timeline our agencies prevent or fight over.

The book has chapters on Infinity Patrol mission types (Reconnaissance, Intervention, Justice), the rival Centrum's classification system, parallel-world taxonomy, and detailed treatment of how a cross-time agency operates as an organization.

Critical structural difference: Infinite Worlds is parallel-worlds; we are single-timeline temporal. Our agencies are not fighting over multiple Earths — they are fighting over one Earth's history. This makes paradox and mutability rules MORE central for us than in Infinite Worlds (where you can just go to a different parallel and avoid the problem).

What to steal

  • Agency mission classification: Infinite Worlds breaks Patrol missions into Reconnaissance, Intervention, and Justice services. Clean three-tier model for our ATA mission types.
  • The Secret as a doctrinal pillar: keeping parachronic knowledge out of public consciousness is a first-order mission constraint. Adopt for ATA wholesale.
  • Resource asymmetry as gameplay engine: Infinity has bigger budgets; Centrum operates leaner and dirtier. Maps directly to ATA vs. EPOCH.
  • Operational vocabulary: GURPS slang (Outstairs, Mule, etc.) gives the world texture. We should invent our own equivalent in-universe slang.

What to skip or invert

  • The multiverse framing itself. We are single-timeline. This is our biggest design departure.
  • Quantum-level mechanics — we replace with the gradient mechanic (distance-from-Origin determines cost).
  • "Centrum could shift echoes between quanta" — N/A; our equivalent is "EPOCH can shift causal weight backward through history."

What We Need to Invent (No Direct GURPS Analog)

  1. The Gradient mechanic itself. GURPS Infinite Worlds treats inter-timeline travel as binary (you can reach a Q-level or you can't). Our setting needs cost-scales-with-distance as a core mechanical lever — likely a fatigue/charge/risk roll modified by temporal distance.
  2. Anchoring / embedding rules. Long-term embed agents (Hatshepsut, Cleopatra, Helen) need a mechanical representation. No clean GURPS analog.
  3. Paradox / mutability rules. This is the single biggest mechanical decision still open. See ../Core-Setting/Temporal-Gradient.md open questions.
  4. Myth-asset / genetic engineering construction rules. GURPS Powers can give us a starting point for stat blocks on creatures, but the in-fiction engineering is our own.

Recommended Reading Order (for me, next sessions)

  1. GURPS Lite full read (in progress — got the basics) — finish for skill list, advantage list, combat distillation.
  2. Infinite Worlds Ch. 2 "Parachronic Laboratories" — the actual jump mechanics. Steal what we like.
  3. Infinite Worlds Ch. 1 "Infinity Unlimited" — agency organizational detail. Mine for ATA structure.
  4. Atlantis Ch. 5 "Heirs of Minos" — full read for conspiracy structure template.
  5. Atlantis Ch. 4 "Orichalcum Age" — for fantasy-Atlantis era detail when we expand the in-Atlantis lore.
  6. Powers — only when we get to myth-asset stat construction.

Open design decisions this surfaces

  • [ ] Single timeline vs. branching. Strongest argument for single-timeline: it makes every mission matter. Strongest argument for branching: it solves paradox cleanly.
  • [ ] Quantum-level analog. Do we want a discrete tier system ("Era classifications") or fully continuous gradient distance?
  • [ ] Adopt the three-service Patrol model (Recon / Intervention / Justice) for ATA, or invent our own?
  • [ ] Power level. Are PCs "ordinary humans with training" (Infinity Patrol default), or do they get psionic / engineered edges (Heirs of Minos default)?
