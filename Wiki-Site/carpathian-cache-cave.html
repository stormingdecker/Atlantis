The Carpathian Cache — Present Day

Arc 00, Session 1. The campaign's opening beat: a recruitment "equipment recovery" that is quietly the players' first brush with the impossible. A hike, a bear, a boulder that shouldn't move, and a chamber that has been waiting for these specific people for four thousand years. No time travel yet, no infused gear — six ordinary humans, a mountain, and the first thread of the mystery.

Status: Draft (full-build reference mission). Built to Howland Atoll depth deliberately, as a template exercise — the reusable lessons are collected in Design Notes. Arc context: Arc 00 — The Cryo Train.

Mission Brief

Cover objective (what the team is told). Hike to a classified cache in the southern Carpathians, recover the personal equipment assigned to each of you, and return to staging with the operational orders stored inside. ~4-hour approach, moderate-difficult terrain, two technical climbing sections. Specific training deemed unnecessary — "the way in is solvable by a team of your composition."

Real objective (what the session delivers). The team's first taste of what they have actually joined, delivered entirely through environment rather than exposition. They leave with their Antikythera devices (without yet knowing what they are), the compressed train-heist orders, and a foundational unease they cannot name.

Window. No hard clock. This is the one unhurried session of the prologue — the reveal should breathe. Late autumn in the highlands: cold, clear, cooperative weather. The mountain is not the threat.

Complications.

  1. The scratch-signal. The site is unmarked by design. The only sign is a set of innocent-looking scratches on the rock the team was briefed to find. Miss it and they search.
  2. The bear. A brown bear has denned in the cave mouth for the season. The prologue's one fight, and the game's gentlest combat tutorial: mundane humans versus a single animal that would rather leave than die.
  3. The boulder. The way in looks like no way at all — a stone that reads as immovable, which two strong people can roll aside if they trust the signal and commit to a shove that looks futile.
  4. The chamber. The devices are warm to the touch. The note is on paper that doesn't scan as modern. The names engraved on the devices are not the players' names — yet.

Map Layout

Three connected play surfaces plus a briefing map, per VTT conventions. The team moves through them in sequence, and each surface carries one design pillar.

Map 1 — Approach & Cave Mouth (30-ft scale, wilderness→transition)

Overhead view of the shelf of karst at the top of the second technical climb. The cave mouth is a low dark slot in the cliff at the rear of the shelf.

Features.

  • Climb-top ledge (lower edge) — where the team arrives; a natural staging spot.
  • Scree and low autumn scrub across the shelf — soft cover, uneven footing.
  • The signal rock — a waist-high boulder near the cave mouth bearing the briefed scratch-pattern. Reading it (a Per check, or simply recalling the briefing) confirms the site. On the GM layer only until found.
  • A drop-off along one edge — real fall hazard, mostly for atmosphere and to make the climb-top feel earned.
  • The cave mouth — a dark low opening; from outside, nothing suggests it is anything but a cave.

Pillar: Exploration. Reading terrain, finding the signal, and deciding how to approach a cave that might be occupied (a smart team smells/hears the bear before entering).

Map 2 — The Den (5-ft scale, tactical) — ships lit and dark

The cave's entrance chamber: the bear's den.

Features.

  • Daylit mouth (front) — a bright zone bleeding in from Map 1; strong light/shadow contrast.
  • The nest — dragged brush, gnawed bones, matted bedding; the bear is here or near.
  • Breakdown columns and stalagmites — hard cover, sightline breaks, chokepoints.
  • A narrow throat-passage at the rear — the only way deeper (to Map 3); a natural chokepoint the team can use to funnel or flee the bear.
  • Dark layer — the same tile with the daylit mouth reduced to a glow and the interior lit only by the team's torches/lamps, for a team that tries to slip past the sleeping bear rather than fight it.

Pillar: Combat. The bear. Teaches initiative, positioning, and cover — and the lesson, on turn one of the campaign, that a straight fight is not the only win: driving the bear out the mouth (noise, fire, pressure toward its escape route) clears the room without a kill.

Map 3 — The Boulder & the Hidden Chamber (5-ft scale, tactical → reveal)

The rear passage dead-ends at the boulder; behind it lies the chamber.

Features.

  • The passage — tight, curving, so the dead-end boulder is a genuine "well, that's it, then" moment before the signal is spotted.
  • The boulder — placed as a movable-object token, not baked terrain. It reads as immovable; a second set of the briefed scratches, low on its face, marks the push-point. Two Strong characters coordinating can roll it aside. (See the puzzle beat.)
  • The chamber beyond — ~4m square, dry, dust-thick, undisturbed. A single stone bench centered; on it, a dark wooden tray of six brass-looking devices (one per PC, each engraved with an alias) and a small folded note. Faint orichalcum-radiance markings near the bench (invisible to the eye; a UV lamp reveals flowing patterns that track like stars).
  • Rendered with elevated, atmospheric painterly treatment (per boss-sequence escalation) — not for a fight, but to signal this matters. The reveal tile earns boss-tier art without a boss.

Pillar: Puzzle + Reveal. The boulder (recognize the signal, trust it, commit two people), then the wordless discovery.

Encounter & Beat Detail

The hike (Exploration). A ~4-hour highland approach with two technical climbs, the second requiring team coordination (anchoring, belaying, hauling each other up). Establishes physical capability, interpersonal dynamics, and tone. The team is well-equipped and in no real danger from the terrain — this is establishing, not stakes.

The signal (Exploration). At the cave mouth, the scratch-pattern. A team that studies the rock, or recalls the briefing line ("you will know the place by a mark most would walk past"), finds it and knows they've arrived. A team that doesn't will nose around until the GM lets a Per check land — never a dead stop.

The bear (Combat). The den is occupied. Run the bear as a morale-driven animal, not a monster: it postures, bluff-charges, and would genuinely prefer the team to back off. Three clean outcomes:

  • Drive it off — noise, fire, and pressure toward the daylit mouth send it fleeing. The intended, no-kill win. Reward the tactic.
  • Kill it — viable; a hard fight for unaugmented humans in close quarters. Carries a small, quiet moral weight (they killed a denning animal in its home) — the campaign's first faint ethical texture.
  • Slip past (dark layer) — a stealth-first team can try to creep through to the throat-passage without waking it. Failure wakes an angry, cornered bear (worst case); success teaches that avoidance is a legitimate solution.

The boulder (Puzzle). The dead-end. The trick is entirely trust: spot the low scratches, believe that this immovable stone is meant to move, and commit two Strong characters to a shove that looks pointless. It rolls. The lock is forgiving (retries are free) and non-trivial (the first instinct is to turn back). The briefing only ever hinted: "the way in looks like no way at all."

The chamber (Reveal). No NPC waits here. On the tray: six devices, each engraved with an operational alias none of the players have yet been given (the GM coordinates these names with players in advance — this is the moment they receive their codenames). Beside them, the note:

You will be wondering how these devices came to be here, marked with names you do not yet know are yours. That answer comes later. For now: trust the markings, take your assigned device, keep it close. They will keep you warm. They will not let you down. Your mission has compressed. At the coordinates below, at the hour given, a freight train will pass carrying a person who must not reach the place it is taking them. Intercept. Recover. Return to staging. — A.

Soft tells (reward attention; hold for the return trip if missed): the devices are warm in the cold chamber; the note's paper fiber "isn't modern" (Doc, on return, can confirm she can't identify it); the UV star-map markings by the bench. Let the players sit in the chamber and theorize. This is the campaign's foundational mystery landing — it should land slowly.

Branching Outcomes

  • Clean run. Bear driven off, boulder solved, chamber found, soft tells noticed → the ideal tone-set; the team leaves unsettled and hooked.
  • Bear killed. Mechanically fine; note the moral weight for later callback.
  • Someone mauled. A wound carried into the heist (Session 3+). No death — pull punches; it's Session 1.
  • Boulder stumps them. The signal is forgiving; nudge via the scratches, let them retry. Never a dead end.
  • Soft tells missed. Surface them later through Doc (the paper analysis, the device warmth becoming impossible to ignore).

There is no true failure state. The chamber is found, the orders recovered, the devices taken, and the campaign proceeds. The stakes here are tone and mystery, not survival.

NPCs & Entities

  • The Bear — a large, denning brown bear. Tough but not lethal-to-a-team; morale-driven (flees when pressed, dangerous only when cornered). Tuned as a combat tutorial, not a boss.
  • The Briefing Officer (remote / radio only) — calm, professional, evasive. "Trust the devices, even if you don't yet know what they are." See Arc 00 § Key NPCs.
  • Doc — at staging, not on the mountain. On return, can quietly confirm the note's paper "isn't modern," seeding the mystery without explaining it. (She already knows more than she says.)
  • No one in the chamber — deliberate. The evidence speaks; the players theorize alone.

Music Suite

Four cues per the Music Conventions four-tier structure. Shared palette: cold Carpathian highland instrumentation — sustained low strings, bass clarinet, a lonely hammered cimbalom (regional Central-European color), sparse hand-percussion. Shared mode: D Dorian, which lets the reveal cue bloom modally without a key change. A hidden ancient motif — a slow rise of open fifths over a drone — is buried faintly in the earlier tiers and only fully surfaces at the chamber.

Tier 1 — Exploration (the Approach)

Cold mountain-wilderness theme, sparse sustained low strings and bass clarinet over a distant drone, a lonely hammered cimbalom figure, D Dorian, 68 bpm. A slow three-note ascending motif, half-hidden. Instrumental, spacious, patient, faintly foreboding.

Tier 2 — Anticipation (the Cave Mouth & the Descent)

Substitutes for the Civilization tier: there is no town here, so this is the held-breath cue — the team at the threshold, the quiet before the den, the tight passage.

Tense low-string sustains and a single circling clarinet line over soft heartbeat percussion, cimbalom tremolo shimmer, D Dorian, 72 bpm. The ascending motif creeps a step higher without resolving. Instrumental, taut and expectant.

Tier 3 — Combat (the Den / the Bear)

Driving highland combat cue, urgent low strings and hand-drum ostinato, snarling bass clarinet stabs, cimbalom hammered hard, D Dorian, 132 bpm. A recurring four-note pursuit motif. Instrumental, primal and physical — the sound of animals in a stone room.

Tier 4 — Reveal (the Hidden Chamber)

Substitutes for the Boss tier: the campaign's foundational-mystery crescendo. No fight — awe. The buried ancient motif finally blooms in full.

Slow, reverent swell — the hidden open-fifths motif rising in full over a warm sustained drone, cimbalom and glassy high strings, a distant wordless choir pad seeping in, D Dorian turning luminous, 60 bpm. Instrumental, vast, ancient, and quietly overwhelming — wonder edged with dread.

Design Notes — Template Lessons

This mission was built full-depth on purpose: to derive a reusable pattern for the rest of the campaign. What it teaches:

  1. Three pillars in one compact tile-set. Exploration (Map 1), Combat (Map 2), Puzzle+Reveal (Map 3) — one pillar per surface, in sequence. A mission doesn't need sprawl to hit all three; it needs one good surface per pillar.
  2. Movable-object tokens as puzzles. The boulder is a token that looks like terrain but isn't. This is a cheap, reusable puzzle primitive for the projector app — a prop the GM can slide, hiding a path. (Directly exercises the app's token-on-map model.)
  3. Lit / dark layers unlock playstyle choice. Shipping the Den in both layers turns one combat room into "fight it or sneak it" without new art beyond a relight. Every interior tile should consider a dark layer.
  4. Soft-tell environmental storytelling. Warmth, wrong-paper, UV star-maps — reveals that reward attention and degrade gracefully (Doc surfaces them later if missed). Never gate the plot behind a spotted clue.
  5. Tutorial combat via a morale-driven animal. A bear that would rather leave teaches the system at survivable stakes and plants the "a fight isn't the only win" lesson on turn one.
  6. A reveal tile can earn boss-tier art without a boss. Escalate the art to mark emotional weight, not just mechanical danger. Map 3 is the proof.
  7. Briefing map vs play surface are different jobs. The parchment overview sells the fiction and the route; the painterly top-downs hold minis. Author both, and don't blur them.

Open Design Questions

  • The engraved aliases. Coordinated with players ahead of time (they "receive" their PC codenames here) — but what if a player hasn't settled on one? Fallback: the device is engraved with a symbol the player later chooses to name.
  • Bear statline. Needs a concrete tutorial-tuned block once the system is chosen (the campaign's biggest open variable — see Arc 01 § Open Questions).
  • "— A." who signed the note. Left deliberately ambiguous through the prologue (see Arc 00 § Open Design Questions); may recur as a signature on later cryptic communications.
  • Map 3 split. Kept boulder + chamber on one tile for continuity of the reveal. If the app prefers, split into a boulder-puzzle tile and a separate chamber tile with a dramatic transition between them.
