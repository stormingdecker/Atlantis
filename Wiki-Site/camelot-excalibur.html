The Camelot Arc — Recovering Excalibur

Status: Draft. The flagship legendary-artifact recovery arc — the gear-up spine's blade. Built on the Excalibur candidate. Multi-session (a small arc, not a one-shot).

Premise

The whole Matter of Britain — the sword in the stone, the glowing blade only the true king can draw, the Lady of the Lake, Merlin the impossible advisor, the return of the sword to the water — is the folk-memory of a botched EPOCH operation in post-Roman Britain, layered over a quiet multi-agency contest for the island as Rome's order collapses.

The plain facts under the legend:

  • Excalibur is an EPOCH Mark VI luminous longsword — smoke-clear alloy, a shard core that makes it always lit and always warm, self-sharpening, boost-cost zero. In 6th-century Britain it simply is a magic sword.
  • The "sword in the stone" is its EPOCH delivery cradle — a biometric-keyed mount. Only the handler EPOCH keyed it to can draw it free; everyone else finds it immovable. That is the whole "only the true king" mythology: a hardware lock, reframed as destiny.
  • The Lady of the Lake is, almost certainly, the EPOCH embed handler who lost it — and who wants it back. She can feel the blade across the valley (it's a bonfire to agent-sense); "returning the sword to the lake" is EPOCH's reclamation, or the site of their local anchor.

EPOCH's play was to seat a keyed god-king — Arthur — as a stability anchor for a Britain friendly to the trajectory that produces the hegemony (the same pattern as the Spear of Destiny and Kusanagi). It went wrong — the blade ended up in-era, in the wrong hands, and the operation calcified into legend.

Why it matters

  • The flagship blade. Recovering Excalibur is the single biggest step on the gear-up curve — the best weapon in any ATA armory, and prime Mark VI reverse-engineering (accelerated by Ember).
  • Denial. Even if ATA can't keep it, taking it away from EPOCH denies them a god-king anchor in Britain — a strategic win in its own right.
  • A recurring ally. If Merlin is ATA (see below), identifying and linking up with him opens a durable contact in the deep past.

The board (cast)

Players must map the board before they can act — two rival agents are in the court, both agent-sense-detectable, both playing long games:

  • Merlin — an ATA embed, seated as Arthur's advisor to counter the EPOCH god-king play (steer the king, contest the Lady, keep Britain from calcifying EPOCH's way). The party's secret ally — once they realize the terrifying wizard reads as their side.
  • The Lady of the Lake — the EPOCH handler. Capture-tier antagonist. Ran the god-king op, wants Excalibur back, and plausibly holds its biometric key.
  • Arthur — the keyed wielder. The arc's central ambiguity: EPOCH's installed pawn, an unwitting mundane who happened to match the key, or a man who can be turned? What the team decides about Arthur shapes everything.
  • Morgan / Mordred — optional rival assets (a third-party or EPOCH-aligned agent working the court's fault lines) — the internal-politics pressure that complicates any clean grab.

The arc

I — Insertion & the board. The team inserts into the court (as knights, envoys, or a visiting lord's retinue). Immediately, agent-sense lights up: someone like us is here — two someones. The session is investigation and cover: work out who Merlin and the Lady are, who Arthur is, and where the sword is. No grabs yet.

II — The Sword. Locate Excalibur — in its cradle (the stone) if undrawn, or in Arthur's hand if the legend's already moving. It's trivial to find (it glows; it screams to agent-sense) and hard to take: the keyed-wielder problem gates access, and the Lady is watching it.

III — The Contest. EPOCH moves to reclaim (the Lady's recovery op) while court politics (Morgan/Mordred, succession, war with the Saxons) churn. The team must out-maneuver both — win the blade on their terms (a battle, a ceremony, a theft, a negotiated hand-off) rather than an open agent-vs-agent firefight that blows every cover in Britain.

IV — The Lake (climax). "The return of the sword to the lake" becomes the extraction event — but on whose terms? The Lake is the Lady's site, plausibly an EPOCH local anchor (worth investigating: what's in it?). The team either drops home with Excalibur under a bonfire of agent-sense pursuit, or the Lady reclaims it, or it's lost to the water for another age.

The keyed-wielder problem

Excalibur is biometric-keyed to its handler — a story gate, not a bonding mechanic. To take a working blade home the team must solve one of:

  • Bring the wielder — extract Arthur (or whoever's keyed) with the sword (huge ripple; is Arthur willing?).
  • Take the cradle — lift the whole delivery mount and let ATA re-key it at base (the foundry route — needs Ember).
  • Get the key — obtain the biometric credential from the Lady/EPOCH (a heist or a capture).

A blade taken without solving this is a beautiful, glowing brick — recoverable as reverse-engineering material and denial, but not a usable weapon until ATA cracks the lock.

The glow in play

The moment the team holds Excalibur, they hold a beacon: it can't be sheathed dark, it's warm to the grip, and every EPOCH agent in Britain (starting with the Lady) feels it. Extraction happens under that pressure. If the team doesn't yet have an ATA suppression scabbard (an Ember/EPOCH-kit unlock), the first exfil with Excalibur is loud — a running fight, not a quiet fade. That scabbard becomes an obvious next build goal.

Rewards

  • Excalibur — the campaign's flagship blade (once the key is solved), or a denial + reverse-engineering prize if not.
  • Foundry boost — reverse-engineering Mark VI alloy/self-sharpening (Ember accelerates).
  • Merlin — a durable ATA contact in the deep past, if identified and allied.
  • Strategic — EPOCH denied a Britain god-king anchor; intel on their sub-Roman operation.

Failure states (per Mission-Design tiers)

  • Decisive win: blade + key recovered, EPOCH's op rolled up, Merlin allied.
  • Win with cost: blade recovered but keyless (a brick until cracked), or a cover burned, or Arthur lost.
  • Tactical defeat / strategic win: the Lady reclaims Excalibur, but the team exposes EPOCH's entire Britain god-king program.
  • Failure with survival: the sword returns to the lake for another age; the legend closes; the team leaves with the map of the board for a later attempt.

Design decisions (canon)

  • Merlin is ATA — a deep-past visible embed and the party's secret ally. Full page: Merlin. The arc is a two-agency chess game.
  • The Lake is an EPOCH anchor — the climax is a raid on a submerged facility, not just an exfil. The Lady of the Lake deploys from it and holds Excalibur's key.
  • Recovering Excalibur ends the Arthurian age. The legend's fall (Camlann, Avalon, the sword returned to the water) is the agencies withdrawing once the artifact is gone — the canon-clean, poignant way to close the arc.
