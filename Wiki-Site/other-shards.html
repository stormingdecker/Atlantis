Other Shards

GM-ONLY. The cosmological iceberg — most of it is below the waterline.

Both ATA and EPOCH know of two shards: the Origin (Mediterranean, ~4000 BCE) and the Kunlun Shard (Tibet, ~2200 CE). They each privately suspect there are more. Neither has proven it. Neither has admitted the suspicion to the other.

This file holds the live worldbuilding dials for what other shards might exist, who owns them, and what they imply for the campaign.

Why More Shards Probably Exist

The Origin meteor was multi-dimensional and fragmented in 4D on atmospheric entry (see The Origin § 4D Fragmentation), producing displaced (spatially and temporally) secondary impacts. Physics-of-the-setting reasons:

  • A multi-dimensional object passing through normal spacetime would naturally generate many fragments traveling on many trajectories.
  • The temporal-displacement mechanism that delayed the Kunlun arrival by ~6200 years could just as easily have produced fragments delayed by 5000 years (still unarrived), or arriving at negative temporal displacement (impacts before 4000 BCE — but no gradient, so no one knows).
  • Even small fragments could anchor minor gradients usable by small operations.

Candidate Shards

1. The Andean Shard

  • Hypothetical location: somewhere in the Andes mountains, possibly under the Atacama or in a Peruvian highland.
  • Hypothetical impact date: ~1500 BCE, coinciding with the rise of Chavín culture in Peru.
  • Hypothetical owner: a third agency none of the players have heard of, with a strict isolationist doctrine. They have NOT joined the ATA-EPOCH war because they don't want their existence revealed. They run a tiny gradient anchored at ~1500 BCE with maybe 3000 years of reach in either direction (reachable: ~4500 BCE through ~1500 CE).
  • Why interesting: the Mesoamerican feathered-serpent deployments (Quetzalcoatl, Kukulkan) that BOTH agencies disclaim attribution for — were they actually this third agency's work? See ../Myth-Engineering/Overview.md "Quetzalcoatl Deployment."

2. The Australian Shard

  • Hypothetical location: somewhere in the Western Desert or Pilbara region.
  • Hypothetical impact date: very recent — possibly future, possibly already-arrived-and-undiscovered.
  • Hypothetical owner: unknown, possibly no one.
  • Why interesting: Aboriginal Australian cosmology has strong "Dreamtime" concepts that align eerily with the agency vocabulary about meteor-shaped time. If a shard arrived in deep prehistory and was discovered by Aboriginal peoples, it could be the source of those traditions — and any modern agency seeking to claim it would face the moral question of doing so over the objections of the traditional custodians.

3. The Sub-Saharan Shard

  • Hypothetical location: Ethiopian highlands or East African Rift.
  • Hypothetical impact date: ~3000 BCE.
  • Hypothetical owner: lost. Discovered by an early civilization, controlled briefly, then lost again — possibly buried by tectonic activity, possibly deliberately concealed and the keepers killed off.
  • Why interesting: there is a third dormant gradient somewhere in Africa that no one is using. If ATA or EPOCH found it, they could double their operational range. If a third party found it first...

4. The Pacific Shard

  • Hypothetical location: ocean floor, possibly near the Mariana Trench.
  • Hypothetical impact date: ~6000 BCE — predates the Origin.
  • Hypothetical owner: none, because the impact is so deep and so isolated that no human has ever encountered it.
  • Why interesting: if a pre-Origin shard exists and remains undiscovered, then in theory the gradient extending from it reaches before the Origin's gradient. Whoever finds it first gets access to deep prehistory — and could, in principle, alter human history before either ATA or EPOCH could detect or counter it. A campaign-ending threat.

6. The Further Future Shard

  • Hypothetical location: unknown — possibly Earth, possibly somewhere offworld.
  • Hypothetical impact date: 2500+ CE — even further in our future than the Kunlun Shard.
  • Hypothetical owner: no one yet, because it hasn't impacted by the time of any current operation.
  • Why interesting: if the pattern of shard arrivals is intentional (see ../Core-Setting/The-Kunlun-Shard.md "Why It Arrived Late"), then more shards are scheduled to arrive in our future. A third agency may be waiting to be born in 2500 CE, 3000 CE, or further out. From their perspective, both ATA and EPOCH are past civilizations to be observed and possibly manipulated. From ATA and EPOCH's perspective, the future is the most dangerous direction — there could be a foe out there with even greater technological superiority than EPOCH has over ATA. This is the deepest dread of agency leadership: we are not the last move in this game.

5. The Hidden Atlantean Cache

Not really another shard — but worth noting here. The agency's Bronze Age surface-cover era of operations (no native civilization — see ../Core-Setting/Atlantis.md), during its peak, may have deliberately broken off small pieces of the Origin meteor and seeded them in remote locations as backup gradient anchors. If any of these caches survive, they would be:

  • Smaller than even the Kunlun Shard.
  • In locations chosen by Bronze-era agency strategists for reasons we don't know.
  • Potentially recoverable by ATA, EPOCH, or whoever finds them first.

If this happened, there could be a dozen tiny gradients across the world, most of them long forgotten, occasionally rediscovered by accident.

What This Implies for Campaign Design

  • The "two-agency Cold War" is the visible layer of the conflict. Beneath it, there's room for third parties of varying scale.
  • Late-campaign reveals can introduce new agencies, new gradients, and entire new doctrines.
  • Players who become aware of other shards face strategic choices: report to ATA central command (who will weaponize the knowledge) or keep it private (who knows what they could do with their own gradient?).
  • A competing trilateral, quadrilateral, or n-lateral version of the Cold War metaphor becomes available — the campaign can drift from "spy vs. spy" to "great game of nations" in late seasons.

Recommended Reveal Sequence (working draft)

  • Arc 1–3: strictly ATA vs. EPOCH. Players don't know other shards exist.
  • Arc 4: first hint — an unexplained mission outcome implies a third party was operating. Players ask questions. Command deflects.
  • Arc 5–6: confirmation of a third agency exists. Investigation into who, where.
  • Arc 7+: the player crew has to decide their stance toward the multi-agency cosmology — alliance? Containment? Exposure?
  • Endgame: the question of what the meteor IS (see other GM-Secrets files) becomes inseparable from "why does it keep producing shards in patterns that look almost intentional?"
