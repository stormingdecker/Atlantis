Rasputin

EPOCH sublimated senior asset. Natural psi pre-recruit, extracted 1916 Petrograd. Currently operational at Shambhala. The most dangerous EPOCH field agent ATA expects to encounter.

Cover Identity

Grigori Yefimovich Rasputin, born 1869 in the village of Pokrovskoye, Tobolsk Governorate, Russian Empire. Peasant origin. Reputed wandering holy man (strannik) from the 1890s onward. Acquired influence over the Russian imperial family — particularly Tsarina Alexandra — from approximately 1905, ostensibly through his apparent ability to alleviate the symptoms of Tsarevich Alexei's hemophilia. Notorious figure of the late Imperial Russian court. Officially killed December 16/30, 1916, at the Yusupov Moika Palace in Petrograd, by a small group of nobles led by Prince Felix Yusupov.

The historical record of his pre-extraction life is substantially accurate. He really did wander Russia as a religious mystic. He really did acquire influence at court. He really did have the documented effect on Alexei's symptoms. He really was the subject of intense political controversy in the final years of the Romanov dynasty. The fiction's embellishments — the more lurid sexual rumors, some of the more sensational political conspiracies — are the product of contemporary press hostility and posthumous mythology, not the actual man.

Within EPOCH he operates under a 2200s operational designation (ATA archives transliterate it as "Vetka" — "branch" or "shoot"). The designation is bureaucratic, not chosen by him; he was never asked. He continues to refer to himself, in his own internal language, as Grigori.

Inside ATA intelligence archives he is referred to as Rasputin in operational chatter and as Subject Vetka in formal reports.

True Identity

A natural psi of unusual breadth rather than unusual depth. ATA's working assessment classifies him as a Grade II generalist — multiple psi capabilities at moderate levels rather than a single capability at high levels. His historical capability set, before EPOCH's enhancement protocols:

  • Empathic influence (suggestion). Born talent for impressing his will on others through sustained eye contact and physical proximity. Not mind control; closer to a powerful, persistent, unconscious nudge. In his pre-recruitment life this read as "personal magnetism" and "hypnotic gaze" — both attested in voluminous contemporary accounts.
  • Bio-tactile healing. Touch-based suppression of pain and certain symptom clusters. His effect on Alexei's hemophilic episodes was real: a temporary palliative for bleeding and pain, achieved through what modern EPOCH researchers identify as a localized vasoconstriction/coagulation effect plus pain-suppression empathy. He could not cure the condition; he could meaningfully manage acute episodes.
  • Anomalous physical resilience. Significantly elevated baseline pain tolerance, drug tolerance, and constitutional toughness. The historical record of his survival of multiple attempted poisonings (well-documented before the assassination) reflects real metabolic anomaly, not theatre.
  • Limited precognition. Fragmentary near-term flashes — usually combat-tactical, rarely strategic. Less reliable than Moriarty's or Sigerson's; useful only at close range and under stress.

EPOCH identified him around 1903, via observation of his early reputation in the Siberian religious circuit. His file in the EPOCH archives shows that he was pre-recruited in approximately 1909 — meaning he was contacted while still alive, told a curated version of the agency's existence (he was, after all, a religious mystic; the framing leaned heavily into "powers beyond the visible world"), and gradually integrated into EPOCH's intelligence apparatus while continuing his public life as a holy man. By 1914 he was a witting EPOCH asset operating against ATA's interests in the late Imperial court.

His role in EPOCH's 1910s Russian operations is now believed to have included: actively destabilizing the late Romanov court (encouraging Alexandra's growing isolation and the political chaos that surrounded Alexei's illness), influencing the Tsar's wartime decision-making during 1914–1916 (multiple decisions that accelerated military and economic collapse were taken on Rasputin's "spiritual counsel"), and providing EPOCH a forward intelligence node inside the imperial inner circle. He was, by EPOCH's measures, a high-value asset whose loss would be politically and operationally costly.

He was not originally slated for sublimation. EPOCH preferred to keep him operational in his own era for as long as possible. The sublimation in 1916 was a reactive operation, triggered by intelligence that the Yusupov conspiracy was forming and would likely succeed in killing him within weeks.

The Yusupov Operation

The assassination of Rasputin in the basement of the Moika Palace on the night of December 16/30, 1916, was the exfiltration event. The public-record narrative — Yusupov and his fellow conspirators (Grand Duke Dmitri Pavlovich, Vladimir Purishkevich, Sergei Sukhotin, Dr. Stanislaus de Lazovert) luring Rasputin to the palace, feeding him poisoned wine and cakes, shooting him when the poison failed, beating him, and ultimately throwing his body into the Malaya Nevka river under the ice — is what Yusupov and the conspirators believed they did. It is not what actually happened.

What actually happened: EPOCH intelligence had been tracking the conspiracy for approximately six weeks. The agency assessed (correctly) that Yusupov was unstable, motivated, and would act regardless of EPOCH's preferences. Rather than attempt to disrupt the conspiracy — which would have alerted the broader court and accelerated other security risks — EPOCH chose to use it. Yusupov was identified as an unwitting cover-provider: a conspirator whose evident commitment to killing Rasputin would supply the perfect narrative cover for an EPOCH extraction.

The operation, reconstructed from later EPOCH archive fragments:

  • Surveillance and timing. EPOCH operatives were positioned around the Moika Palace on the night of the planned assassination. The agency had pre-staged a substitute body — a deceased male of Rasputin's approximate build and age, prepared at an EPOCH facility, dressed in clothes matching what Rasputin was known to be wearing that evening.
  • Pre-event briefing. Rasputin himself was briefed earlier that day by his EPOCH handler. He knew the conspiracy was active. He knew the extraction would occur. He was not told the precise mechanism, on the principle that his behavior during the assassination needed to be authentically surprised for the conspirators to accept what they were seeing.
  • The poisoning. Lazovert's cyanide was real but ineffective — Rasputin's elevated drug tolerance, augmented by an EPOCH-supplied prophylactic taken hours earlier, neutralized the dose. His apparent lack of reaction terrified Yusupov, as planned.
  • The shooting. Yusupov shot Rasputin in the chest. The bullet was real; Rasputin's psi-augmented resilience (combined with the prophylactic) prevented immediate death. He collapsed, was assumed dead, and was briefly left unattended while the conspirators panicked upstairs.
  • The extraction. During the brief window when the basement was unattended, two EPOCH operatives entered through a service corridor, extracted Rasputin (alive, conscious, in pain but ambulatory with support), and placed the pre-prepared substitute body in his stead.
  • The "recovery." When Rasputin (later, in the conspirators' belief) "revived" and "attacked" Yusupov, what Yusupov actually saw was the substitute body, animated briefly by an EPOCH neural-stim device set to produce a few seconds of crude motor response. The device was designed for exactly this kind of cover operation; the conspirators' panic ensured nobody examined the "revived" body closely. Yusupov shot the substitute several more times. The conspirators wrapped the body and disposed of it in the river per the original plan.
  • The autopsy and the burning. The substitute body was recovered three days later. The autopsy was performed on the substitute. The disputed forensic findings in the historical record (drowning vs. gunshot, body in poses inconsistent with the conspirators' account) reflect the substitute's actual condition, not Rasputin's. The body was buried at Tsarskoye Selo, then exhumed and burned during the Revolution in 1917 — an event that destroyed the substitution evidence and that EPOCH operatives may have facilitated, though the chaos of the period makes this unprovable.
  • The extraction transit. Rasputin was treated at an EPOCH field facility in Petrograd for approximately 48 hours (stabilization of the gunshot wound), then transited via boost-jump to Shambhala in late 1916 (subjective EPOCH time approximately 2225). His revival, infusion, behavioral conditioning, and integration as a senior EPOCH asset followed standard sublimation protocols.

Yusupov, Pavlovich, Purishkevich, and the others. None of them ever knew. Yusupov in particular spent the rest of his life believing he had killed Rasputin. His later writings (including his memoir Lost Splendor, 1953) describe the assassination in detail and contain numerous specific errors that ATA's Reality Analysis division has identified as EPOCH cover-narrative seeding. EPOCH did not significantly suppress Yusupov's account; the inaccuracies were operationally useful, contributing to the lasting confusion about what actually happened at the Moika Palace.

Sublimation, Training, and Deployment (1916–present)

Rasputin's integration into EPOCH followed standard protocols for coerced-becomes-cooperative sublimations. Initial resistance was significant — he understood, on revival in the 2200s, that he had been used as well as saved — but EPOCH's behavioral conditioning protocols and his own pragmatic temperament eventually produced functional cooperation. He has been operational at Shambhala for approximately 109 subjective years as of the campaign's present.

His training arc:

  • First 5 years (subjective): medical revival (the gunshot wound left long-term complications that 2200s medicine resolved), infusion with EPOCH's standard senior-asset package (saturated; significantly above ATA infusion baselines), behavioral conditioning and ideological integration. The conditioning was successful — he is now broadly a believer in EPOCH's mission, though with his own characteristic religious-mystical framing of it. He understands the agency's stake in the 2200s hegemony and accepts it as the contemporary form of what he previously understood as God's order.
  • Years 5–20: advanced psi training. His native generalist profile was deepened across all four of his original capabilities; he now operates at Grade III enhanced on the EPOCH classification scale — significantly more dangerous than his pre-extraction baseline. EPOCH also added one cultivated capability: a moderate-grade psychic durability shield that further enhances his combat resilience.
  • Years 20+: operational deployment. He has been used by EPOCH for approximately ninety subjective years as a field-deployable senior asset — the agency's most-feared close-encounter agent. He is deployed sparingly, almost exclusively to operations where his combination of personal charisma, healing-bait, and combat resilience can be decisive. His operational tempo averages 2–4 missions per subjective year; most are denied to ATA intelligence entirely.

ATA confirmation of his survival came relatively late — approximately the 1990s in subjective real-time, based on intercepted EPOCH archive fragments and one inconclusive sighting in Lhasa in 1998. The agency's intelligence picture of his deployment history remains fragmentary. Confirmed appearances since extraction include: Berlin, 1923 (counter-ops during the early Soviet-German diplomatic period); Manchuria, 1936 (operations adjacent to the Japanese puppet state); somewhere in eastern Europe, 1968 (Prague Spring counter-ops, suspected); and the Lhasa sighting in 1998. ATA suspects many more deployments are simply unconfirmed.

Capabilities (Current, Post-Enhancement)

  • Empathic Suggestion (Active). Rasputin's primary social weapon. Distinct from Helen's passive glamour (continuous low-amplitude amplification), his suggestion is active, directional, and targetable. He selects an individual within ~5m line-of-sight, makes sustained eye contact, and projects an empathic suggestion — typically a single specific impulse ("set down the weapon," "trust me," "leave the room"). Mundane targets resist on Will checks at significant penalty (-4 or equivalent). Infused agents resist at standard difficulty but are not immune. The suggestion is sustained as long as he maintains concentration and line-of-sight; it dissolves when broken. Targets often do not remember resisting (or complying) clearly afterward — the experience is described as religious, or as a moment of inexplicable conviction.
  • Bio-tactile Healing / Pain Suppression. Touch-based suppression of pain and acute symptom clusters in the target. Tactical use: he can stabilize a wounded ally rapidly, briefly cancel pain penalties on a combatant, or (more rarely) suppress an environmental hazard's effect on someone he is touching. Cannot cure conditions, restore HP, or revive the dead — this is suppression, not regeneration. The historical Alexei effect was this capability applied to hemophilic crises.
  • Combat Resilience (Augmented). EPOCH's enhancement of his native toughness produced one of the agency's most physically durable senior agents. He has elevated HP, significant damage reduction equivalent (passive psychic shield), high pain threshold, drug/poison tolerance, and accelerated regeneration. He is very hard to put down. The historical narrative of his impossible-to-kill survival in 1916 reflects his pre-enhancement baseline; he is significantly more durable now. Killing him in a single encounter is a tier above standard senior-agent difficulty.
  • Tactical Precognition. Short-range, fragmentary, primarily combat-applicable. He receives flashes of incoming attacks, immediate environmental hazards, and short-range tactical setups. Mechanically: defensive bonus in combat, occasional "anticipated the trap" moments. Not a strategic asset like Moriarty's or Sigerson's deeper precog; useful only in his own field engagements.
  • Standard EPOCH senior-asset infusion: saturated agent-sense (he reads loud in psi terms — other agents can feel him at unusual range), unaging, accelerated healing, the full enhancement suite.
  • Cultural and linguistic: Russian (native), English (fluent — agency training), Mandarin (fluent — agency training, second-language workhorse at Shambhala), passable French and German. He has retained his Orthodox Christian religious framework and continues to practice it in his quarters; EPOCH has not interfered with this and considers his religious self-conception an operationally stabilizing factor.

Limitations and Constraints

  • Suggestion requires line-of-sight and concentration. Blindfolding him, breaking eye contact, or forcing him to split his attention reduces his social weapon to baseline charisma. Helmets with full visors are effective against him. So is shooting him from behind cover.
  • Healing is suppression, not restoration. He cannot bring agents back from the brink of death; he can only buy them time. ATA after-action reports indicate this has limited his utility in extraction operations where his teammates have taken serious wounds.
  • Combat resilience does not equal invulnerability. He can be killed — by sustained sufficient damage, by area-effect weapons that bypass his shield, by attacks that don't require him to actively defend (poison gas, drowning under conditions where his drug tolerance can't compensate, environmental destruction). The 1916 historical narrative documents his pre-enhancement limits; modern ATA tactical doctrine accepts the heavier resilience as a fact but does not consider him invincible.
  • Precognition is short-range and tactical-only. He cannot anticipate strategic moves, distant ambushes, or non-immediate plans. He has been successfully tactically deceived in past ATA engagements when the deception operated at a layer his precog could not sense.
  • Personal religiosity. He retains a sincere Orthodox Christian framework. This is not in itself a vulnerability, but ATA tradecraft has noted that he is moved by religious symbolism in a way that can be operationally exploited (a hostile religious figure, a sacred site, a moment of personal religious meaning) — not as a paralyzer but as a momentary distraction.
  • Saturated infusion is loud in agent-sense. Any infused ATA agent in his vicinity (~50m range) will feel him. He cannot infiltrate ATA secure facilities undetected. His operational deployments are correspondingly overt — he goes in expecting to be detected and fights through it.
  • Yusupov was unwitting. This means there is no protected EPOCH cover narrative around the 1916 events that ATA cannot, in principle, unwind. ATA's Reality Analysis division has reconstructed the operation in retrospect; the EPOCH archive fragments are damning. If the reconstruction were ever publicly disseminated (and could be made culturally credible), the political-historical consequences could be operationally costly for EPOCH — though by what mechanism remains unclear.

Operational Role

  • Field-deployable senior asset. Rasputin is EPOCH's go-to operator for missions requiring direct personal presence with high stakes — close-range confrontations, courtly insertion in regimes where his religious-mystic profile provides natural cover, encounters where his particular combination of charisma and combat resilience is decisive.
  • Boss-tier encounter signature. When ATA detects that Rasputin is deploying to a theater, ATA's standing doctrine is to avoid direct confrontation and reorient missions around stealth, denial, or escalation to higher-tier ATA assets. Junior ATA agents are explicitly warned away from engagement.
  • Late-Imperial-Russia specialist. His era-specific knowledge of the Petrograd court, the Russian Orthodox church, the pre-revolutionary intelligence environment, and the specific personalities of the late Romanov circle make him EPOCH's first call for any mission that touches that era.
  • Romanov-thread operations. ATA suspects (without confirmation) that Rasputin has been operationally involved in EPOCH's handling of the Romanov sublimation thread — that he was personally involved in the 1918 Yekaterinburg events, either as participant or as overseer. If true, his knowledge of what happened to which family members is a strategic intelligence target.

What He Knows (Of Operational Interest to ATA)

  • The full EPOCH organizational structure at Shambhala — names, faces, capabilities, internal politics. A captured Rasputin would be the largest single intelligence haul ATA has ever achieved.
  • The actual events of December 1916 — the Yusupov operation in EPOCH-internal detail, including the operatives involved, the substitute body's origin, and the post-extraction transit.
  • The Romanov fate — what really happened at Yekaterinburg in 1918. Confidence in this is high but unconfirmed.
  • Multiple EPOCH operations across the 20th century that he personally participated in. Specific operations are not catalogued in ATA's intelligence picture.
  • Moriarty's current condition and operational tempo — the two senior EPOCH precog/social assets have presumably collaborated. Rasputin would know things about Moriarty that even Sigerson does not.

Vulnerabilities (Engagement Doctrine)

ATA's standing tactical guidance for Rasputin encounters:

  1. Do not engage in his preferred terrain. Indoor close-quarters, dim lighting, narrow lines of approach — these favor him. Force engagements into open terrain with clear sightlines.
  2. Negate the suggestion vector. Visors, gas masks, mirrored eyewear, communications-routed targeting (let snipers fire while Rasputin is engaging someone else). Suggestion-immune ATA agents (rare; a small cadre exists with native psi-defense traits) should be deployed when possible.
  3. Exploit area-effect engagement. His personal resilience is high; his ability to defend against distributed damage is lower. Indirect-fire ordnance, gas, environmental destruction (collapsing the building on him) are doctrinally favored over direct small-arms engagement.
  4. Plan for survival, not victory. A successful Rasputin encounter for an ATA team is typically escape with intelligence and minimal losses. Killing or capturing him is a tier above standard senior-NPC difficulty and should be the centerpiece of a dedicated multi-mission arc, not an opportunistic action.
  5. Anchor-jamming priority. If he is forced to retreat, his drop-home is cheap (EPOCH has dense modern-era infrastructure). Anchor-jamming or fuel-dampening equipment is the only reliable way to deny him exfiltration. ATA's tactical doctrine prioritizes anchor-jamming over kinetic engagement.
  6. The religious-symbolic vector is the only documented social-engineering weakness. Use sparingly; it cannot be relied on but has produced exploitable hesitation in past engagements.

Pronouns and Presentation

Rasputin presents as male. Pronouns: he/him. Appearance largely matches the historical record — tall, dark, bearded, intense eyes; he has aged into a form that looks roughly mid-fifties biological by infusion locking, with the distinctive presence that contemporary accounts always noted. He continues to dress in modified versions of his original religious-mystic style for cover operations in compatible eras; in 2200s Shambhala he wears EPOCH operational uniform with his cross visible.

Encounter Notes (For Mission Design)

If players encounter Rasputin:

  • He is a boss-tier encounter, EPOCH-side equivalent of Helen (and arguably more dangerous in direct engagement, since his capability set is field-applicable rather than social-glamour-based).
  • The encounter should be telegraphed. ATA intelligence will warn the team that Rasputin is deploying to a given theater. If the team chooses to engage anyway, they are doing so with full knowledge of the threat level. Surprise Rasputin encounters are bad mission design unless the surprise is the centerpiece of the arc.
  • The encounter should be survivable. Players who follow ATA doctrine (avoid direct engagement, prioritize anchor-jamming, plan for escape with intelligence) can have a satisfying encounter without dying. Players who insist on direct combat against him are warned in-fiction that they are choosing to take catastrophic risk.
  • The capture arc is one of the campaign's potential mid-to-late-game centerpieces. A multi-mission arc culminating in Rasputin's capture would be a major intelligence and political win for ATA — and a major resource investment to set up correctly. Worth pre-planning if the campaign reaches that scale.
  • Killing him is the easiest outcome and the worst long-term — denies the intelligence and biotech sample value, generates EPOCH escalation, and politically gives EPOCH a martyr they can use internally to motivate further operations. ATA doctrine strongly prefers capture or even defeat-without-kill.
  • Players who fail against him should not necessarily die. Rasputin's preferred operational outcome is not killing ATA agents — it is completing his own mission and exfiltrating. A defeated ATA team that flees with losses is, for him, an acceptable result. He may pursue and finish off agents who could threaten his exfiltration, but he is not a sadist.

Open Questions

  • Yusupov's awareness. Working assumption: zero. But if the GM wants a late-campaign twist, Yusupov could have been partially witting — recruited or coerced by EPOCH at some point in his post-1916 exile, with his subsequent decades of memoir-writing constituting deliberate EPOCH cover-narrative seeding. This would change the political stakes of the historical-record recovery.
  • Rasputin's personal feelings about EPOCH. Working assumption: functional cooperation tinged with private resentment. He is not a perfect EPOCH believer; the conditioning was successful but not total. Under sufficient pressure he might be turnable — though no ATA approach has come close to this yet.
  • Operational pace. ATA estimates 2-4 missions per subjective year. The actual rate may be higher; the agency's intelligence picture is incomplete.
  • Family. Historical Rasputin had three surviving children (Dmitri, Matryona, Varvara). All three lived into the 20th century; one (Matryona) emigrated to France and later the United States, dying in 1977. Did Rasputin ever see them after his sublimation? Did EPOCH manage contact? Could ATA approach surviving descendants for intelligence about his pre-extraction psychology? Worth pre-thinking.
  • The captured-Rasputin endgame. If the campaign reaches the point where a multi-mission capture arc succeeds, what does ATA do with him? Trial? Reconditioning attempt? Confined consultant? Trade with EPOCH for ATA assets they hold? Multiple campaign-shaping resolution options.
