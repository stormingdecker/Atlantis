Prehistory

The era before The Origin event — and the impact itself.

Before Impact (pre-~4000 BCE)

Largely undefined. Humanity exists as scattered hunter-gatherer and early agricultural populations. The Neolithic agricultural revolution is well underway in the Fertile Crescent; villages and proto-cities exist in Mesopotamia and the Levant. But empires, writing, and organized state-level culture have not emerged.

Pre-impact prehistory is technically reachable but operationally pointless. The Origin's gradient extends in both directions from the impact moment (~4000 BCE), so ATA can in principle send agents to ~5000 BCE or even ~6000 BCE — but there is no organized civilization to influence and almost no embedded anchors. The only entities ever to make pre-Origin journeys are ATA xenoarchaeology specialists and the occasional GM-secret-tier expedition.

If a pre-Origin shard exists elsewhere on Earth (the Pacific Shard hypothesis), then someone or something has the capability to reach pre-Origin prehistory with operational efficiency. Neither known agency has confirmed this. Both fear it.

The Impact (~4000 BCE)

The meteor strikes the eastern Mediterranean seabed. The moment is later reconstructed (by ATA astronomers, working forward from impact-effect evidence) as the founding of The Origin.

What we know:

  • The atmosphere flashed visible spectrum bright enough to be recorded in oral tradition across three continents.
  • Tsunami activity reshaped coastlines from the Levant to Iberia.
  • The crater formed and stayed warm.
  • Bronze Age population genetics show a modest "Mediterranean diaspora" radiating outward in the centuries following — consistent with displaced coastal populations moving inland and eventually contributing genetic stock to the subsequent rise of Sumerian and Egyptian populations.

Post-Impact, Pre-Construction (~4000–3500 BCE)

The ~500-year window between impact and the start of the agency's Bronze Age surface-cover construction era is the least active era of the gradient. The meteor sits in its still-warm crater, monitored intermittently by future-agency reconnaissance teams who survey the site and prepare for the construction era to come. Local human populations (scattered hunter-gatherers and early agriculturalists in the eastern Mediterranean) have no contact with the future-agents and no awareness of the site's significance.

This era is the earliest reachable point in the temporal gradient — but operationally the most dangerous for travel. The gradient is unstable in the immediate post-impact centuries (the meteor's effects on local spacetime are still settling). Language, customs, and infrastructure for embedded agents are all minimal. Missions to this era are rare, high-risk, and usually limited to reconnaissance observation rather than active intervention.

Beginning around ~3500 BCE, larger agency operations arrive to establish the surface cover-civilization that will be remembered as Bronze Age "Atlantis." Construction of the facility itself begins in this period — see ../Core-Setting/Atlantis-Facility.md for the construction history. There were never "native Atlanteans" in the mythical sense: the surface-cover population was the agency's earliest era of operations, posing as a coastal civilization. See ../Core-Setting/Atlantis.md.
