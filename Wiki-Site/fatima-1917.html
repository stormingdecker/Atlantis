Fátima, 13 October 1917

Status: Draft. The capstone set-piece of The Long 1910s — the belle-époque surge cresting in front of 70,000 witnesses. See also the Missions Overview and the Reality Log.

Premise

On 13 October 1917 an estimated ~70,000 people stood in the rain at the Cova da Iria, drawn by three shepherd children — Lúcia, Francisco, and Jacinta — who had reported monthly Marian apparitions since May and been promised a "miracle." For several minutes the crowd watched the sun dance, spin, pulse through colours, and plunge toward the earth, then recede. The record calls it the Miracle of the Sun. ATA calls it a problem.

The true nature is a mass-observed psi / Mark broadcast — a large-scale psionic-optical projection driving shared perceptual entrainment across the whole hollow, the same phenomenon as the Dancing Plague of 1518 run at an unprecedented scale. The three "seers" are not frauds and not saints: they are unusually strong natural psi-sensitives who have been partially receiving the broadcast for months. Their "three secrets" are precognitive and telepathic leakage, dressed by devout children in the only vocabulary they have.

A year after the Yusupov Cellar put a face on the surge, Fátima is where it goes loud. What the mission will not hand you is the source. That is the central question the team is sent to answer (see Open Design Questions). 1917 Portugal sits far from ATA's Bronze-Age Mediterranean strength but deep inside EPOCH's cheap-reach window — so ATA arrives here as the transit-stretched away-team, thin and outnumbered, contesting the enemy on ground the enemy can reach almost for free. See Far-Traveler.

Map & Setting

Two linked play surfaces (per top-down VTT conventions); the team works the crowd below while the source waits above.

  • Primary — the Cova da Iria. A wide, rain-soaked natural hollow packed shoulder-to-shoulder with pilgrims: umbrellas, mud, cart-tracks, a small holm-oak shrine, the children's vantage, clergy and press at the edges, Republican gendarmes and hostile secular officials working the fringes. Social + exploration pillar. Muddy earth, dense soft-cover crowd tokens, sightlines choked by bodies and weather. Ships overcast (pre-event) and light-storm (the broadcast, when the sky-glare rewrites lighting across the tile).
  • Secondary — the Ridge Farmhouse. A stone smallholding and outbuilding on the rise overlooking the hollow: a clean firing line of sight to the whole crowd, the natural spot to hide an emitter and its operators. Puzzle + combat pillar. Escalates to boss-sequence dressing if the source is a live EPOCH rig (see boss-sequence escalation).

Objectives

  1. Insert under period cover into a wartime, anticlerical, WWI-era Portugal — and hold it in a crowd of 70,000, not an empty tomb.
  2. Locate the broadcast source by triangulating the projection across the hollow (the light behaves like it has an origin).
  3. Assess the three children — confirm they are receivers, not the source, and decide what to do about what they now carry.
  4. Confront and shut down the source — an EPOCH psi rig and operators, or a rogue/overloaded asset — before the "miracle" runs a second time.
  5. Extract without adding to the anomaly — every overt act in front of this crowd deepens the largest Timeline Attention spike in the campaign.

Containment-over-force is incentivized: this is the most public anomaly the team will ever touch. A firefight witnessed by 70,000 people and photographed by the press is a self-inflicted paradox wound the Reality Log will carry for the rest of the arc.

Encounters & Beats

Exploration — reading the crowd (the approach).

  • Arrival hours ahead of the event; the cover holds but the hollow is already filling and the weather is against everyone.
  • Triangulate the source: canvass where witnesses later say the sun "moved," watch the children's line of sight, and read the entrainment's geometry back to a point on the ridge.
  • Find the tell at the farmhouse — period-inappropriate hardware, an orichalcum signature, or the physiological wreckage of an asset pushed past its limits.
  • Contend with the weather and the timing: the "miracle" is anticipated for midday, and the team has one morning to reach the ridge before the crowd's attention locks skyward.

Puzzle / Social — working 70,000 people.

  • The crowd is the puzzle: move a team through a packed, praying, panicking hollow without becoming the story. Manage clergy, the skeptical Republican press, and hostile secular officials who each want a different version of events.
  • The children are a live wire — sympathetic, watched by everyone, and leaking precognition. Approaching them without spooking handlers, family, or the Church is its own social encounter.

Combat / Climax — the Ridge Farmhouse.

  • The source, per GM's chosen truth: an EPOCH broadcast rig with a small operator team (a field test of mass-broadcast Mark tech, or a deliberate op to sway a devout, war-shaken nation at a pivot), or a single overloaded psi asset the team must subdue before it burns out or the emitter cooks the crowd's perception a second time.
  • The tactical problem is timing and noise: the fight must end before the broadcast peaks again, and must stay invisible from the hollow below. Muzzle flash on the ridge is 70,000 new witnesses.
  • If EPOCH-run, expect a cheap drop-extraction the moment the op is blown — the same "cut them loose and they fall up the well" hazard that makes EPOCH so hard to hold in their home era.

Open Design Questions

  • Who is the source? (a) An EPOCH operation — mass-broadcast Mark field test, or a deliberate belle-époque push on a devout wartime nation; (b) an ATA attempt to contain a malfunctioning, leaking psi asset that has slipped its handlers; or (c) an uncontrolled gradient/shard discharge with no operator behind it at all. Each reframes the climax from firefight to rescue to disaster-response. Recommend seeding clues for all three and committing late.
  • The children. Extract them, brief-and-leave them, or simply let history run? Their "secrets" are precog leakage — how much of the real future did three shepherd children just download, and does either agency want it?
  • Paradox math. This is the campaign's ceiling for public anomaly. How large a Timeline Attention spike does 13 October 1917 already carry as canon fact, and how much slack does that leave the team before they tip it into EPOCH/investigator attention?
  • Sublimation risk. If a child is that strong a natural psi, are they a sublimation target — is the "miracle" partly a recruiting beacon?
  • WWI as cover. How hard does the team lean on a continent already drowning in the inexplicable? Does the war make sanitization easier (noise) or harder (everyone is looking for signs)?
  • Map footprint. One vast crowd tile with a linked ridge inset, or the hollow rendered in swappable sections (fog-of-war-by-tile) as the team pushes through the press toward the source?
