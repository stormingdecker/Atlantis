Artifacts

Legendary objects — the player-facing reward layer for the loot economy. Most mythic artifacts in human tradition are agency-tech reframed by mundane culture.

The Principle

Throughout history, both agencies have used technology that mundane observers couldn't categorize. When an EPOCH operative drops a Mark VI infused weapon in 5th-century Britain, it doesn't get logged as "lost equipment" — it gets logged as Excalibur, and a thousand years of cultural accretion build a mythology around it.

This is the artifact reframing principle: most legendary objects in human mythology are real, agency-produced, and recoverable. Their mythic properties are usually a folkloric description of their actual mechanical effect, distorted by cultural transmission.

For how these objects are built — the materials-and-power craft behind the "magic," the unhideable shard-glow, and how luminous artifacts function as persistent player upgrades — see Anachrotech.md.

Why Artifacts Matter for Play

Artifacts give the loot economy (Temporal-Gradient.md, drop-transit profile) something to be about. Specifically:

  1. Persistent player rewards. A captured artifact stays with the team across sessions. Unlike consumable resources (fuel, charges, intel), an artifact is a trophy the player can name.
  2. Mechanical upgrades. Most recovered artifacts give the team a capability they didn't have before — better infusion-mark weapons, psi-resistant materials, regenerative kit, knowledge access.
  3. Macro-progression layer. Recovered EPOCH artifacts feed ATA's foundry mark (per Orichalcum.md). Each artifact recovered is a real step in the campaign's tech-curve catch-up.
  4. Faction politics. Major artifacts attract attention. Both agencies' leadership want them. Internal ATA factions may also want them. Players choose what to report and what to keep quiet.
  5. Story payoff. Discovering that the Spear of Destiny is real, and is sitting in the Hofburg treasury in Vienna right now, is a player moment no system mechanic produces on its own.

Categories

Artifacts fall into four operational types:

1. Mark-Leak Tech

EPOCH or future-ATA equipment that ended up in a historical era it shouldn't have. Usually because of a failed extraction, a deliberate political move, or an embed's death-in-the-line.

  • Examples: Excalibur, the Spear of Destiny, certain Holy Relics with "miraculous" properties.
  • Mechanical effect: Direct upgrade to a team's gear capability. An EPOCH Mark VI sword in ATA hands is the best blade in their armory.
  • Risk: Carrying recognizable EPOCH tech in EPOCH-active eras is a flag. Trained EPOCH operatives can identify their own missing equipment.

2. Refined Fuel & Orichalcum Artifacts

Objects that are themselves meteor-derived material, recovered from ancient cultures that didn't know what they had.

  • Examples: The Philosopher's Stone, certain "sacred" relics with persistent radiance, lost Atlantean orichalcum tablets.
  • Mechanical effect: Resupply ATA's foundry. The single most valuable category — they directly extend the agency's operational range and infusion-mark capability.
  • Risk: Same as raw fuel. Catastrophic if mishandled, attractive to every interested party.

3. Knowledge Artifacts

Documents, scrolls, books, encrypted media containing information ATA wants and doesn't have.

  • Examples: Lost scrolls of the Library of Alexandria, Newton's missing alchemy notebooks, Atlantean technical tablets, Cleopatra's personal archive.
  • Mechanical effect: Unlock specific research, intel about EPOCH operations, location of other artifacts, foundry technique recovery.
  • Risk: Knowledge isn't always safe. Some recovered texts are themselves the threat — survivable only by infused readers, or encoding information ATA central command would rather players not have.

4. Biological Artifacts

Preserved samples from myth-engineered assets, captured EPOCH operatives, or unique genetic lineages.

  • Examples: Sphinx remains, Anubis-line tissue samples, Helen's surviving descendants, the embalmed body of an EPOCH operative recovered from a tomb.
  • Mechanical effect: Feed ATA's Bio-Anomaly Division. Each sample accelerates ATA's understanding of EPOCH biotech by years.
  • Risk: Custody disputes. A living descendant is a person with rights, not a research subject — but ATA central command's ethics committee doesn't always win those debates.

This shapes how the GM tunes mission rewards. A campaign that's struggling with foundry-mark pacing should drop a refined-fuel artifact. A campaign that needs an EPOCH-doctrine reveal should drop a knowledge artifact. A campaign that needs a moral-dilemma session should drop a biological artifact.

Recovery Mission Profile

Artifact recovery missions tend to share structural traits:

  1. The artifact is somewhere in the historical record — the team needs to research first, then insert.
  2. The era is usually a hot zone when the artifact is at its most accessible — meaning EPOCH (or another party) is also aware of it.
  3. The artifact is in someone's hands — a king, a priest, a museum, a tomb. Acquisition requires either negotiation, theft, ritual fulfillment, or careful timing of a transition (battle, death, ceremony).
  4. Extraction is via drop transit — heavy loot is cheap to bring home, but only once the team is at their extraction point with the artifact in hand.
  5. Provenance matters — the team often needs to leave a cover artifact in place so the historical record doesn't show the original simply vanishing. A well-aged forgery (sometimes infused-but-inert, sometimes mundane) is standard issue for these ops.

Cover Forgeries

Standard ATA recovery doctrine: never just take the artifact. Replace it with something the local culture will accept as the original.

  • Period-accurate forgery for low-value or low-attention items. Mundane craft, no infusion.
  • Inert-infused replica for items expected to "do something" — looks like the real thing, includes trace orichalcum for that faint mythic glow, but no functional payload. Most "holy relics" venerated in medieval Europe are this category, planted by ATA after recovering originals centuries earlier.
  • Functional decoy for the highest-stakes recoveries — actually does something, but a different something than the original. Useful when the artifact's historical effect must continue (a recovered Spear of Destiny "still" rallies an army because the decoy carries a basic morale-boost psi loop).

Open Questions

  • Does ATA maintain a museum/vault of recovered artifacts? Where? Players want to visit eventually.
  • What happens when an EPOCH recovery team is also in the era hunting the same artifact? Race-condition missions are obvious. Negotiation missions are more interesting.
  • Are there artifacts ATA central command has refused to recover for political reasons? (E.g., "the Holy Grail is in the Vatican, leave it there, the diplomatic cost is too high.")
  • What's the rule for player-personal artifacts? Can a PC carry a recovered EPOCH blade as their signature weapon, or does central command requisition everything?
  • Are there cursed artifacts in the proper sense — recovered items that turned out to be EPOCH traps, designed to be found and bring something bad home?
