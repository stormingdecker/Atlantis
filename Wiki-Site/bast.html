Bast

The team's infiltration specialist. Egyptian repatriation activist turned professional thief turned ATA agent. Chief bailed her out of a London prosecution. She has not forgiven him for the price.

Cover Identity

A late-twenties Egyptian woman, currently living abroad on what appears to her old social network to be a private consulting career. The public-record version of her: a Cairo-born, British-educated archaeologist who turned to "art recovery work" after graduate school, was implicated in a London museum theft, and quietly disappeared from public view after the charges were dismissed in a manner that did not invite scrutiny.

The dismissal was a political-financial favor of substantial size. Her old activist colleagues assume she paid for it with information; nobody is sure exactly what information. The truth — that Chief paid in cash and the cash trail is buried under enough layers of agency cover that no investigator will ever find it — is known only to her, Chief, and a handful of senior leadership.

Her real name is in British court records (the trial that almost happened) and Egyptian birth/educational records (which she did not get to choose to redact). ATA has worked to make the public version of her life trail off into nothing remarkable after the dismissal — she lives quietly, works contracts, has no notable address, has no notable social presence. The previous her is findable; the current her is invisible.

True Identity

Within ATA she is Bast — the Egyptian cat goddess, deliberately chosen as a codename that signals her origin without revealing it, and that doubles as the kind of callsign professional thieves give themselves (cat burglar, cat-quiet, cat-precise). She accepted the name with quiet amusement; she had picked similar working names for herself across her career, and Bast was already in her personal rotation.

She is approximately 27 at the start of the campaign. Born in Cairo to a middle-class family — her father an engineer, her mother a translator. She has two younger siblings (a sister doing a PhD in linguistics in Germany; a brother working in tourism in Hurghada). Her family knows she "got into trouble in London" and is "lying low" — they do not know she has joined a covert agency, and the agency has worked to keep her family unaware.

Backstory

Grew up in Cairo. Attended the American University there, then went to University College London for a master's in archaeological heritage. Her thesis was on colonial-era artifact removal — the systematic transfer of cultural objects from Egypt to European museums during the 19th and early 20th centuries. The Rosetta Stone, the Nefertari papyri, the Amarna princess statues, the Beit el-Wali reliefs, the Nimrud ivories partially Egyptian, hundreds of mummies, thousands of smaller objects. The academic version of her thesis was diplomatic. The lived version of her thesis was a slow radicalization.

She joined a loose network of activists — a mix of academics, art-recovery lawyers, and a smaller hardcore wing willing to take action — that pursued repatriation through multiple channels. Most of the network worked through legal and diplomatic means. Bast started there. Within two years she was working with the hardcore wing.

The work was real heists. Not amateur kid-stuff: professional crews who studied targets for months, who employed disguises and forgeries and counter-surveillance and good technical lockpicking. Their targets were specific objects with documented removal-from-Egypt provenance. Their goal was to return the objects to Egypt — through smuggling routes the group had built over years. They did not steal for money. They stole for restitution.

She participated in approximately eight successful operations between 2021 and 2024. The objects mostly returned to Egypt through her network's channels; some were quietly handed over to Egyptian government cultural authorities who pretended they had been "recovered." She was good at the work. She liked it. She believed in it.

The ninth operation went wrong.

A coordinated multi-team heist on a smaller London museum — the team meant to hit three rooms simultaneously and exfiltrate with eight named objects. An inside informant (the group later determined: a Western intelligence asset planted to watch for exactly this kind of activity) tipped off security. Two of the three teams escaped clean. Bast's team did not. She was caught with one object in her possession (a small stone funerary statuette dated to the 18th Dynasty) and arrested.

The British charges were serious. Aggravated theft, conspiracy, cultural-property offenses. She was looking at 5–8 years.

She did not see Chief coming. He visited her in custody — a polite older American man in a quiet suit, who introduced himself as a lawyer working on her behalf via "a sponsor who would prefer to remain anonymous." He explained the offer in twenty minutes:

The charges will be dropped. The arrest will be expunged. You will leave the UK within 72 hours of release. You will work for an organization I represent. The work will use your skills. You will not be required to do anything you find ethically intolerable. You will sign a contract that you will not understand the full meaning of until later. You may walk out of this cell and into a free life in approximately three days, or you may serve the next six years here. Your choice.

She asked who the sponsor was. He told her he could not say. She asked what the work would be. He told her it would be infiltration. She asked why her. He told her he had been watching her for two years and her skills were specifically what his organization needed.

She signed. She was released within 72 hours. The dismissal was effected through bribes routed across three jurisdictions; the paperwork was clean enough that no British investigator ever found the inconsistency. Chief is good at this.

She left for what she was told was a training facility in Eastern Europe (the staging site outside Bratislava). She has been there approximately six months when Arc 00 — The Cryo Train begins.

Her Relationship with the Agency

She has not fully bought in. She joined to avoid prison. She finds her teammates likable; she finds the work interesting; she has not yet been told the deeper truth about ATA (the prologue is when she learns it).

But her primary loyalties remain to the repatriation cause she was working for before her arrest. Some of the artifacts she helped return to Egypt are now in Cairo museums. She follows their stories from afar. She still believes the work was right. She has not informed her old network that she's alive and working; she's been told (by Chief, gently but firmly) that contacting them would expose her cover and possibly endanger them.

When she eventually learns that ATA itself runs artifact recovery operations — moving real artifacts through history, sometimes taking them from cultures of origin and storing them at Atlantis — the moral friction will be substantial. She may push back on operations she perceives as colonial-pattern-repeating. The campaign should leave room for this tension. She is not a passive employee; she will argue.

This is, in fact, why Chief recruited her specifically. He wants someone in the agency with a strong moral compass about cultural property. He believes the agency needs internal critics on this specific issue. He has not told her this yet.

Capabilities

  • Lockpicking and bypass. Professional-grade. Mechanical locks, electronic locks, biometric workarounds (with the right tools). She can defeat museum-grade security in a working session; given time, she can defeat almost anything below sovereign-grade.
  • Climbing and physical infiltration. Slim, agile, exceptional balance. Free-running and climbing experience from her Cairo youth (she grew up in older neighborhoods where rooftop traversal was a normal way to get around). Comfortable with ropes, harnesses, vertical access.
  • Stealth and surveillance. Trained by her old network in patient observation, target study, route-planning. She can sit still for hours. She notices things other people don't.
  • Languages. Arabic (native Egyptian dialect), English (fluent, slight accent), French (intermediate from school), some Italian (picked up from contacts in the art-recovery world), basic German.
  • Cultural-object expertise. Authentication, provenance research, period identification. She can look at an artifact and tell you within a generation when it was made, where, by whom or by what cultural context. This is valuable for ATA's artifact-recovery missions.
  • Forgery awareness. She knows what forgeries look like — she has worked with forgers extensively for the cover-replacement operations her old network ran. She can usually spot a high-quality forgery within a few minutes; she can sometimes plan one.
  • Networks. She still has some old contacts. Some are dormant. Some would help her if she reached out. She has chosen not to, but the relationships exist.

After infusion (post-prologue), she gains standard agent capabilities: agent-sense signature, memory privilege, slow-aging. Her existing physical skills combine well with infused durability; she becomes a very hard target to catch.

Operational Role

  • Infiltration lead. When the team needs to get into something, Bast plans the route and executes the entry. She is the team's primary "get us in" specialist.
  • Cultural-property consultant. When the team encounters an artifact in the field, Bast is the one who tells them what it is, what era it's from, whether it's significant, whether it's a forgery, and whether it should or shouldn't be removed.
  • Stealth scout. When the team needs to survey a location before committing, Bast is the one who goes ahead alone, observes, and reports back.
  • Skeptic / moral voice. She is the team member most likely to push back on a morally questionable mission. The other PCs and ATA leadership accept this; it's part of why she is on the team.

What She Knows / Refuses to Discuss

  • Her old network's full membership. She knows names. She will not share them with the agency. Some of those people are still operating. She does not want her colleagues' work disrupted by ATA's interests.
  • Her family's existence and location. Known to senior leadership (Chief, the briefing officer who handled her recruitment). Not known to the rest of the team. She does not discuss her family.
  • The truth of the heist that got her caught. She has not told the team that she suspects the inside informant was Western intelligence. She is not entirely sure. She does not know which agency. The unresolved question is part of what makes her cautious about ATA itself.
  • What she refuses to discuss with the agency. Whether she is fully committed. Whether she would defect under sufficient provocation. These are private answers she has not finalized for herself.

Tells and Vulnerabilities

  • Arabic accent. Recognizable. Her English is excellent but her vowels are Egyptian. Other native English speakers occasionally remark on the slight cadence. Egyptians in any setting recognize her on first hearing.
  • Hyper-awareness of cultural objects. She notices artifacts in every space she enters. If someone is watching her closely, the focus of her attention is a tell — she will look at the museum exhibit in the corner before she looks at the people in the room.
  • Her old network as leverage. EPOCH (or anyone) who learned about her past colleagues and threatened them could pressure her significantly. She cares about those people.
  • Family pressure. Same as Major's — her family in Egypt knows nothing and is vulnerable to discovery. ATA actively protects her family's information.
  • Moral friction with the agency's artifact operations. If the campaign features ATA doing things she perceives as colonial-pattern-repeating, she may push back hard enough to threaten her own operational reliability. This is intended as a campaign-long roleplay tension, not a defect.
  • The British court records. Her arrest, dismissal, and the gap in her public-record activity are findable by a competent investigator. EPOCH counter-intelligence with British resources could find her. The dismissal itself is a small flag — a too-clean disappearance from prosecution attracts attention from people who know to look.

Encounter Notes (For Players)

Bast is competent and quietly cynical in early sessions. She knows she's been bailed out of prison and put into a job. She has not yet realized what kind of job it is. She does her work professionally; she does not yet trust the agency.

She is good with people. She reads them quickly, often correctly. She is friendly with the rest of the team but reserves judgment. She has Major's respect early (one professional acknowledging another); she has Doc's affection (Doc finds her interesting, smart, and a little wounded); she does not yet have her own deep social ties at the agency.

When the prologue's reveal lands — that this is a time-travel agency at war with a future agency over the shape of human history — her first reaction is recalibration. She sits with it. She does not immediately commit. She watches how the team handles it. Then she asks a sharp question: "Does this agency move artifacts through history? Have you moved artifacts? Which artifacts? Who decides?"

The answers she gets, and how she reacts to them, are major character beats for her early-campaign arc.

If a player wants their Bast to be more openly committed to ATA, that's a valid roleplay choice; the cynical-skeptic default is just where her character starts. Her moral arc across the campaign — from "tolerating this job because the alternative was prison" to "actively believing in this specific version of what ATA does, with caveats" — is rich material.

Pronouns and Presentation

Bast presents as female. Pronouns: she/her. Her sexuality is not specified by canon; player-chosen. Her cultural identity (Egyptian, Cairene, Arab) is core to who she is and should not be flattened for the team's convenience.

Open Questions

  • Her real name. Per the aliases convention, never revealed in the campaign. The agency archives have it; the players' characters do not need to know it.
  • The exact museum she was caught at. Suggested: a smaller London museum rather than the British Museum itself (which would be too high-profile for a working heist crew to realistically target). The Petrie Museum (UCL), the Horniman Museum, or a private collection are all plausible.
  • The informant who tipped off her arrest. GM call. Western intelligence (MI5, CIA, or a coordinated Western consortium) is the canonical suspicion. Could be developed into a recurring antagonist as a side-thread.
  • Her old network's current status. Worth GM development. Some of those people are still operating. Mid-campaign, an ATA mission could intersect with them. How does Bast handle that?
  • Her relationship with Chief. Complicated. He saved her from prison; he also bought her. She is grateful and resentful at the same time. The relationship has the texture of senior officer to recruit who was conscripted rather than chosen — operationally functional but emotionally unresolved.
  • The repatriation movement in the campaign world. Does ATA take a position on repatriation? Senior leadership canonically has views (probably mixed — some support, some pragmatic skepticism); the agency's overall position is "we don't engage with modern political debates about cultural property unless they intersect operationally." Bast's presence will force the agency to engage. This is a campaign-shaping consequence Chief anticipated when he recruited her.
  • Late-campaign role. As the team's infiltration specialist, Bast remains operationally crucial throughout. Her late-game development is probably moral rather than tactical — what she's chosen to commit to, what compromises she's made, what she's done that she'd never have done before. She might become a senior advocate within the agency for a particular ethical framework, or she might leave the agency on her own terms. Worth thinking about by mid-campaign.
