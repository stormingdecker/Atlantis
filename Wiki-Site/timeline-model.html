The Timeline Model

How time, change, and consequence work in this setting.

Single Timeline, Mutable

There is one timeline. History happens once. When agents in the past alter events, the present and future update accordingly — no branching, no parallel worlds, no "we can just go fix it in a different timeline." Every intervention is an irrevocable edit to the only reality that exists.

This is the engine that makes the Cold War analogy work: ATA and EPOCH aren't fighting over many possible Earths. They are fighting over the one history humanity will have.

Localized Memory Privilege

The defining constraint of the setting:

Only people, objects, and records in close proximity to The Origin (or fueled by meteor radiance) retain memory of prior states of the timeline. Everyone else's memories, documents, photos, and brain chemistry simply update when history is edited.

Practical consequences:

  • Mundane humanity never notices change. When history shifts, civilians remember the new version as if it had always been true. Wikipedia, newspapers, libraries — all retroactively consistent.
  • The agencies are the only observers. ATA and EPOCH personnel, the Atlantean crater facility, the meteor itself, and small amounts of refined fuel are temporally insulated. They remember what was lost.
  • Long-service agents accumulate the most "old memories." Field operatives who spend years near temporal fuel develop a layered memory of timeline states. New recruits start with normal memory and only acquire privilege over time.
  • Players have personal stakes. An agent who returns from a Bronze Age mission to find their grandmother edited out of existence has a real emotional cost. No one else remembers her. There is no fix except more intervention.

Why Both Agencies Exist

The Localized Memory Privilege is the justification for institutional time travel:

  • ATA exists because they alone perceive history as mutable. Without observers who remember the previous version, the rest of humanity simply lives in whatever timeline currently exists. ATA frames itself as "the only ones who can tell what's been lost."
  • EPOCH exists symmetrically. Their independent fuel cache and Chronograph mean they perceive the same mutations. They simply disagree with ATA about which timeline humanity should converge toward.
  • Civilians cannot be told. Even if you tried to tell a non-agency person the timeline had changed, their memory would not contain the old version. They would have nothing to compare against. The Secret enforces itself through cognitive structure.

MAD: Mutually Assured Reality-Collapse

Why is the agency war fought as shadow ops instead of all-out intervention?

If either agency unleashed everything — flooding history with simultaneous high-magnitude interventions — the timeline would suffer reality-fracture: cascading contradictions, regions of indeterminate history, possibly catastrophic feedback through the Chronograph itself. The Origin meteor's behavior under such load is not modeled and not safe to test.

So both agencies fight a deliberately limited war:

  • Small leverage-point interventions only.
  • Tacit norms against attacking the rival's fuel infrastructure (a knockout blow would break the balance, but might also break the timeline).
  • Tacit norms against operating in the same era simultaneously without warning.
  • A small population of "shared agents" who deconflict joint hot zones.

Players will violate or be tempted to violate these norms throughout the campaign. The MAD framework gives every transgression real weight.

Mutual Bootstrap Survival

Beneath MAD lies a deeper, more personal stake: each agency is fighting for its own existence and the survival of the civilization it values.

The conflict isn't tribal (East vs. West) or moral (good vs. evil). It is philosophical — two coherent visions of how a planetary civilization should be organized, each defended by people who genuinely believe their vision is the working one.

  • ATA exists only because the historical chain produced modern democratic institutional capacity — free elections, free press, protected dissent, rule of law, political pluralism, civil society — as a globally distributed achievement. ATA preserves the democratic chain: Japan, India, South Korea, Taiwan, post-Soviet Eastern Europe, the Americas, and increasingly Africa, as well as Western Europe and North America. If EPOCH succeeds in disrupting that chain, the conditions that produced ATA never form, and every ATA agent ceases to have existed.
  • EPOCH exists only because the historical chain produced the 2200s planetary hegemony — a coordinated bloc that, by its own internal measures, solved the catastrophes pluralist systems failed to manage: climate collapse, recurring pandemics, runaway social fragmentation, regional wars. See ../Factions/EPOCH.md. If ATA succeeds in disrupting that chain, EPOCH is never created. Every EPOCH agent ceases to have been born. The 2200s becomes a different century, one without them.

Either side losing decisively is not just "the other side wins history." It is the losing side never having existed at all, and the civilization that side values never having emerged. This is the deepest motivating floor of the conflict and the reason both sides fight with such institutional desperation.

The Philosophical Conflict

The campaign's central moral architecture: freedom-as-chaos vs. coordination-as-care.

ATA holds that pluralism, dissent, and political mess are the price of freedom and worth paying. Democracy is slow, inefficient, and prone to catastrophic failure modes — and that is preferable to the alternative, because the alternative is the suppression of human political agency. The 20th and 21st centuries' failures are tragedies, but the response to those failures should not be the abolition of the freedom that allowed them.

EPOCH holds that pluralism's failures were so catastrophic that coordinated governance is a moral upgrade, not a moral compromise. Their hegemony solved the problems democracy couldn't, and it did so by suppressing the political mess that made those problems possible. From their perspective, ATA's defense of "freedom" is the defense of a system that killed billions across the 20th and 21st centuries through its inability to coordinate.

Both positions have internal logic. Neither is straightforwardly wrong. The campaign's emotional payoff comes from letting players occupy ATA's side wholeheartedly while gradually revealing that EPOCH agents are not stupid, not evil, and not even cruel — they are just fighting for a different vision of what humanity should become.

The late-campaign reveal that EPOCH is a future agency staffed by people whose families exist in a society they consider good is the campaign's deepest moral provocation. Some PCs may begin to wrestle with whether democratic institutional capacity is actually worth the cost of erasing 2200s stability, prosperity, and the families that exist within it.

No right answer is supplied. That is the point.

What This Means For Play

  • The campaign rewards thinking like an intelligence agency: research, surveillance, precision interventions, counter-intelligence.
  • "Just kill EPOCH HQ in 1962" is rejected by the setting's physics, not just by GM fiat.
  • Every successful mission permanently changes the timeline — which means the campaign has persistent state the GM tracks (see Reality-Log.md).
  • Failure has cost. Lost lives, lost relationships, lost canonical history. Players will feel it.

How Change Propagates: The Mechanical Levers

Four interlocking mechanisms drive how interventions ripple through history. Detailed rules will mature as we build out the system, but the levers are:

  1. Historical Inertia. Every targetable event has a difficulty rating — how much "weight" must be applied to shift it. Trivial events: Inertia 1–3. Major events (a famous battle's outcome): Inertia 8–12. Foundational events (Caesar's assassination): Inertia 15+. Higher-inertia events require multiple coordinated missions across eras to genuinely change.
  2. Influence Tracks per Era. Each major historical era has a tracked ATA-vs-EPOCH influence balance. The dominant agency in an era subtly biases which downstream events propagate. Visible as a meta-progress bar for players.
  3. Cascade Rolls. When an intervention concludes, the GM rolls (3d6, naturally) to determine how far the ripple travels. Crits cascade further than intended. Aftershocks emerge in eras the agency wasn't targeting.
  4. The Chronograph. The agencies' shared early-warning system. Detects ripples after they form, providing missions, but also revealing the rival's recent moves. See Chronograph.md.
