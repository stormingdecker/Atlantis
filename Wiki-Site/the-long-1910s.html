The Long 1910s — the Belle-Époque Surge

Status: Draft (arc concept). A meta-arc that threads the campaign's early-20th-century missions into one story: Reichenbach Falls, 1891, Tunguska, 1908, the Yusupov Cellar, 1916, and Fátima, 1917 — hung on a single observation (see the Missions Overview and the Historical Anomalies seed catalog).

The Pattern (why this era)

Plot ATA's incident reports against the calendar and something jumps out: EPOCH activity spikes in the early 20th century. Anomalies cluster in the decades around 1900–1920 far more densely than the quiet centuries of the Renaissance or antiquity. This is not observation bias — it's structural.

As history climbs toward EPOCH's future home, EPOCH can reach it more cheaply. The Kunlun anchor sits in the ~2200s; the early 20th century is near that anchor on the temporal gradient, a cheap drop rather than an expensive deep-past reach (see The Kunlun Shard — the 1900s–2200s are explicitly EPOCH's home turf). ATA has the mirror-image problem: Mediterranean- and Bronze-Age-dense, it is transit-stretched reaching forward into this window, operating at the far, costly end of its own gradient.

The result is a hinge era, weakly held by ATA, where EPOCH pushes hard — close enough to the future that the enemy operates almost at home, far enough forward from the Origin that ATA arrives tired, thin, and outnumbered. The "Long 1910s" is the name for that pressure front: the belle-époque decades when the future leans back into the past and ATA has to decide whether to contest it.

The Cluster (the evidence)

A dated line of incidents, each with its in-setting reframe and, where one exists, its mission page:

  • 1859 — the Carrington Event (precursor). The largest recorded geomagnetic storm; telegraph systems failed and auroras were seen near the equator. Reframe: an early energy signature of the coming surge — the first gradient disturbance strong enough to leave a mark in the mundane record, decades before the cluster proper. A GM's foreshadowing beat, not yet a mission.
  • 1900 — the Antikythera mechanism surfaces. Sponge divers recover the wreck off Antikythera, bringing a lost ATA device into the light. Reframe: the surge's opening move is ATA's own materiel resurfacing at the worst possible time and place — see Artifacts/Candidates.md. The associated cover-shaping op (locking in the "ancient Greek calculator" reading) is a natural first mission.
  • 1908 — Tunguska shard-fall. A trajectory-less impact flattens Siberian taiga — the campaign's textbook "arrived from nowhere" shard-fall. See Tunguska, 1908. Whose operation went wrong is the open hook the arc can answer.
  • 1912–13 — connective beat (GM's choice). An optional bridging incident to keep the drumbeat steady between Tunguska and the war years — e.g., a high-profile disaster or "hoax" retro-fit as an EPOCH op, or a quieter anchor-contest the players only recognize in hindsight. Left deliberately open.
  • 1916 — the Rasputin sublimation. EPOCH converts a target into an operator in a Petrograd cellar. See the Yusupov Cellar, 1916 and Rasputin. The surge's most personal, most legible EPOCH win.
  • 1917 — the "Miracle of the Sun," Fátima (mission). On 13 October 1917 an estimated ~70,000 people gathered at Fátima, Portugal, reported the sun "dancing," pulsing, and changing color for several minutes before an anticipated apparition. Reframe: a mass-observed Mark / psi broadcast event — an emitter or a glamour-psi asset run at full power over a crowd, producing shared perceptual entrainment on a scale nothing else in the cluster matches (see Psionics; thematic sibling to the Dancing Plague of 1518). Note that WWI runs as cover for the whole late cluster: a continent already drowning in the inexplicable makes large ops disappear into the noise.

Arc Shape

A proposed four-mission progression through the window — from noticing the pattern to contesting it — reusing the existing 1891/1908/1916 missions plus the Fátima seed:

  1. Notice — 1908, Tunguska. Start with the loudest anomaly. The team investigates a shard-fall and comes away with a Chronograph fingerprint they can't place — the first hint that this era is saturated.
  2. Confirm — 1900, Antikythera / 1891, Reichenbach. Two feeler missions that turn a hunch into a thesis: ATA materiel resurfacing (Antikythera cover-op) and a re-visit to Reichenbach that reframes the 1891 extraction site as the surge's earliest edge. Output: the team names the Long 1910s and understands why the era is thin for ATA.
  3. Escalate — 1916, Yusupov. The pattern gets a face. EPOCH is not just probing — it's building assets here (the Rasputin sublimation). The team interdicts, witnesses, or documents, and learns the surge has an objective, not just momentum.
  4. Confront — 1917, Fátima. The climax the arc has been aiming at: EPOCH runs a broadcast in front of 70,000 witnesses under wartime cover. The team must find and shut down the source without exposure, in EPOCH's strongest era, at the thin end of their own reach — the belle-époque surge cresting, and ATA contesting it on the enemy's home ground.

Scalable to three missions by folding step 2 into step 1, or expanded with the 1912–13 connective beat as a fifth.

Open Design Questions

  • Does the surge have a single objective, or is it opportunism? Is EPOCH pursuing one goal across 1900–1917 (an asset, an anchor network, a specific unwind) that the arc reveals, or just exploiting a cheap era while it can?
  • How explicit is the gradient reasoning to players? Do agents work out why the era is EPOCH-heavy in-fiction, or is that GM-side framing they only ever feel as "everything here is harder"?
  • The 1912–13 slot. Fill it, or leave the gap as evidence the surge itself has rhythm? A named incident tightens the drumbeat; an absence can be its own clue.
  • Fátima's mission page is now drafted — Fátima, 1917 (mass-broadcast containment among a huge crowd). Open: how firmly to fix the source as canon (EPOCH op vs leaking ATA asset vs uncontrolled discharge) versus leaving it a per-table GM choice.
  • Carrington as more than flavor. Does the 1859 signature ever become playable (a precursor mission), or stay a foreshadowing note in a briefing?
  • Reusing missions across arcs. Reichenbach, Tunguska, and Yusupov all predate this arc. If a table plays them standalone first, does the Long 1910s re-contextualize them on replay, or should the arc claim them from the start?
