Mission Design

How sessions translate into rewards, consequences, and progress.

The Core Constraint

Every mission must produce some meaningful outcome — intel, XP, faction reputation, or Reality Log delta. No session ever ends with players feeling their time was wasted, even if the mission "failed" or was a decoy.

This is the single most important design rule for mission generation. It directly shapes how decoys, spoofing, and failure modes are constructed.

What Every Mission Produces (Minimum)

Regardless of outcome, every mission yields at least:

  1. Intel. A Reality Log entry — at minimum the ripple's measured properties and the team's observations. Even a "failed" mission generates intel about the opposition.
  2. XP. Character improvement points awarded by the GM based on session duration, in-character problem solving, and roleplay — not solely on mission success.
  3. Faction reputation. Often unspoken, accumulated across sessions. ATA leadership notices reliability, creativity, discretion. Sub-factions within ATA may notice things that overall command doesn't.
  4. Personal consequences for at least one PC. Even small ones — a contact made, a relationship deepened or strained, a piece of personal history altered by the Reality Log update.

Mission Outcome Tiers

A mission's quality of outcome determines the reward scale, not just success/failure.

OutcomeDescriptionReward Profile
Decisive WinMission objective achieved with elegance — minimal aftershocks, target ripple fully resolved, possibly rival assets capturedFull XP, major intel, faction-reputation bump, often Reality Log "preservation" entry
Win with CostObjective achieved but with collateral — wrong era affected, civilians noticed, an asset burned, an agent injuredFull XP, modest intel, neutral faction reputation, Reality Log entry with footnoted complications
PartialObjective partially achieved — ripple damped but not eliminated, opposition partially driven off2/3 XP, modest intel, mixed faction reputation, Reality Log "pending" entry that may demand follow-up missions
Tactical Defeat, Strategic WinMission "failed" but the team learned more about EPOCH operations than the original objective was worth2/3 XP, major intel (highest of any tier), mixed reputation, Reality Log "exposed" entry on EPOCH activity
Failure with SurvivalObjective not achieved, team extracted intact1/2 XP, modest intel, faction reputation hit, Reality Log entry on the new (altered) reality
DisasterObjective not achieved, team losses, ripple cascades unexpectedly1/3 XP, sometimes major intel (post-mortem analysis), faction reputation hit, Reality Log entry showing major timeline drift

Note that Tactical Defeat, Strategic Win is a deliberately attractive outcome. It exists to teach players that failing the stated objective is not the worst case — learning nothing is the worst case. This unlocks creative play.

Decoy Mission Outcomes

Decoys are a specific subclass that need their own treatment, because the user has confirmed agencies can spoof ripples — and the constraint is that decoys must not feel like rug-pulls.

Detection Before Insertion

If the team's Reality Analysts (or psi-augmented analysis) detect a spoofed ripple before the team deploys:

OutcomeReward Profile
Decoy detected, mission scrubbedModest XP for analysis. Significant intel ("EPOCH spent fuel on this; what were they trying to distract us from?"). Investigation hook for the next session — where was the real op?

This is a satisfying outcome because it opens a new investigation rather than closing the session abruptly. The decoy detection IS the mission's intel payoff.

Detection Mid-Mission

If the team detects the decoy after insertion but before fully committing:

ChoiceReward Profile
Extract cleanModest XP, modest intel (confirmed it was a decoy), no faction risk, but no positive accumulation either
Pivot to investigate the trapHigh variance — usually high payoff (the trap reveals the rival's real intent, methods, identity of operatives), but high risk
Attack the trapHighest variance — best possible payoff (potentially capture rival agents and turn the trap inside-out) or worst (walk into prepared opposition)

Critically: even Extract clean yields something. It is never zero.

No Detection — Trap Sprung

If the team walks into a trap without detecting it:

OutcomeReward Profile
Trap sprung, team survivesMajor intel from post-mortem analysis (the trap REVEALS the rival's intent, the operational signature, sometimes identities). Faction reputation cost. High XP from the brutal lesson.
Trap sprung, team lossesAll of the above, plus a PC down. The campaign now has a personal stake in counter-intelligence — that PC's replacement, or the survivors' grief, becomes a long-arc thread

The point is that even being trapped teaches the team something. Spoofing has a real intelligence return — the team becomes better at detecting future spoofs after each one.

Why Spoofing Costs the Rival, Too

A reminder that should be drilled into players: every decoy EPOCH plants burns real EPOCH fuel. The fuel is finite. Every spoof is a charge they cannot spend on a real operation. So even being decoyed has a strategic upside — EPOCH is spending down its reserve.

ATA central command tracks "estimated EPOCH fuel burn" as a campaign-wide variable. Players can see it. When EPOCH's reserve runs low, the rival becomes more cautious, more selective — and players have measurably won something by absorbing decoys.

Session Structure

A standard session arc that supports all of this:

  1. Briefing (5–10 min real-time). Central command presents the Chronograph reading. Ripple properties. Suspected origin. Mission type and parameters. Open Q&A.
  2. Preparation (5–10 min). The team chooses approach, equipment, contacts, era-appropriate cover. This is also when Reality Analysts (PC or NPC) can flag spoofing suspicions.
  3. Insertion and Operation (bulk of session). The actual mission play.
  4. Extraction and Debrief (10–15 min). Return to modern day. GM updates the Reality Log live with the team. Personal deltas for each PC are noted. XP awarded.
  5. Cliffhanger (optional). A new Chronograph reading at the very end of the session, setting up next session's hook.

Open Design Questions

  • Exact XP scale — total points per session, points-per-skill cost? Need to lock once we've drafted the distilled character creation system.
  • How is faction reputation tracked mechanically? Simple +/- counter per faction? Reputation tiers?
  • Should there be a "burn rate" mechanic for the team's own fuel consumption — limiting how many missions they can run per in-game month?
  • How do we handle missions where the players invent objectives command didn't sanction? (Player-driven side-quests in the timeline.)
