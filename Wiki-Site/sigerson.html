Sigerson

The detective who was never fully a detective. Natural precog. Recovered from 1891. ATA's most valuable modern-era infusion target.

Cover Identity

He was, before his preservation, Sherlock Holmes — a consulting detective in late-Victorian London whose abilities were chronicled (with some literary embellishment) by his friend John Watson under the byline of Arthur Conan Doyle.

The Conan Doyle stories are substantially accurate. Holmes really did live at 221B Baker Street. He really did solve the cases as described, more or less. He really did have a brother named Mycroft. He really did face an adversary named James Moriarty (see Moriarty below). The fiction's deliberate exaggerations: Watson made him slightly more theatrical than he was, slightly more arrogant, and considerably more rakish in social style than the real man, who was quieter, more depressive, and more troubled by his own intellect than the stories let on.

Within the agency he is Sigerson — the alias he used during the "Great Hiatus" (1891–1894 in canon, though in the real campaign these are the years of his earliest cryo storage). Sigerson is a clean codename: it preserves continuity with his pre-extraction history, signals deep familiarity with the literary record to anyone who recognizes it, and conceals nothing operationally important. Casual nickname: Sig.

His real birth name — the historical "Sherlock Holmes" — is classified, per the agency's standard aliases convention. Inside ATA archives, his pre-recruitment identity is preserved (he is, after all, a known historical figure under his real name) but operationally always referred to as Sigerson.

True Identity

A natural precognitive psi of unusually high grade, born in Yorkshire, 1854. Recognized by ATA in the 1880s during routine surveillance of unusual cognitive performers in modern (then-modern) Europe. His detective work was, in functional terms, partial precognition — the ability to extrapolate chains of cause and effect far enough into the future to seem like reading minds and predicting events. ATA classified him as a Grade I natural precog and began the long process of preparing him for recruitment.

The complications: he was a cocaine and morphine addict. His health was poor. His emotional life was fragile (Watson's stories deliberately understate this). Transporting him forward through the temporal gradient in 1891 would have killed him; the boost cost alone would have been substantial, and his body was too compromised to survive infusion at the time's protocols.

ATA's solution was the cryogenic pause: preserve him at the historical moment when his career arc reached a plausible exit point, then wait for modern medicine to advance enough to safely revive him, treat his addiction damage, and complete his infusion. The plan was decades-long. As of the campaign's start (2020s), it has been ~130 years.

The Reichenbach Operation

The "death" of Sherlock Holmes at Reichenbach Falls (May 4, 1891, in Conan Doyle's The Final Problem) was the exfiltration event. The public-record narrative — Holmes and Moriarty grappling and plunging together over the falls — is the cover story Watson published at ATA's request to provide a plausible end to Holmes's documented life.

What actually happened: an ATA extraction team intercepted both Holmes and Moriarty at Reichenbach. Holmes went into a cryo unit smuggled to the site weeks earlier. Moriarty — who was, unknown to Holmes at the time, an EPOCH-recruited operative — was simultaneously extracted by an EPOCH team that had been shadowing the situation. Each side took its asset; both sides published "death" cover stories to cover their movements; neither side knew with certainty what the other had done with their counterpart.

ATA suspects Moriarty was pulled forward in time to EPOCH's 2200s facility. EPOCH almost certainly suspects something similar about Holmes, though they could not confirm it until the cryo-pod abduction of Arc 00 — The Cryo Train.

The Cryo Years (1891–2020s)

After his preservation, Sigerson was stored at a hidden ATA cryo facility in northern Europe. The facility was constructed specifically around his pod and a few others (other late-19th and early-20th-century natural psi assets whom ATA preserved for similar reasons). It is staffed minimally — a small team of long-service caretakers who rotated through over generations, maintaining the equipment, monitoring the pods, and adding new technology as it became available.

Sigerson is the most important pod at the facility. He has been preserved at near-absolute-zero in a stasis chamber that combines orichalcum-infused refrigeration tech (smuggled forward from later eras) with progressively-upgraded modern cryogenics. His consciousness, in the canonical reading, is not active during stasis — he is biologically frozen. He has experienced the intervening 130 years as a moment.

Doc's mid-20th-century work on retroviral acceleration was the breakthrough that made his eventual revival feasible. By the 2020s, ATA's medical protocols can:

  • Reverse his addiction damage (cocaine and morphine had done significant neurological harm)
  • Complete his infusion safely
  • Wake him without the cognitive degradation that earlier-era cryo protocols would have caused

The plan was: revive him on a schedule that gave Doc time to do the work properly. Wake him over ~6 months. Read him into his new reality gradually. Integrate him as a senior consulting agent.

Then EPOCH found the facility, and the plan compressed into a single train heist.

Capabilities (Post-Revival)

Once Sigerson is safely revived and his infusion is complete, his operational profile:

  • Natural precognition. His baseline before infusion was already remarkable — short-range chains-of-events forecasting that read as detective brilliance in his original era. After infusion, his precog reaches genuine future-vision territory: brief, fragmentary glimpses of likely outcomes hours-to-days ahead. Not reliable enough to be used as oracle; reliable enough to be a major augmentation to mission planning.
  • Deductive synthesis. Even setting aside the psi, his analytical capability is exceptional. He sees patterns. He notices things. Long after his precog reads start fading, his deductions persist as a major intelligence asset.
  • Hands-on detective tradecraft. Disguise, surveillance, evidence analysis, interrogation. All of it polished by 1880s-1890s London experience, all of it still applicable to modern operations.
  • Multilingual. French, German, Latin, Greek, plus enough Italian and Spanish to function. ATA accelerates his language acquisition once he's awake, adding modern Russian and Mandarin to his repertoire over the first year.
  • Standard agent capabilities from infusion: agent-sense signature, memory privilege, slow-aging.

Limitations and Adjustments

He is also, very real-ly, a man from 1891 waking up in the 2020s.

  • Cultural displacement. Social conventions are wildly different. He spent his first months awake watching modern television obsessively to recalibrate his sense of how people now talk, walk, dress, joke. He still says "I beg your pardon" to apologize, still finds the casual physicality of modern social touch slightly improper, still flinches at certain modern cuss words.
  • Personal grief. Watson is long dead. Mycroft is long dead. The London he knew is unrecognizable. He grieves quietly. The agency's senior staff understand and give him space.
  • Lingering addiction. Doc's treatment cleared the worst of the physical damage but the behavioral patterns are deep. He has not used cocaine or morphine since revival; he probably never will. But under stress, his cravings flare. He has chosen, on advice, not to keep alcohol in his quarters.
  • Period-coded speech patterns. He has worked to modernize his English but slips frequently. His default register is late-Victorian intellectual: precise, slightly formal, fond of constructions that aged out of common usage in the 1920s. Other agents tease him gently. He accepts the teasing with quiet amusement.
  • Skepticism about precognition itself. He understands what he can do, he uses it, he trusts the technique he developed for himself. But he is fundamentally a rationalist and the experience of seeing the future as anything other than deductive extrapolation has been hard for him to fully embrace. He frequently checks his precog reads against deductive reasoning and reports only the intersection — the cases where both his psi and his analysis agree.

Operational Role

  • Senior consulting agent. Not a field-deployed combat asset; his physical condition, even after rejuvenation, is not suited to hard combat work. Used as an analytical and prognostic specialist.
  • Mission planning consultant. Pre-mission, Sigerson reviews intelligence and offers precognitive readings supplemented by deductive analysis. His readings are not binding but they're frequently load-bearing in decision-making.
  • Reality Analysis collaboration. He works closely with the Reality Analysis division and (eventually, after introduction) with Old Doc's team in the Genetics Lab. The agency is intensely interested in mapping the boundary between his natural precog and the infusion-enhanced version.
  • Occasional field deployment for missions requiring his specific deductive expertise. He is not commonly on combat operations but is sometimes brought to era-period missions in late-19th-century Europe where his cultural fluency is irreplaceable.

Moriarty

Sigerson's central unresolved thread.

Professor James Moriarty is, in this universe, a real person who lived 1843–[unresolved, see below]. He was a natural psi of grade comparable to Sigerson, recruited by EPOCH in the 1870s after EPOCH's London station identified him through observation of his early academic career (his mathematical-prodigy phase). EPOCH groomed him through his 30s; his criminal organization in 1880s London was an EPOCH operational front using his precog to coordinate complex multi-actor crimes.

The Holmes-Moriarty rivalry of 1888–1891 was an EPOCH-vs-ATA proxy war between two natural-precog assets in the same city. Reichenbach was the climax. ATA extracted Sigerson; EPOCH extracted Moriarty.

Moriarty's current status: ATA believes he was pulled forward to EPOCH's 2200s Shambhala facility and remains operational there. His subjective age is now ~150 years. Like Sigerson, he was preserved (probably awake, given EPOCH's harsher attitude toward operatives) and is now a senior EPOCH precog consultant. He may have been deployed against ATA operations during the 20th century; some of EPOCH's anomalously accurate predictions are plausibly his work.

Sigerson, once awake and read in, will eventually ask about Moriarty. He gets a redacted answer initially. Late in the campaign, the question becomes load-bearing: an arc where Sigerson and Moriarty meet again — across time, at separate ends of a shared mission — would be a major narrative payoff. See ../Campaigns/Arc-00-The-Cryo-Train.md for the thread.

Why this is a keystone (design note). Two natural precogs, born rivals in one Victorian city, each recruited by an opposing temporal agency and each preserved across more than a century to keep fighting — it is the campaign's cleanest human mirror of the whole ATA-vs-EPOCH war, and it is canon, not coincidence. Protect it. Don't spend Moriarty cheaply in a mid-campaign skirmish; the reunion across the gradient is one of the campaign's marquee end-game payoffs, and every earlier appearance should raise its value rather than cash it in.

What He Knows

  • His own history. All of it. He is, after all, a documented historical figure with extensive contemporaneous records (Watson's, his own).
  • Victorian London in operational detail. Streets, faces, institutions, slang, social customs, criminal networks. Period-specific intelligence asset for late-19th-century missions.
  • His own precog technique. He has been refining his approach to chain-of-events analysis since his teens. Even now, after his enhanced infusion-era precog, he prefers to check his readings against his deductive framework.
  • What ATA tells him about the modern campaign. He is read in to the full agency framework after his revival.
  • What he refuses to discuss. His regrets. Watson's death (which he learned about during his initial briefing — the agent who awakened him handled it with great care; even so, he was inconsolable for a week). His addiction history, and his fear of relapse. His unresolved feelings about Moriarty.

Tells and Vulnerabilities

  • Period-coded English. Anyone listening carefully recognizes his speech patterns as not-modern. He has a constructed cover story for the 21st-century world (long-term British expatriate, lived in remote-rural settings for decades, recently re-entered mainstream society) that explains his odd register adequately. Sophisticated investigators would not be fooled.
  • Documented appearance. Photographs of Sherlock Holmes exist (Conan Doyle commissioned several illustrations for the stories based on real models). His face is findable in historical records. Modern facial recognition with access to the Conan Doyle illustrations and any surviving photographs could match him. ATA maintains careful management of his public appearances accordingly.
  • Psi vulnerability. Like all precogs, he can be jammed, confused, or actively misled by skilled hostile psi work. Moriarty, if encountered, could plausibly counter-precog Sigerson — they share the technique.
  • Relapse vulnerability. Under sufficient stress (combat, loss, betrayal), the cocaine and morphine cravings flare. He has the agency's medical support and his own discipline; under extreme pressure he could break.
  • Sentimental attachment to Watson's memory. If EPOCH ever discovered this and weaponized it (a faked communication, a fabricated diary, a manufactured "Watson lived longer than the public record claims" deception), they could cause him significant operational distress.

Pronouns and Presentation

Sigerson presents as male. Pronouns: he/him. His pre-extraction historical identity also presented as male and used he/him pronouns.

Encounter Notes (For Players)

After the prologue (Arc 00), Sigerson is unconscious. PCs do not interact with him for the first several in-game months while Doc completes his revival.

Once he wakes:

  • He is quiet at first. He spent six months in stages of returning consciousness while Doc worked on him. He has had time to process the basics. The big emotional impacts (Watson, London, Mycroft) have already hit. He is, when the players meet him, recovering rather than freshly grieving.
  • He is curious. He has a hundred and thirty years of catching up to do. He asks the players about their lives, their cultures, their slang, what they think about politics, what music they listen to. He is genuinely interested.
  • He is humble about his abilities. He doesn't volunteer precog reads. He provides them when asked. He flags his uncertainty levels carefully.
  • He is protective of the agency. He understands what ATA does. He believes in the mission. He will not betray his colleagues. But he is also not a true-believer fanatic — he sees the moral complications clearly and worries about them aloud sometimes.

If a player wants to bring him along on a mission as an NPC consultant, the GM should run him as a thinking presence rather than a combat presence. He observes. He notices. He sometimes interrupts the team in the middle of an action sequence with an observation that turns out to be critical. He is not a fighter.

Open Questions

  • The other pods at the cryo facility. Sigerson was the most important but not the only asset preserved there. What happened to the others during EPOCH's raid? Are some still in storage somewhere? Recovery-mission material.
  • Sigerson's role in late-campaign arcs. Does he go to Atlantis, or stay at a modern-era ATA facility? Could be either; the answer affects his availability.
  • The Watson question. Did Watson know about ATA? The historical record suggests no — but ATA's interventions are subtle. If Watson knew and was complicit in the Reichenbach cover story (which is plausible), his stories take on new meaning. Worth GM consideration.
  • Eventual reunion with Moriarty. When and how. A late-campaign arc with these two former adversaries meeting across the temporal gradient is one of the campaign's juiciest possible reveals.
  • Whether to allow a player to PC Sigerson eventually. If yes (recommended for the right player), the player gets a unique character with major precog capability but also major Victorian-cultural-adjustment challenges and the Moriarty thread as their personal arc. If no, he stays as a recurring senior NPC.
