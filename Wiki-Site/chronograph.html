The Chronograph

The agencies' temporal seismograph. The early-warning system that makes ATA and EPOCH plausible institutions.

What It Is

An instrument tuned to the resonance of the Origin meteor. It detects ripples — perturbations in the temporal field that propagate outward from significant historical changes. The Chronograph cannot travel through time, alter time, or predict the future (see Psionics.md for that). It does one thing: it tells the agencies that something has changed, and gives them enough data to dispatch a response team.

Without the Chronograph, ATA and EPOCH would have no way to know what the rival had done, or even that the timeline had been edited. With it, they have an intelligence apparatus.

Where It Lives

  • ATA's Chronograph is built into the floor of the crater facility, directly above the meteor. It is the original instrument, the most sensitive ever constructed, and the one with the broadest detection range. ATA central command monitors it continuously.
  • EPOCH's Chronograph is a smaller, jury-rigged instrument built from reverse-engineered designs and stolen meteor fragments. Its range is narrower, its resolution is coarser, and its operators have learned to interpret noisy data with more aggressive heuristics. Its physical location is one of EPOCH's most closely held secrets.
  • Field-portable miniature Chronographs exist but have extremely short range — useful only for confirming a ripple is still active, not for detecting new ones.

How a Reading Works

When the Chronograph detects a ripple, command receives a reading with the following measurable properties:

PropertyWhat it tells you
EpicenterDate and geographic location where the change originated. Tells the mission team where and when to insert.
MagnitudeRichter-style scale. M1.0 = a single life altered; M5.0 = a city's outcome reshaped; M10.0 = a civilization rerouted. Drives mission urgency.
Bearing / SignatureThe "shape" of the ripple. ATA ops have a recognizable signature (surgical, low-magnitude, narrow-focus). EPOCH ops have a different one (higher-magnitude, often violent, wider focus). Natural historical drift has a third. An unknown signature is the most alarming reading — someone else is in play.
AftershocksSecondary ripples that arrive after the main one, as downstream consequences propagate. Sometimes the aftershocks are larger than the original event.
Propagation delayThe Chronograph does not detect ripples instantly. A change near the Origin registers within hours. A change at the far end of the gradient may take days. This creates a response window before the new reality hardens. Miss the window and the change is permanent.

This asymmetry means each agency frequently spots ripples the other missed. ATA may detect a subtle Bronze Age shift EPOCH cannot resolve. EPOCH may interpret an ambiguous reading as exploitable opportunity while ATA dismisses it as drift. Both agencies race to anomalies the other side either doesn't see yet or is also racing toward — which is the basic engine of inter-agency competition.

Spoofing and Counter-Intelligence

Ripples can be faked. A sufficiently skilled operation can deliberately generate a false ripple — an intervention crafted to produce a specific Chronograph signature, designed to deceive the rival agency's analysis. Use cases:

  • Decoy. Draw the rival's attention to a non-significant event so a real operation can run undetected elsewhere.
  • False flag. Fabricate a ripple with the rival agency's signature, to provoke their command into reacting to "their own" action.
  • Trap. Plant a ripple whose epicenter is a kill zone — the rival's response team walks into prepared opposition.
  • Burn. Fake a high-magnitude EPOCH-signature ripple to force EPOCH to spend fuel on a damage-control mission that was never necessary.

Spoofing is expensive. Fabricating a false ripple burns real meteor-fuel charge. Decoys are not free — every decoy is intelligence in itself, because the rival just spent budget on misdirection. Detection of spoofing is a skill check on the Chronograph reading itself: trained analysts can flag anomalies in the aftershock pattern that hint at fabrication. Detection is never automatic — sometimes you act on suspicion you're wrong about.

In-Agency Slang

  • ATA: the Chronograph is formally called the Chronograph. Field agents call it "the Watch" ("we've got a Watch hit at M4.2 in Lyon, 1188 CE").
  • EPOCH: formally called Chronograf (slight transliteration tic). Agents call it "the Loom" — partly because of its woven-pattern output display, partly because of the implication that EPOCH is weaving the timeline rather than just observing it.
